Putting Mindbody Contracts and Auto-Pay Status Inside Zoho CRM in Real Time

Why End-of-Month Contract Reports Are Useless

Contract-based memberships are predictable revenue until an auto-pay fails. The failure window is short: 24 to 72 hours from the failed charge to the member quietly looking for another studio. Most studios run a contract status report on the first of every month, which means a failed payment on the third lives in the dark for 27 days. By the time the report runs, the member is gone.

Hypothetical: Mirror Lake Pilates (illustrative) is a single-location studio with 360 contract-based clients. The sales team uses Zoho CRM for everything except contract details, which live in Mindbody PDFs. End-of-month reconciliation eats half a day. Auto-pay failures get caught on Day 28, recovered at maybe 30 percent, and the rest churn.

Why Off-the-Shelf Tools Fail Here

Mindbody contracts are nested objects with multiple state changes. Most generic glue tools pull the contract at creation and never update it. The auto-pay status, the retry attempts, the scheduled changes, and the cancellation timestamps all live on child objects that require specific API calls to refresh.

A real solution has to:

  • Treat contracts as their own object with live state.
  • Push every auto-pay state change as a near-real-time event.
  • Recompute “current state” daily as a safety net.

Custom builds do this, but the rate-limit accounting for daily reconciliation across 360 contracts is the kind of thing that breaks two months in.

What MB Contracts Pushes to Zoho CRM

The MB Contracts custom object carries every field needed for live workflows:

Zoho CRM custom object: MB Contracts
- contract_id
- contact_id (links to Zoho CRM contact)
- contract_name                e.g. "12-Month Unlimited Reformer"
- start_date / end_date
- status                       active|pending_cancel|expired|terminated
- autopay_status               current|retrying|failed|suspended
- autopay_method               card|ach
- autopay_next_charge_date
- autopay_next_charge_amount
- autopay_last_attempt_at
- autopay_last_attempt_result  success|declined|insufficient_funds|...
- autopay_retry_count
- monthly_amount
- balance_remaining

The autopay_status field is the trigger for everything that matters. Status flips to failed or retrying, workflow fires, recovery starts.

Designing the Failed-Payment Recovery Workflow

The Zoho CRM workflow on the MB Contracts object:

  1. Trigger: autopay_status changes from current to failed or retrying.
  2. Same hour: Auto-send SMS to client, “Hey, our system flagged your payment didn’t go through. Quick update here: [update_card_link]. Reply HELP if you need a hand.”
  3. +4 hours: If status is still failed and client has not updated card, create a high-priority task for the membership specialist with full context (last successful payment, retry count, amount).
  4. +24 hours: Auto-send email from the studio owner, “Want me to give you a quick call?”
  5. +48 hours: Owner call task created, due same day.
  6. Recovery branch: When autopay_status flips back to current, suppress remaining tasks and send a confirmation SMS.
  7. Escalation branch: At +5 days unresolved, lead status changes to “At-Risk Member” and the in-person retention conversation is calendared.

The same-day SMS recovers most of the volume. Failed cards are usually expired cards or insufficient funds events that the member did not know about. Catching the failure within hours, not weeks, is the single biggest factor in recovery rate.

Renewal Dates as Pipeline Forecasting

The end_date field on the MB Contracts object lets the sales team forecast renewal revenue weeks in advance:

  • 90-day forward pipeline: sum of monthly_amount for contracts with end_date in the next 90 days, weighted by historical renewal rate.
  • Renewal call queue: contracts with end_date - now() <= 45 days and status = active, sorted by client lifetime value.
  • Auto-renewal vs. manual-renewal split: filter on the renewal flag on the underlying membership, plan staffing for the manual side.

The forecast is only useful because the data is live. End-of-month reports get the forecast wrong by 5 to 15 percent because the input is stale.

Sample forecast query (Zoho CRM Reports):
  Select contact_id, monthly_amount, end_date
  From MB_Contracts
  Where end_date Between today() And today() + 90 days
    And status = 'active'
  Order By client_ltv DESC

Representative Recovery Rate at 360 Clients

Illustrative composites, not a real customer:

  • Auto-pay failure recovery rate baseline (end-of-month catch): roughly 25 to 35 percent.
  • Recovery rate with same-day SMS + 24-hour owner email: a lift into the 60 to 75 percent range.
  • Revenue saved per quarter from earlier failure detection: a meaningful share of the contract base, depending on average contract size.
  • Owner hours per month on contract reconciliation: cut from roughly 4 to under 1, with the remaining time spent on the genuinely at-risk members.

The mechanism is fast detection and a sequence the member can actually respond to. The integration provides the detection. The sequence has to be honest about asking for the update.

Builder Takeaway

  • Treat contracts and memberships as separate custom objects. A client can have one membership and three contracts (or vice versa). One record per concept keeps queries sane.
  • Build the failed-payment recovery on autopay_status change events, not on a scheduled report. Latency is the whole game.
  • Use end_date as your renewal pipeline forecast. It is the most reliable forward-looking revenue number a studio has.
