Cutting Pediatric No-Shows With Salesforce Flows Triggered by Cliniko Appointment Status

Fred Lumiere Dec 29, 2025 阅读时间：5分钟
＃卫生保健#Cliniko#Salesforce#CRM连接

After-school slots are the most valuable real estate in a pediatric therapy clinic. They are also the slots families miss most often, and the cost of those misses is asymmetric: an unused 3:30pm slot is harder to fill on short notice than a midday slot, and families that miss after-school sessions tend to miss again the next month if nothing changes.

Take an illustrative example: Little Roots Pediatric Therapy, a hypothetical 4-clinic network with 18 pediatric OTs and physios and around 2,400 active families. They run at a 19 percent Did Not Arrive rate. Care coordinators chase reschedules from a Cliniko report each morning, working a list that is already out of date by the time they start. The same families lapse again the next month. Net effect: clinical capacity is underused, families do not get the consistency their care plan needs, and revenue leaks at scale.

Why pediatric after-school slots are the highest-cost no-show

A clinic running on a typical $135 per session fee, with a 3:30pm to 5:30pm peak that fills 8 weeks in advance, loses roughly $270 of marginal revenue every time an after-school slot goes empty (the session itself plus the difficulty of filling the slot on short notice). At 19 percent DNA and 20 after-school appointments per clinic per week, the network is bleeding low five figures per week in those slots alone. The morning Cliniko report does not catch the problem fast enough because by the time the coordinator calls, the family has already moved on with their day.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

Flat-schedule reminder tools (24 hours before, 2 hours before) do not adapt to family context. A 5-year-old’s parent on a Wednesday afternoon needs a different cadence than a 14-year-old client’s parent. Recovery is the bigger gap: most reminder tools have no concept of “the appointment just got marked Did Not Arrive, contact the family within 30 minutes.” Pediatric care coordinators need a real-time queue, not a morning report.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to Salesforce streams Cliniko Appointments as Salesforce custom objects, with status transitions (Booked, Arrived, DNA, Cancelled) propagating in near real time. Salesforce Flow can trigger on any status change, which gives care coordinators a live queue rather than a stale report. The integration also distinguishes individual from group appointments, so social skills groups get their own reminder cadence without distorting one-to-one DNA reporting.

Concrete steps

  1. Enable Cliniko Appointments as Salesforce custom objects. Confirm that status changes propagate within a few minutes.
  2. Build the DNA recovery Flow. Trigger: appointment record’s Status changes to “Did Not Arrive.” Action: create a Salesforce task assigned to the family’s care coordinator with a 30-minute SLA, send an SMS to the parent via your Salesforce-connected SMS provider, log the touch on the contact timeline.
  3. Build the repeat-DNA flag. Schedule a Salesforce Flow that runs nightly: for any contact whose linked patient has 3 or more DNA appointments in the trailing 90 days, set a “Repeat DNA Review” flag and create a task for the clinical director and the clinic manager.
  4. Build the reminder cadence by patient age. Use the Cliniko patient date of birth (exposed on the Salesforce contact) to split families into reminder cohorts. Younger children (under 8) get a longer-lead and a same-day reminder. Older clients get a single 24-hour touch.
  5. Build the care-stage cadence. Use the Cliniko Case Information (assessment vs ongoing therapy) to send different reminder copy. An initial assessment reminder needs to set expectations differently from a 15th ongoing therapy session reminder.
  6. Handle group sessions separately. Social skills groups have their own appointment management object in the integration. Build a separate group reminder Flow so individual session DNA reporting is not contaminated by group attendance signals.
  7. Build the DNA dashboard. By clinic, by practitioner, by appointment slot (morning, midday, after-school). Track DNA rate, recovery rate (rebooked within 7 days), revenue recovered. Compare to the pre-deployment baseline.

Outcome

A pediatric network matching Little Roots’ profile typically pulls DNA from 19 percent down to 10 to 12 percent within a quarter. The biggest single contributor is the 30-minute SLA on the DNA recovery Flow: families that get a same-hour personal call from their actual care coordinator rebook at 2 to 3 times the rate of families called the next morning. The repeat-DNA flag surfaces a small group of families (typically 4 to 7 percent of caseload) whose patterns warrant a clinical and operational conversation, which is impossible to spot in a flat monthly report. After-school slot utilisation moves from the high 70s into the low 90s, which at network scale is meaningful weekly revenue.

What to avoid

Two pitfalls worth naming:

  • Do not let the DNA recovery SMS read like a marketing message. Families respond to a personal note from “your care coordinator at [clinic name],” not to a templated brand voice. Use the practitioner identity and clinic name fields the integration already provides.
  • Do not trigger automated recovery on cancelled appointments the same way as DNA. A cancellation is a notified cancellation. Sending a “we noticed you missed” message to a family that politely cancelled erodes trust. Branch the Flow on the actual status.

Builder takeaway

  • DNA recovery is a 30-minute-SLA problem, not a next-morning-call problem. Salesforce Flow triggered on Cliniko status change is the mechanism that makes that SLA achievable.
  • Repeat-DNA detection is the report you cannot build any other way. Build it. The patterns it surfaces drive clinical and operational decisions.
  • Group sessions get their own reminder and reporting stream. Mixing them with one-to-one metrics breaks both.
