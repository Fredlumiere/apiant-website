Turning Cliniko Appointment Data Into Smarter ActiveCampaign Nurture for a Podiatry Practice

Fred Lumiere Dec 15, 2025 阅读时间：5分钟
＃卫生保健#Cliniko#积极行动#CRM连接

A nurture sequence that cannot see whether the lead became a patient is a nurture sequence that is guessing. It will send the wrong message to the wrong people for as long as you let it run. The fix is not better copy. The fix is feeding it the truth about who actually showed up.

Take an illustrative example: Riverside Foot and Ankle Care, a hypothetical single clinic with 4 podiatrists and around 3,200 active patients. They spend on Google ads for diabetic foot care and orthotics. Leads land on a service page, fill out a form, and drop into an ActiveCampaign list. From there, ActiveCampaign sends an educational sequence (here is what diabetic foot care involves, here is what to expect at your first appointment, here is a special offer). All of it goes to every lead, regardless of whether they ever booked, showed up, or became a paying patient. The marketer has no idea who actually converted, so the sequence cannot adapt.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail here

ActiveCampaign on its own is a high-quality automation engine, but it only knows what the form fill told it and what the user has clicked since. It cannot see Cliniko. So three useful things are invisible to it:

  • Whether the lead ever booked an initial consult.
  • Whether the lead ever arrived (booked is not the same as arrived).
  • Whether the patient has come back for a second visit or bought orthotics.

Without those facts, every contact gets the same message. New leads get nurture they no longer need (because they already booked). Booked patients get pre-appointment offers (because the system does not know they booked). Patients who never arrived never get a recovery touch. The most expensive ad clicks waste their value because the post-click experience is uniform.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to ActiveCampaign syncs appointment counts (booked, arrived, cancelled, no-show) and key appointment metadata onto every ActiveCampaign contact, and applies tags when significant events happen (initial consult completed, first orthotic dispensed, lapsed past 60 days). That gives the automation engine the one thing it was missing: a signal for whether the person on the other end of the email is a prospect, a booked patient, an active patient, or a lapsed patient. The automations branch accordingly.

Concrete steps

  1. Enable the patient sync. Confirm that Booked Appointments, Arrived Appointments, Cancelled Appointments, No-Show Appointments, Last Appointment Date, Next Appointment Date, and Lifetime Value are appearing as custom fields on the ActiveCampaign contact.
  2. Switch on Appointment Completion Tagging. When Cliniko marks an appointment as arrived, the integration applies a tag (for example, “Patient: Initial Consult Completed” or “Patient: Orthotic Fitting Completed”) to the matching ActiveCampaign contact.
  3. Restructure the diabetic foot care automation. The first decision branch in the sequence becomes: does this contact have a Booked Appointment count greater than zero? If yes, suppress the prospect nurture and route into the pre-appointment branch. If no, continue the prospect nurture.
  4. Add a no-arrival recovery branch. Trigger: contact entered the prospect automation, booked an appointment (Booked count went from 0 to 1), and then 7 days passed without that booking moving to Arrived. Send a soft recovery email, then a second message offering to rebook.
  5. Add a post-conversion branch. Trigger: a contact gets the “Initial Consult Completed” tag. Wait 5 days, send a what-to-expect-next email keyed to whether orthotics were dispensed (use the orthotic-fitting tag).
  6. Build the ROI report. In ActiveCampaign, group contacts by source (UTM or list of origin) and report on Arrived Appointments and Lifetime Value. Now your “diabetic foot care landing page” report shows leads in, arrived appointments, and revenue, not just opens and clicks.

Outcome

A practice matching Riverside’s profile will typically see two things move within the first 60 days. First, unsubscribe rate from the diabetic foot care nurture drops by 30 to 50 percent, because booked and converted patients stop receiving prospect messaging. Second, the no-arrival recovery branch tends to recover 10 to 18 percent of contacts that booked but did not show, which at a $220 initial consult and a normal monthly cohort is meaningful revenue that previously walked away in silence. The cost per arrived patient (not per form fill) on the underlying ad campaign becomes calculable for the first time.

Where ActiveCampaign tags should not replace Cliniko fields

A common mistake: marketing starts to treat the ActiveCampaign tag layer as the patient record. It is not. ActiveCampaign tags are a signaling layer, useful for automation branching. The patient record lives in Cliniko. If you find yourself building marketing tags like “patient is currently in care” or “patient has chronic foot condition,” stop. The first should be derived from Cliniko activity (and stays accurate without your intervention). The second should not exist in marketing infrastructure at all. The integration’s job is to keep ActiveCampaign in sync with operational signals from Cliniko, not to recreate the clinical record.

Builder takeaway

  • The minimum viable upgrade is four custom fields on the ActiveCampaign contact (Booked, Arrived, Cancelled, No-Show counts) plus completion tagging. Everything else branches off those.
  • Always add a no-arrival recovery branch. Booked-but-did-not-arrive is the highest intent group in the funnel and almost nobody talks to them.
  • Report ROI on arrived appointments and revenue, not form fills. The integration makes that possible without adding a BI layer.
