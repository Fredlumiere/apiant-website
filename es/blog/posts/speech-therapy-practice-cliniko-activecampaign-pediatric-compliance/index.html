Marketing Without Crossing the Line: a Compliance-First Cliniko to ActiveCampaign Setup for a Pediatric Speech Practice

Fred Lumiere Mar 31, 2025 Lectura de 5 minutos
#Cuidado de la salud#Cliniko#CampañaActiva#CRMConnect

A pediatric speech practice has a particular problem that most off-the-shelf marketing automation patterns get wrong. The patient is a child. The marketing contact is a parent. Default automation behavior, which assumes the patient and the recipient are the same person, will produce messages that address the child by name, expose case context to the wrong reader, or treat the parent’s email as the child’s marketing record. Any of those is a real problem.

Take an illustrative example: Soundbridge Speech and Language, a hypothetical practice with 8 speech-language pathologists and around 1,800 active families, mostly pediatric. The clinical director is right to be cautious about adding ActiveCampaign on top of Cliniko. The right answer is not “no”, it is a governance-first setup with explicit rules for what crosses the boundary.

Why pediatric caseloads break standard CRM patterns

The defaults baked into most marketing CRMs were designed for adult B2C. They assume:

  • The patient is the recipient.
  • Personalization fields refer to the recipient.
  • Marketing consent attached to the patient applies to the recipient.

In a pediatric practice every one of those assumptions is wrong. The patient is the child (whose name and case context should not appear in a marketing email). The recipient is the parent (whose consent governs whether anything gets sent at all). The marketing contact and the patient record are two different things that have to stay distinct.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to ActiveCampaign supports field-level configuration, so the integration can map the parent contact details (email, phone, name) as the ActiveCampaign contact while keeping the child as the Cliniko patient record. Appointment events and completion tags are surfaced for automation, but field mapping is scoped so clinical content (case notes, assessment scores, therapy goals, narrative) never leaves Cliniko in the first place. Consent and unsubscribe live on the parent record and propagate one direction only.

The field map

Before configuring anything, write down what crosses the boundary and what does not. The list looks like this:

Syncs to ActiveCampaign (parent contact): - Nombre del padre/madre, correo electrónico, teléfono y dirección postal. - Consentimiento para fines de marketing y preferencias de comunicación. - Fecha, hora y lugar de la cita, e identidad del profesional. - Estado de la cita (Reservada, Presente, No se presentó, Cancelada). - Número de citas y fecha de la última cita.

Stays in Cliniko (never syncs): - Nombre y fecha de nacimiento del niño como campo de personalización de marketing. (La identidad del niño puede existir en el historial del paciente de Cliniko, pero nunca se utiliza en la automatización del marketing). - Puntuaciones de evaluación, resultados de pruebas estandarizadas. - Objetivos terapéuticos y planes de tratamiento. - Notas de caso, notas de sesión, contenido narrativo. - Diagnósticos e impresiones clínicas. - Cualquier campo clínico de texto libre.

The clinical director and compliance lead should sign off on this map before the integration goes live, and review it quarterly.

Concrete steps

  1. Configure the contact map. The parent is the ActiveCampaign contact, with parent name and email as the identity fields. The child remains the Cliniko patient. No child personalization fields are mapped into ActiveCampaign.
  2. Enable the appointment sync for status and scheduling only. Map appointment date, time, location, and practitioner. Do not map appointment type if appointment type reveals clinical specifics (replace specific labels with generic operational labels at the Cliniko side if needed).
  3. Configure Appointment Completion Tagging safely. A tag like “Session Completed” or “Session Cancelled” is operationally safe. A tag like “Progress in Articulation” or “Goal Met: Receptive Language” is clinical content and does not belong in ActiveCampaign.
  4. Mirror marketing consent and unsubscribe one-way, Cliniko to ActiveCampaign. The parent’s consent setting in Cliniko is the source of truth. If the parent unsubscribes via ActiveCampaign, that change should be reflected back in Cliniko via a controlled process (manual or scripted), but ActiveCampaign should never be the authoritative consent record.
  5. Build automation templates that address the parent, not the child. Personalization tokens reference the parent’s name. Appointment reminders say “your appointment with [practitioner]” rather than “[child’s name]’s appointment for [therapy type].” This is a default that must be enforced at the template level, not left to copywriter discretion.
  6. Suppress appointment-driven follow-ups that could expose case context. A reminder is safe. A “we noticed you missed [child’s name]’s session” message that triggers from a DNA status crosses the line. The integration can surface the signal. The practice’s policy decides whether it becomes an automated email or a clinician-led conversation.

Quarterly governance review

Calendar the review now. Every quarter, the integration owner, the clinical director, and the compliance lead spend 20 minutes confirming:

  • The field map is unchanged, or document any additions and the rationale.
  • A sample of ActiveCampaign records to confirm no clinical content is appearing.
  • The list of automations and their personalization tokens to confirm none reference child-specific fields.
  • The unsubscribe and consent flow to confirm it is still one-way Cliniko to ActiveCampaign and that any parent who unsubscribed last quarter is reflected correctly in Cliniko.

This is the cheapest insurance the practice can buy.

Outcome

A pediatric practice matching Soundbridge’s profile that adopts this pattern gets the marketing benefit (consistent appointment reminders, lapsed-family recall, family newsletter) without the governance exposure. The clinical director can describe in one paragraph exactly what crosses the boundary and what does not. The integration becomes something the compliance lead approves rather than something the compliance lead fears.

Builder takeaway

  • The parent is the marketing contact. The child is the patient record. Do not collapse them.
  • The field map is a written document signed before configuration starts. Nothing crosses the boundary that is not on the map.
  • Personalization tokens reference the parent. Templates enforce that default. It is too important to leave to copywriter discretion.
