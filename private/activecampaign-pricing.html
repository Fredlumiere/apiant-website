Note on AWS costs. Ranges reflect light to heavy real-world workload on each footprint, not raw EC2 plus EBS. For an integration platform at steady state, the bill is driven primarily by data egress to third-party APIs and NAT gateway data processing, which together often exceed compute cost. Secondary drivers include EBS snapshots and AWS Backup, CloudWatch logs and metrics, public IPv4 charges, Route 53, Secrets Manager, and any managed services you add. Light workloads and quiet integration schedules trend to the low end of each range; high-volume bi-directional sync, large payloads, or chatty APIs trend to the high end or beyond. Region, reserved instance or Savings Plan commitments, and taxes also shift the final bill.

04 · How We Price This

Integration capacity, not seats or tasks.

01

Price on capacity, not consumption.

You get predictable monthly cost tied to how much production integration you actually run. No per-seat tax, no per-task meter.

02

A connection is not a unit of value.

One connection can be low-value on a small customer and high-value on a large one. Tiered capacity fits better than overage pricing.

03

Software license and infrastructure stay separate.

AWS is a pass-through at cost. The software value stays on the APIANT license, cleanly.

The material terms every enterprise buyer needs up front.

Renewal pricing increases are capped at the greater of 5% per year or the U.S. CPI change over the prior term.
