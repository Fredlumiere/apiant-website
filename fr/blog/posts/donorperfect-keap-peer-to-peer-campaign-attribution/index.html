Attribution for peer-to-peer fundraising: syncing DonorPerfect campaign and solicitation codes into Keap

Fred Lumiere Nov 27, 2024 6 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#Keap#CRMConnect

A peer-to-peer fundraiser is a flash flood. A weekend 5K brings in 300 to 800 gifts in 48 hours, each one tied to a specific team captain, a specific channel (Instagram story, group text, captain’s email blast), and a specific appeal. By Monday morning, the attribution should be clean. In practice, it is a mess of partially-tagged records and a development associate building a pivot table from a CSV export.

The root cause is not the volume. It is that the four codes that matter (appeal, campaign, solicitation, source) live in DonorPerfect, while the donor stewardship workflow lives in Keap. If those codes do not flow as structured Keap fields, attribution collapses the moment the gift is recorded.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

Generic Zapier-style connectors pull DonorPerfect gifts into Keap as activity records. They flatten the code metadata or push it as free text into a notes field. Free-text attribution is unusable. You cannot build a Keap dashboard on it, you cannot trigger a sequence on it, and you cannot reconcile it against the original ask. By Tuesday you have a Keap audience full of “P2P donors” with no way to tell which of the 14 team captains drove which gifts.

The other failure: tools that sync the gift but lose the team-captain association. Most P2P platforms record the captain in DonorPerfect as a solicit code or a flag. If that piece of metadata gets dropped at the integration boundary, you cannot send personalized thank-yous and you cannot run the leaderboard the captains expect.

Latency is the third problem. Nightly batch syncs are useless during an event weekend. Captains who refresh the leaderboard every 30 minutes during Saturday’s run will see yesterday’s snapshot. They notice. The retention rate for captains who feel ignored during their event is dramatically lower than for captains who see real-time recognition, and replacing experienced captains is one of the most expensive recurring costs in P2P fundraising.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and Keap preserves all four codes as structured Keap fields. The pieces:

  • CodeSync mirrors DonorPerfect’s Appeal, Campaign, Solicitation, et Source dropdowns as native Keap custom-field dropdowns. Values stay consistent across both systems automatically.
  • Flags as Tags converts DonorPerfect flags (team captain names, P2P event identifiers, milestone flags) into Keap tags that the dashboard and sequences can read.
  • Detailed donation information sync carries gift amount, date, designation, and all four codes into Keap as the gift is recorded.
  • Real-time sync means a Saturday morning gift hits Keap inside minutes, not overnight. Captain leaderboards stay live.

Concrete steps

For the illustrative scenario of Patriot Bridge Veterans Foundation, a fictional national veterans support nonprofit running a 5K-plus-virtual P2P program, the attribution flow looks like this:

  1. Define the code taxonomy upfront. In DonorPerfect, set up Campaign = 2026-P2P-RUCKMARCH, Appeal = P2P-SOCIAL or P2P-EMAIL or P2P-DIRECT, Solicitation = TEAM-<captain-slug>, Source = web or mobile.
  2. Map all four in CodeSync. Each becomes a Keap custom dropdown. The values list stays in sync automatically; new team captains added in DonorPerfect appear in Keap without manual work.
  3. Sync team-captain flags as tags. Flags-as-Tags converts captain-jsmith flags into Keap tags. Every gift attributed to that captain inherits the tag.
  4. Build the captain leaderboard. A Keap saved report grouped by Solicitation field, sorted by total raised, refreshes live during the event weekend. Captains see their standing on a shared link.
  5. Personalized thank-yous. Keap sequence triggered by gift sync, merge field pulls captain name into the email: “John raised this gift for our team. Here is why your support matters.”
  6. Channel performance dashboard. Group gifts by Appeal to see whether social, email, or direct messaging drove the most dollars per captain. Pre-event coaching for next year focuses on the channels that worked.
  7. Roll attribution back to DonorPerfect for the annual report. Bi-directional sync writes final totals per captain back as DonorPerfect record fields, so the annual report comes out of DonorPerfect cleanly without a second reconciliation.

Outcome

Representative numbers from comparable national P2P programs after wiring this up:

  • Captain leaderboard goes from a Monday-morning Excel rebuild to a live Keap view, refreshing every few minutes during the event.
  • Channel attribution clarity moves from “we think Instagram worked” to “Instagram delivered $42 average gift; group SMS delivered $68 average gift; next year shift email spend toward SMS coaching.”
  • Per-captain thank-you send time drops from “within a week” to under 15 minutes from gift posting.
  • Captain retention into the next event year typically rises 15-25 percent when captains see real-time recognition and receive personalized post-event reports.
  • Annual report turnaround drops from a multi-week reconciliation exercise to a same-day export, because final attribution lives in DonorPerfect cleanly the moment the event closes.

The honest caveat: the integration cannot fix bad code hygiene at the donation form. If captains share generic links instead of their personalized ones, attribution still breaks. Train the captains, then wire the integration. The other caveat is that real-time leaderboards can create unhealthy competition if not framed well. A few foundations have seen captains burn out chasing the top spot in ways that hurt next year’s recruitment. Frame the leaderboard around team contribution and personal growth, not individual rank, or the long-term cost can outweigh the short-term lift.

Donor privacy still applies in P2P events. Captains see aggregate counts and totals for their team, not individual donor identities unless donors opt in. The integration should respect anonymity flags on individual gifts; captains do not need to know which of their personal contacts gave $500 versus $50 unless the donor has actively chosen to be visible.

Post-event follow-through

The event-weekend rush is only half the value. The week after the event is where retention is built or lost. Donors who gave to a captain’s team should hear from the captain (or a templated message bearing the captain’s name) within 5 days of the event. Donors who attended but did not give should be tagged for a soft follow-up about next year. First-time event donors should enter a separate cultivation track aimed at converting them into year-round donors, not just event-day participants.

The integration makes all of that possible because it preserves the metadata. Without team-captain tags, channel attribution, and gift-versus-attendance distinction flowing into Keap, the post-event follow-through is a manual exercise that gets shelved by Wednesday. With the metadata in place, the follow-through is just three saved sequences that fire on Sunday night and run through the following week without intervention.

Builder takeaway

  • Set the code taxonomy before the campaign launches, not after. Retrofitting solicit codes once gifts are in DonorPerfect is painful and error-prone.
  • Real-time matters here. A leaderboard updated nightly is a wasted leaderboard during a 48-hour event.
  • Flags-as-tags handles the captain dimension cleanly. Trying to encode captain identity in the solicit code only works until you have more than 20 captains, then it becomes a maintenance disaster.
