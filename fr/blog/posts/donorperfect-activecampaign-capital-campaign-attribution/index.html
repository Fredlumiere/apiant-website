Attribution that survives the audit: connecting ActiveCampaign sends to DonorPerfect gifts for capital campaigns

Fred Lumiere Mar 3, 2025 6 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#CampagneActive#CRMConnect

Capital campaigns get audited. Annual fund decisions get debated at board meetings. In both cases, the development office is asked the same question and rarely has a defensible answer: which message actually drove which gift?

Email opens are not gifts. Clicks are not gifts. The link a donor clicked in March and the $5,000 gift they sent in May are separated by twelve weeks, an offline conversation, and a board member’s nudge at a dinner. The campaign attribution that survives the audit has to connect the ActiveCampaign send to the DonorPerfect gift with enough metadata to defend the spend.

Most museums never get there. The development office reports open and click rates because they have them. The board approves next year’s email budget based on opens and clicks because the gift-level attribution does not exist.

The attribution problem looks different from the donor cultivation problem. Cultivation is about getting the right message to the right person. Attribution is about proving which message produced which dollar.

ActiveCampaign reports message-level engagement perfectly but cannot see the gift. The donor clicks the campaign link, lands on the donation page, and gives. ActiveCampaign records the click. DonorPerfect records the gift. Without an integration, nothing connects the two events.

UTM tracking on donation forms can pass campaign metadata to DonorPerfect, but only if the donation form is configured to capture and store the UTMs as custom fields on the gift record. Most forms are not.

Manual reconciliation works for a 30-gift capital campaign. It collapses on a 3,000-gift annual appeal.

Native DonorPerfect campaign codes solve part of the problem (which Campaign drove the gift) but not the granular question (which email send, which subject line, which segment).

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and ActiveCampaign treats each gift as a first-class data object that carries its campaign metadata across both systems. The features that make audit-grade attribution possible:

  • Suivi simplifié des dons writes each DonorPerfect gift as an ActiveCampaign deal with amount, gift date, gift type, campaign, solicitation, and sub-solicitation attached.
  • Insights de performance des campagnes keep ActiveCampaign’s deal property values aligned with DonorPerfect campaign codes via real-time sync.
  • CodeSync pour les menus deroulants keeps campaign code lists synchronized. Add a new appeal code in DonorPerfect, the ActiveCampaign dropdown updates automatically.
  • Custom field sync carries UTM parameters captured at the donation form through to the deal so source and medium ride with the gift.
  • Bi-directional sync writes attribution roll-ups back to DonorPerfect for the audit trail.

Building audit-grade campaign attribution

Illustrative scenario: Coastline Maritime Museum, an 11,000-member regional museum running four annual capital appeals (hypothetical example, not a real organization).

Step 1. Tag the send with a campaign code that survives the gift. In ActiveCampaign, every campaign send should carry a custom URL parameter that maps to a DonorPerfect campaign code. A spring capital appeal might send a campaign called 2026_SPRING_CAP_VESSEL_RESTORATION. The donation link in the email carries ?utm_campaign=2026_SPRING_CAP_VESSEL_RESTORATION&utm_source=activecampaign&utm_medium=email&utm_content=lead_email.

Step 2. Capture UTMs on the donation form. Configure the DonorPerfect donation form to write utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content, et utm_term into custom fields on the Gift record. This is a small JavaScript snippet on the form that reads the query string and populates hidden form fields. Once in place, every gift carries its origination metadata.

Step 3. Map UTM fields to ActiveCampaign deal properties. In CRMConnect’s field mapping, create five custom deal fields in ActiveCampaign (Original Source, Original Medium, Original Campaign, Original Content, Original Term) and point each to the matching DonorPerfect UTM custom field. The mapping fires on every new gift sync, so attribution is captured at the deal level, not the contact level.

Step 4. Build the deal pipeline for the capital campaign. A multi-touch capital campaign typically has stages like Identified, Cultivated, Proposed, Pledged, First Payment Received, Paid in Full. Each stage corresponds to a different point in the giving journey. CRMConnect populates the appropriate stage based on DonorPerfect Gift Type. For multi-year pledges, create a deal per payment in a Cash pipeline and a separate Pledge Commitment deal for the total in a Commitments pipeline. This is the only way to keep cash-based reporting accurate.

Step 5. Build the attribution dashboards. Three reports do most of the work for capital campaign attribution:

  • Revenue by Email Send. Group deals by Original Content (which carries the email send identifier) and sum the deal amount. Sort descending. The top three sends typically produce 60%+ of attributable revenue. The bottom half can be retired.
  • Revenue per Recipient. Divide attributed revenue by the number of recipients of each send. This is the metric that matters for campaign optimization, not open rate.
  • Multi-Touch Path Analysis. For donors who received multiple sends before giving, list the sequence of touches that preceded the gift. ActiveCampaign’s contact activity log makes this readable.

Step 6. Surface attribution to the board. Build an ActiveCampaign dashboard that displays revenue by campaign, revenue by segment, and pipeline value by stage. Share with view-only board access. The board meeting moves from “let me walk you through the email metrics” to “here is the dashboard, here is what we are doing next.”

Pitfalls that kill attribution accuracy

Counting pledges as revenue. A $50,000 pledge is not $50,000 of revenue this quarter. It is a commitment that may produce $10,000 of revenue per year for five years. Keep pledges in a separate pipeline from payments. Report on both, but never sum them.

Multi-year commitments paid in a future year. A pledge made in 2026 and paid in 2027 should attribute to the 2026 campaign that produced the pledge, not the 2027 reporting period that happened to receive the payment. Carry the Original Campaign field on each pledge payment so the attribution survives the payment date.

Matching gifts. A $1,000 donor gift produces a $1,000 matching gift from the donor’s employer two months later. The matching gift should carry the same Original Campaign as the originating gift, not whatever campaign happened to be active when the matching gift arrived.

Soft credits. When a foundation gives but a board member sourced the relationship, the board member deserves attribution on the relationship side and the foundation deserves attribution on the cash side. Track these as separate deal types so they do not double-count.

Last-touch versus first-touch. UTM parameters capture only the last touch. For capital campaigns with long consideration windows, supplement UTM-based attribution with a first-touch view: which campaign first introduced the contact to the museum’s capital appeal? Use the contact’s first deal in the capital pipeline to establish first touch.

What it looks like in production

For an illustrative museum the size of Coastline (11,000 members, $5.2M revenue, four capital appeals per year), implementing audit-grade attribution typically reveals that one or two campaigns per cycle are producing 50% to 70% of attributable revenue, while two or three are producing single-digit ROI. Reallocating budget toward the proven winners often lifts cycle-over-cycle revenue 30% to 50% without incremental spend. The board moves from approving last year’s budget plus inflation to approving a portfolio with documented performance per dollar.

The audit win is the headline. The internal cultural win is that the development office stops defending decisions with engagement rates and starts defending them with dollars closed.

Builder takeaway

  • Tag the send with a UTM campaign that maps to a DonorPerfect campaign code. The mapping is what makes everything else possible.
  • Capture UTMs on the donation form into DonorPerfect custom fields. Without this step, the entire attribution chain breaks at the moment of gift.
  • Pledges and payments live in separate pipelines. Counting a five-year pledge as current-quarter revenue is how attribution loses credibility.
