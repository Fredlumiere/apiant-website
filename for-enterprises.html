Where Enterprise Integration Breaks Down

Every department adopts new SaaS tools. Every tool has an API. No one is connecting them deeply enough to trust the data flowing between them.

You have two bad options today: DIY tools like Zapier and Make that your teams cobble together without IT oversight, or enterprise iPaaS platforms like MuleSoft and Workato that demand specialized developers and six-figure contracts. Meanwhile, the deep integrations your business actually needs don't get built.

Shadow IT Integration Debt

Your teams are building Zapier workflows and Make scenarios without IT's knowledge. No error handling. No monitoring. No compliance review. When one breaks at 2 AM and corrupts customer data, you find out from the customer, not from a dashboard.

iPaaS Sticker Shock

Enterprise iPaaS platforms demand specialized developers, extensive training, and contracts that escalate with every connector and every transaction. You're paying enterprise prices for integrations that are still only medium-deep.

The Multi-Department Wall

Marketing needs CRM integrations. Operations needs ERP connections. HR needs HRIS syncs. Finance needs billing reconciliation. Each department fights for engineering resources that never come, or builds fragile workarounds that IT can't support.

What the gap actually looks like

A fitness franchise with 232 locations needs booking data, membership changes, and purchase history synced to a single HubSpot instance with custom objects, bi-directional updates, and rate limiting. A shallow integration creates 232 disconnected data streams. A deep integration understands that when a client cancels Tuesday's class, their "next scheduled visit" refreshes across the entire system. That's the difference between data you can act on and data that misleads you.

Frequently asked questions

Where does our data live, and who has access to it?

Every APIANT deployment runs on a dedicated, isolated server under your control. No shared tenancy. No data commingling with other customers' workloads. Credentials are encrypted at rest, webhook endpoints are HTTPS-only, and role-based access control in the Admin Console governs who sees what. Specific compliance documentation (SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR) is available on request from our team.

How does APIANT fit next to our existing iPaaS (MuleSoft, Boomi, Workato)?

Many enterprises run APIANT alongside a heavyweight iPaaS for specific workloads where that tool is too slow, too expensive, or too shallow. APIANT shines on customer-facing integrations, multi-location deployments, and verticals the big platforms do not cover well. The AI Co-Pilot lets you build a new connector in hours instead of weeks, which is the common reason teams bring us in even when they have an incumbent platform.

What does an SLA look like, and what is your uptime history?

Enterprise customers get a contractual SLA covering uptime, response time, and escalation procedures; specific numbers and credits are negotiated per agreement. Because every deployment is dedicated infrastructure on AWS, uptime is not dragged down by other customers' workloads. Ask our team for the current SLA template and operational history during procurement.

Can APIANT connect to internal systems behind our firewall?

Yes, through secured endpoints, VPN, or private link configurations depending on your environment. Dedicated deployments give you control over network rules, IP allowlists, and credential handling. For SAP, Oracle, legacy ERPs, or internal microservices, we scope the network topology during the pilot phase.

How do we handle change management across dozens of departments on one platform?

The Admin Console gives centralized control over users, automations, connections, and deployments with role-based access. Bulk deployment lets you test a change on one unit, then push it to every department simultaneously; version comparison shows exactly what changed. Master routing directs incoming data to the right business unit automatically, so adding a new department does not require building a new endpoint.

What does the managed pilot actually include?

Under the Managed Pilot, APIANT scopes, builds, and deploys your highest-priority integrations on your dedicated server within weeks. Your IT team gets full visibility through the Admin Console, monitoring dashboards, and execution logs, without carrying the build burden. Support, monitoring, and upgrades are handled by us during the pilot. If the results validate the platform, your team takes ownership of new integrations from there.

What happens to our integrations if we leave APIANT?

You own your data, your credentials, and your connection configurations. Integration logic lives in the Automation Editor as visual recipes; export and documentation are available so your team can rebuild elsewhere if needed. Dedicated deployments mean there is no shared vendor lock at the data layer. Contract terms cover data portability and transition assistance explicitly; we can share the standard enterprise agreement during evaluation.

If Claude Code operates the platform, how do we control and audit what it does?

Every action Claude Code takes runs as a discrete MCP tool call against your dedicated server, and the Admin Console logs it with full execution history. Permissions are scoped: you control which environments and operations Claude Code can reach, and writes to production gate on explicit confirmation. Anything Claude Code builds or changes is visible, attributable, and reversible, and your team reviews automations before they go live. The AI accelerates the work; your governance controls stay in place.
