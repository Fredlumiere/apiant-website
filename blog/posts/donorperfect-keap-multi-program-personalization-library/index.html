Branch-by-branch personalization: a multi-program communication strategy with DonorPerfect and Keap

Fred Lumiere Feb 21, 2025 6 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#Keap#CRMConnect

A library foundation that runs six branch programs (children’s literacy, ESL, digital access, teen workforce, senior services, makerspace) has six donor stories to tell. A donor who funds ESL classes does not want a children’s literacy update. A donor who funds the makerspace does not care about senior services. Yet most foundation newsletters lump everyone together because Keap has no clean view of which programs each donor cares about, and rebuilding six lists by hand every month is not realistic.

The data exists in DonorPerfect. Gift designation, program flag, and giving history per program are right there. The problem is operational: that data has to flow into Keap as structured fields, not as a notes blob, or program-level personalization stays theoretical.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

Generic connectors push the gift amount and the gift date into Keap and lose the rest. The designation gets dropped or stuffed into a free-text field nobody can segment on. Without designation as a structured field, “program-aware personalization” reduces to “we sometimes remember which donors give to which programs.” Six programs and an unstructured field means the segmentation work has to happen in someone’s head every month.

The other failure mode: six separate Keap audiences for six programs. This works for two weeks. By the third month the audiences drift, a cross-program donor exists in four lists at once, and the foundation is back to either spam-blasting or hand-curating.

The third problem is interest without giving. A donor who has attended three ESL fundraisers and given to children’s literacy is genuinely interested in both programs. If the integration only sees designations on gifts, it misses the engagement signal that says “talk to this donor about ESL.” Foundations that rely solely on gift designations leave a large share of upgrade and cross-program opportunity on the table.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and Keap gives you program-level personalization without six parallel databases:

  • CodeSync mirrors DonorPerfect’s Program Designation dropdown as a multi-select Keap field. A donor who gives to three programs has all three values on their record.
  • Flags as Tags converts DonorPerfect program-interest flags into Keap tags. Interest can exist without a gift (volunteer hours, event attendance), so flags and designations together give a richer picture than gifts alone.
  • Detailed donation information sync keeps per-program giving history (lifetime giving to literacy, lifetime giving to ESL) on the donor record.
  • Real-time data sync means a designated gift triggers the relevant program track inside minutes.

Concrete steps

For the illustrative scenario of Heritage Library Foundation, a fictional city library foundation with 2,800 donors across 6 branch programs, the personalization flow looks like this:

  1. Map program designations. In CodeSync, mirror Program Designation as a Keap multi-select dropdown with six values. Mirror Program Interest Flag separately as another multi-select.
  2. Sync per-program giving fields. Custom fields for Lifetime Giving - Literacy, Lifetime Giving - ESL, etc., populated from DonorPerfect totals.
  3. Build one dynamic monthly newsletter. Single Keap email with conditional content blocks. The literacy block shows only if Program Designation contains Literacy OR Program Interest Flag contains Literacy. Same pattern for each program.
  4. Trigger program-specific appeals. When a designated gift comes in, Keap fires a thank-you sequence that speaks to that program specifically: a new literacy donor hears about reading-buddy outcomes; a new ESL donor hears about citizenship class graduations.
  5. Handle cross-program donors gracefully. Donors with two or three designations see all relevant blocks in the monthly email but only one thank-you sequence per gift (triggered by the most recent designation). Avoid the “you got six thank-yous in a week” failure mode.
  6. Coordinate print with digital. When a branch sends a direct-mail appeal, Keap suppresses the same donors from the digital appeal in the same window so they do not get two asks for the same program in the same week.
  7. Per-program ROI reports. Saved Keap reports grouped by Program Designation: cost per acquired donor, second-gift rate, average gift, going back to each branch manager monthly.

Outcome

Representative numbers from comparable mid-size library foundations after wiring this up:

  • Average open rate on the monthly newsletter climbs roughly 8-12 points because content blocks are relevant to each reader.
  • Designated-gift retention (gives again to the same program within 12 months) typically lifts 10-15 points over the undifferentiated baseline.
  • Branch managers stop asking the development office for monthly reports because they have standing Keap views.
  • Cross-program donors give to a second program more often when their first program track surfaces complementary work.
  • Direct-mail print costs drop because the foundation can use targeted print appeals to designated-program donors instead of full-list mailings.

The realistic ceiling: program personalization is most of the lift, not all of it. Subject lines, story quality, and timing still drive opens. The integration removes the structural reason for one-size-fits-all.

The opt-out story matters at a library foundation, where many donors are long-time community members who expect to be treated thoughtfully. Respect Donor Preferences ensures Do Not Email and any program-specific opt-outs (“interested in literacy programs only”) are honored across both systems. A donor who tells the foundation they prefer to hear only about children’s literacy should never receive an ESL appeal, and the integration’s job is to enforce that without requiring the marketing team to remember every preference.

Channel mix and timing

Most library foundations send both print and email. The integration’s value compounds when print and digital share the same source-of-truth segmentation. A donor who receives the children’s literacy print appeal in early March should not also receive the children’s literacy digital appeal that week. Use the synced program designation to coordinate: print one week, digital the next, with the suppression handled automatically.

Print appeals also benefit from designation-aware copy. The shell remains the same, the inserts and the giving form change based on the donor’s primary program. The donor receives a piece of mail that looks like it was written for them, because in a meaningful sense it was. Print costs may go up slightly per piece, but per-dollar-raised drops.

Timing matters across channels. Branch managers tend to want to launch program-specific appeals around their own program milestones (summer reading kickoff, fall ESL enrollment, holiday digital-access drives). The integration’s program-designation field makes those branch-driven calendars feasible without each branch needing its own marketing infrastructure. The development office becomes the orchestrator, not the bottleneck.

Builder takeaway

  • Designation as a multi-select, not single. Real donors give to multiple programs; modeling it as single-select forces ugly compromises.
  • One dynamic email beats six segmented emails. Six parallel newsletters means six approval cycles, six layouts, six chances to make mistakes. Conditional blocks scale.
  • Suppress digital when print is in flight. Cross-channel coordination is the difference between thoughtful and exhausting.
