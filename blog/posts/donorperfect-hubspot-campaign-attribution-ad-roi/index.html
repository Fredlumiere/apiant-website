Closing the loop on fundraising ad spend: campaign attribution from DonorPerfect into HubSpot deals

Fred Lumiere Sep 8, 2025 6 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#HubSpot#CRMConnect

Most nonprofit marketing directors can tell you their Meta cost per click to the penny. Ask them their Meta cost per acquired donor and the answer gets vague. Ask them the lifetime value of a donor acquired through a specific Google search campaign versus a direct mail drop and you get a shrug and a promise to pull it together for the next board meeting.

The data exists. It just lives in three places that do not talk to each other. UTM parameters live in the ad platform. Form submissions live in DonorPerfect. Email engagement lives in HubSpot. Stitching them together happens once a quarter in a spreadsheet that nobody trusts.

Nonprofit marketers have tried this every way. Each fails for a specific reason.

DonorPerfect’s native reports tell you which Campaign or Solicitation code drove a gift, but only if someone tagged the gift correctly. Online donation forms drop UTM parameters on the way in, so a $250 gift that started from a Meta ad ends up coded as a generic Online Donation.

Google Analytics and Meta Ads Manager report on goal completions and conversions, but they cannot see the actual dollar amount of the gift. A $25 gift and a $2,500 gift look identical in the goals report.

Generic iPaaS connectors sync the contact but not the gift, or sync the gift as a flat dollar amount with no campaign code attached. You end up knowing that someone gave, but not why.

DIY spreadsheets work in February when there are 40 gifts to reconcile. They collapse in November when there are 4,000.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and HubSpot is built to carry the full Gift record, not just the donor. Combined with the optional Gifts Pipeline add-on, every DonorPerfect gift becomes a HubSpot deal with the full campaign metadata attached.

The features that make attribution actually work:

  • Gifts Pipeline (optional add-on) writes each DonorPerfect gift as a HubSpot deal with amount, gift date, gift type, campaign, solicitation, sub-solicitation, and appeal code.
  • Campaign performance tracking keeps DonorPerfect Campaign and Solicitation codes flowing into HubSpot deal properties.
  • CodeSync for Dropdowns mirrors DonorPerfect campaign codes into HubSpot dropdowns automatically. Add a new appeal in DonorPerfect, the HubSpot deal property updates, the existing dashboards keep working.
  • Custom field sync carries UTM parameters captured on the donation form into HubSpot deal properties so the source and medium ride along with the gift.
  • Advertising ROI Reporting (requires HubSpot Marketing Hub Enterprise and connected ad accounts) closes the loop between ad spend and reported revenue.

Closing the attribution loop in five steps

Illustrative scenario: Lakeside Symphony Society, a mid-size regional symphony running 14 paid digital campaigns per year (hypothetical example, not a real organization).

Step 1. Capture UTMs at the donation form. Configure the DonorPerfect donation form to write utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content, and utm_term into custom DonorPerfect fields on the Gift record. Most online giving setups can do this with hidden form fields populated by a small JavaScript snippet that reads the query string. This is the only manual configuration step on the DonorPerfect side.

Step 2. Map UTM fields to HubSpot deal properties. In CRMConnect’s field mapping, point each UTM custom field to the matching HubSpot deal property. Create the deal properties if they do not exist: Original Source, Original Medium, Original Campaign, Original Content, Original Term. Keep them on the deal, not the contact: a single donor can give multiple times from different campaigns, and you want to attribute each gift to its actual source.

Step 3. Build a HubSpot deal pipeline that mirrors the giving lifecycle. A symphony deal pipeline typically has stages like Inquiry, Pledged, First Payment Received, Paid in Full, Lapsed. CRMConnect can populate the appropriate stage based on DonorPerfect Gift Type (Pledge, Pledge Payment, One-Time). For multi-year pledges, each payment becomes its own deal so cash-based reporting stays accurate.

Step 4. Build the closed-loop dashboards. Three HubSpot reports do most of the work:

  • Revenue by Original Source, last 90 days: a bar chart of summed deal amount grouped by Original Source. Cross-reference with ad spend pulled in via the Marketing Hub ad integration.
  • Cost per Acquired Donor by Campaign: for each campaign, total ad spend divided by the count of unique contacts whose first deal is attributed to that campaign.
  • Twelve-Month Donor LTV by Acquisition Source: sum of all deals over twelve months for contacts whose Original Source matches each ad channel. This is the number that actually drives budget decisions.

Step 5. Use the data mid-quarter. A symphony has 14 campaigns a year, so eight weeks of underperformance is half a campaign cycle. Build a weekly automated email from HubSpot that lists every active campaign, its spend, its attributed revenue, and its cost per acquired donor. Give the marketing director permission to reallocate budget based on the report, not the quarterly board deck.

Edge cases that quietly break attribution

Pledges spanning fiscal years. A donor pledges $5,000 in May, pays $1,000 in May, $2,000 in October, and $2,000 in February. If you treat the pledge as a single deal, your May campaign report shows $5,000 in revenue that you did not collect. Configure CRMConnect to create a deal per payment and a separate Pledge Commitment deal for the total, in different pipelines. Most attribution dashboards should use the payment pipeline.

Soft credits. A foundation gives $25,000 but the proposal was driven by a board member who deserves credit on the relationship side. Soft credits should sync as a separate deal type tagged for relationship attribution but excluded from cash-based ROI dashboards.

Multi-touch attribution. A donor sees a Meta ad in June, opens three email campaigns in August, clicks a Google search ad in October, and gives in November. UTM parameters capture only the last touch. For a symphony with long consideration windows, supplement UTM attribution with HubSpot’s first-touch and multi-touch revenue attribution reports, which already exist in Marketing Hub Enterprise.

Matching gifts. A $500 gift from an individual produces a $500 matching gift from their employer two months later. Tag the matching gift with the same Original Source as the originating gift so your campaign ROI does not undercount the true revenue impact.

What it looks like in production

For an illustrative organization the size of Lakeside Symphony ($6.8M budget, 14 paid campaigns), a properly built attribution stack typically reveals that two or three campaigns are producing 60% to 70% of attributable revenue, while a long tail of “we have always done it” campaigns are producing fractional ROI. Reallocating spend toward the winners usually moves total acquired-donor revenue 25% to 40% within two campaign cycles, with no incremental budget.

That is the headline number. The quieter win is that the marketing director stops defending campaign decisions with vibes and starts defending them with deals closed.

Builder takeaway

  • Capture UTM parameters at the donation form into DonorPerfect custom fields. That is the one piece of manual setup that makes the rest of the stack work.
  • Attribute on the deal, not the contact. A donor who gives twice from two different campaigns is two attribution events, not one.
  • Create a deal per pledge payment instead of one deal per pledge. Cash-based dashboards need to match cash that actually arrived.
