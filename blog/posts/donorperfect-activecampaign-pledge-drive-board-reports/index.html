Live pledge-drive dashboards for board and underwriting: DonorPerfect data piped into ActiveCampaign reports

Fred Lumiere Feb 3, 2026 7 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#ActiveCampaign#CRMConnect

Public radio fundraising is a leadership exercise compressed into 72 hours. Two pledge drives a year carry most of the station’s sustainer growth. The board treasurer wants hourly numbers during the drive. The underwriting team wants to know which on-air pitches are landing. The general manager wants to make the call on whether to extend the drive by a day.

Without the data, those decisions get made on intuition and yesterday’s totals. The DonorPerfect gift report is run once a day, at most. The board treasurer asks for an update at 4 PM and gets it at 5 PM tomorrow. By then the drive has moved past the point where the data would have mattered.

Pledge drives are the worst stress test of a nonprofit data stack. Volume spikes, latency intolerance, and stakeholder demands all hit at once.

Native DonorPerfect reports are powerful but designed for scheduled runs, not live dashboards. Running them every fifteen minutes during a drive is technically possible and operationally exhausting.

Generic iPaaS connectors that batch sync every six hours are useless during a 72-hour drive. By the time the batch arrives, half the drive is over.

Custom-built dashboards on top of DonorPerfect work for the analyst who built them and nobody else. When the analyst is in the studio managing the drive, the board treasurer cannot read the dashboard.

ActiveCampaign has the dashboard and segmentation muscle but cannot see DonorPerfect gifts without a real-time integration.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and ActiveCampaign syncs in real time, which is the only mode that matters during a pledge drive. The features that carry the dashboard:

  • Real-time donor sync writes new sustainer signups, recurring upgrades, and one-time gifts into ActiveCampaign within seconds of the DonorPerfect record.
  • Easy Donation Tracking writes each gift as an ActiveCampaign deal with amount, gift type (Recurring, One-Time, Pledge), and campaign code attached.
  • Flags as Tags Sync carries DonorPerfect Flags as ActiveCampaign tags so new sustainers are immediately tagged for thank-you sequences.
  • CodeSync keeps Drive Day Codes consistent across both systems.
  • Bi-directional sync rolls sustainer LTV calculations back into DonorPerfect custom fields after the drive.

Building the live pledge-drive dashboard

Illustrative scenario: WPRN Public Radio, an NPR member station with 18,500 sustaining members running two on-air pledge drives per year (hypothetical example, not a real organization).

Step 1. Tag every gift with a drive identifier. Configure the DonorPerfect online and phone-in donation forms to write a Drive Day Code into a custom field on every gift during the drive window. CodeSync mirrors this code into ActiveCampaign as a deal property automatically.

Step 2. Build the board-facing dashboard. Four numbers carry the entire board view:

  • Gifts per hour, rolling. A line chart of gift count per hour for the trailing 24 hours, overlaid against the same window in the previous drive. This shows pacing better than any other single metric.
  • New sustainers added today. A counter that increments in near real time as new recurring gifts land in DonorPerfect.
  • Average monthly amount of new sustainers. Today’s new sustainers averaged against this drive’s running total and last drive’s final number.
  • Total drive revenue against goal. The fundraising thermometer. The single number the board treasurer texts the general manager about at midnight.

Step 3. Build the underwriter view. A separate dashboard with different cuts:

  • Conversion by on-air pitch. Tag each on-air pitch with a unique campaign code (in the form 2026SPRING_PITCH_DAY1_HOUR3). Track gift count and total revenue per pitch. The morning team can adjust the afternoon pitches based on what landed.
  • Segment conversion. New donors versus renewing sustainers versus upgrading sustainers. Some pitches resonate with new prospects, others with the renewal base. Segmenting reveals what is working with whom.
  • Channel split. Online versus phone versus mobile. If online is underperforming a normal drive cycle, the underwriting team can check whether the form is broken or whether the audio promo is weak.

Step 4. Trigger thank-you sequences instantly. The moment a new sustainer signs up in DonorPerfect, the gift syncs to ActiveCampaign as a deal. Trigger a sustainer welcome series on the deal-created event:

  • Within 5 minutes: a thank-you email with the sustainer’s gift amount, schedule, and a station-specific welcome from the general manager.
  • Day 2: a “welcome to the sustainer family” message with member benefits (event invites, advance ticket access, member newsletter signup).
  • Day 7: a behind-the-scenes story about the station’s reporting from a recent week.

Step 5. Post-drive: write LTV back to DonorPerfect. After the drive closes, calculate twelve-month sustainer LTV per cohort in ActiveCampaign and write the value back to DonorPerfect custom fields. The development director sees campaign performance in DonorPerfect without opening ActiveCampaign.

Avoiding dashboard fatigue

It is tempting to put twenty metrics on the drive dashboard. Resist. The four numbers above (gifts per hour, new sustainers, average monthly amount, revenue against goal) drive 90% of the operational decisions during the drive. Adding more metrics dilutes attention and makes the dashboard harder to read during high-stress moments.

Save the deeper cuts for the underwriter dashboard, which the underwriting team reviews on a different cadence (every two to three hours) for tactical adjustments.

After the drive, build a post-mortem dashboard with everything: conversion by pitch, by hour, by segment, by channel. That is where the strategic lessons live. During the drive, four numbers are enough.

Handling the edge cases

Recurring upgrades versus new sustainers. A donor who increases their monthly gift from $10 to $25 is not a new sustainer. They are an upgrade. Tag them separately so the new sustainer counter does not inflate.

Sustainer cancellations. Some donors cancel during a drive. Show net new sustainers (additions minus cancellations) as well as gross. The board treasurer needs both numbers.

Phone gifts processed in batches. Phone gifts often come through a separate batch processor that lands in DonorPerfect on a delay. Build a delay indicator into the dashboard so the team knows the phone numbers may lag by an hour.

Hard bounces. During a drive, you will get a flood of new email addresses, many of them mistyped. ActiveCampaign will mark bounces. Write them back to DonorPerfect so the post-drive followup cycle does not waste mail on dead addresses.

Communication preferences. Respect them. A new sustainer who opts out of email at signup should not get the welcome series. The DonorPerfect communication preference field must flow through to ActiveCampaign suppression on the same sync.

What it looks like in production

For an illustrative station the size of WPRN (18,500 sustainers, $6.9M budget), moving from yesterday’s gift report to a live dashboard during pledge drives typically shifts two operational decisions per drive (extending the drive, reallocating on-air pitch focus) that together produce 5% to 10% incremental drive revenue. On a $400,000 drive, that is $20,000 to $40,000 of incremental revenue per drive, twice a year, attributable entirely to better decisions made faster.

The harder-to-measure benefit is institutional trust. The board treasurer stops asking “what is happening” and starts asking “what should we do next.” That conversation only happens when the data is in front of both people at the same time.

Builder takeaway

  • Four numbers carry the board view during a drive. Adding a fifth and sixth metric makes the dashboard harder to read when reading speed matters most.
  • Tag every gift with a campaign code that maps to the pitch that drove it. Pitch-level attribution is the data the underwriting team needs to adjust mid-drive.
  • Trigger the sustainer welcome series instantly, not at the end of the drive. A new sustainer who waits three days for a thank-you is a sustainer at higher churn risk.
