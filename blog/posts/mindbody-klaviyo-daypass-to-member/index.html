Tracking Day-Pass to Membership Conversion in Klaviyo Using Mindbody Visit Data

Fred Lumiere Apr 13, 2026 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Klaviyo#CRMConnect

The Day-Pass Attribution Problem

Day passes are the cheapest and most common entry point into a climbing gym, a yoga studio, or any drop-in fitness business. They are also the worst-tracked piece of the funnel. A walk-in buys a day pass, climbs for two hours, leaves, and either becomes a member three weeks later or never returns. Whether that conversion is 5 percent or 25 percent should govern your entire acquisition strategy. Most operators do not know which.

Hypothetical: Basalt and Belay Climbing Co (illustrative) is a 1,100-member gym with a healthy day-pass mix. Their Klaviyo account has every email subscriber and every membership signup. What it does not have is a clean link between the two. The day-pass purchase, the second visit, the third visit, and the eventual membership signup are four separate events to Klaviyo, and they are not tied to a single identifier.

Why Off-the-Shelf Tools Fail Here

The shared identifier problem is real. Mindbody assigns a client ID at the first transaction, but if the same human buys their next day pass with a slightly different email (autofilled by a different browser, or a guest checkout), Mindbody creates a duplicate. Klaviyo then sees two profiles. The conversion math falls apart at the deduplication step, not at the analytics step.

A Zap or a custom script can sometimes match on email, but it cannot reliably dedupe on name plus phone, and it cannot reconcile the visit count when a profile is merged later. The integration is doable, but the deduplication and visit reconciliation are where most builds stall.

The APIANT Approach

CRMConnect Mindbody to Klaviyo handles this with two features that work together:

  • Visit Tracking captures recent client visits, with daily reconciliation so re-entered or merged records do not create duplicate visit events.
  • Purchase and Sales Line Items Sync captures the day-pass purchase as a structured event with the SKU intact, separate from a membership signup SKU.

On the Klaviyo side, profiles are matched on email and updated in place. If a Mindbody record is later merged, the unified history follows. The result is a single Klaviyo profile per real human, with the day-pass purchase, the visit count, and the eventual membership signup all on the same timeline.

What Visit Tracking Surfaces in Klaviyo

Each profile carries a running set of attributes that make day-pass attribution possible:

Klaviyo profile attributes:
- mindbody_first_purchase_sku        e.g. DAYPASS-ADULT
- mindbody_first_visit_at            ISO timestamp
- mindbody_visit_count               int
- mindbody_last_visit_at             ISO timestamp
- mindbody_membership_status         active|expired|none
- mindbody_membership_started_at     ISO timestamp (null until conversion)

The conversion event you care about is the moment mindbody_membership_started_at flips from null to a timestamp on a profile whose mindbody_first_purchase_sku was a day pass. That single comparison is the answer to “how many day-pass buyers became members.”

Designing the Daypass-Visitor Conversion Flow

Once the attributes are in place, the flow is straightforward:

  1. Trigger: Mindbody day-pass purchase event.
  2. Wait 4 hours. Same-day climb context.
  3. Email 1 (evening of climb): “Hope the session was good. Here is your 7-day return pass at $5 off.”
  4. Branch on mindbody_visit_count after 7 days:
  5. Still 1: send “first climbs are the hardest” email with a beginner clinic offer.
  6. 2 to 3: send “you are getting hooked” email with a membership comparison.
  7. 4 or more: send “do the math” email with the break-even point for membership vs. day passes.
  8. Suppress on mindbody_membership_status = active. Stops nagging anyone who converted.

The third bucket is the high-conversion segment. Four visits in a week from someone paying day pass rates is a member who has not signed up yet. The email basically writes itself.

Reading the Conversion Report Honestly

The temptation is to count every membership signup that follows any day pass. The honest number is narrower. Use this segmentation in Klaviyo:

  • Cohort: day-pass buyers in calendar month N.
  • Window: 60 days from first purchase.
  • Converted: mindbody_membership_started_at falls within the window.

Report two ratios: 30-day conversion and 60-day conversion. Anything past 60 days is a separate marketing story and should not be credited to the day-pass channel. Operators who collapse the window to “ever” overstate the channel by 2x or more.

Representative Conversion Rate at 1,100 Members

Illustrative composites, not a real customer:

  • 30-day day-pass-to-member conversion baseline (no targeted flow): roughly 4 to 7 percent.
  • 30-day conversion with the flow above: a lift into the 9 to 14 percent range, mostly from the 2-to-3-visit and 4-plus segments where the offer is well-timed.
  • Average day passes per converted member before signing up: 2.6 (which is itself a useful number, because it tells your front desk where to start the conversation).

Your numbers will move with pricing, climber demographics, and how punishing the day-pass-to-membership math is. The mechanism is the same.

Builder Takeaway

  • The whole game is on the shared identifier. Get email-based dedup right at the integration layer or none of the downstream math works.
  • Use mindbody_visit_count as the branching key, not days since first visit. Two visits in a week beats two visits in a month for membership intent.
  • Constrain your conversion window to 30 or 60 days. “Ever” is a flattering lie that hides where the channel actually stops working.
