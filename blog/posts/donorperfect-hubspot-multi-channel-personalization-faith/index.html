From one generic newsletter to four life-stage tracks: multi-channel personalization for a faith community

Fred Lumiere Sep 17, 2024 6 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#HubSpot#CRMConnect

A faith community is not a marketing audience. It is a network of relationships at different life stages, different giving capacities, and different points in their walk with the community. Treating it like a single email list flattens everything that matters about it.

Most congregations end up there anyway. The pastoral team wants to write one personal note per family per month. The reality is one generic newsletter blasted to 540 households, with a hopeful “Dear Friend” salutation and no real segmentation. The new family who joined last week gets the same message as the legacy donor whose family has given for three generations. Engagement rates show the cost.

Faith communities have specific constraints that generic marketing tools do not handle well.

Pastoral sensitivity matters. A donor going through a hard season should not get an aggressive year-end ask. A family that just lost a member should not receive the standard “we missed you on Sunday” template. The data needed to honor those moments lives in pastoral notes inside DonorPerfect (or its companion church management system), not in a marketing platform.

Tribute gifts need careful handling. A memorial gift in someone’s honor often comes from a family member during grief. The thank-you, the acknowledgment to the honoree’s family, and the future communication cadence are all different from a standard gift.

Communication preferences are not optional. A congregation that does not respect “do not contact” or “email only, no phone” preferences is breaking trust the worst possible way.

Generic newsletter tools, even good ones like Mailchimp or ConvertKit, cannot do this on their own because the segmentation data they need lives in DonorPerfect.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and HubSpot was designed for the data shape of a relational organization, not a transactional one. Faith communities benefit from the same features that work for advocacy groups and small foundations:

  • Donor Type sync carries the Donor Type code (Member, Visitor, Friend, Legacy Society, etc.) from DonorPerfect into HubSpot as a contact property and dropdown.
  • Flags Sync as Tags mirrors DonorPerfect Flags as HubSpot multi-select tags. A congregation that already tracks Youth Ministry Parent, Choir, Bereaved (Last 12 Months), or Capital Campaign Donor as flags gets those tags inside HubSpot automatically.
  • Custom Fields carry ministry interests, household role (Primary Giver, Secondary, Adult Dependent), and pastoral care status into HubSpot for segmentation.
  • Communication Preferences flow through: No Email, No Phone, No Mail, Preferred Method. HubSpot workflows must respect these without exception.
  • Tribute Management carries tribute description, honoree name, and acknowledgment recipient into HubSpot deal properties so memorial gifts route to a separate, sensitive workflow.
  • CodeSync for Dropdowns keeps Ministry Interest, Donor Type, and other dropdown values in lockstep across both systems.

Designing four life-stage tracks

Illustrative scenario: Grace Hill Congregation, a 950-member community with 540 giving households (hypothetical example, not a real organization).

Track 1: New Family (joined in the last 12 months). A 90-day onboarding cadence focused on belonging, not giving. Welcome email from the lead pastor. Invitation to a newcomer brunch. A short video tour of ministries the family might find a home in. A first ask, if appropriate, only after day 60 and only if the family has shown engagement signal (attended an event, RSVP’d to a small group). Triggered by Donor Created Date in DonorPerfect or by a New Member flag.

Track 2: Established Giver (regular giving household, more than 12 months). A monthly cadence with three components: a pastoral message tied to the liturgical calendar, a story from a ministry the household has expressed interest in (pulled from Ministry Interest flags), and a brief year-to-date giving statement quarterly. Avoid generic asks. The household has chosen to give; the message should reinforce why.

Track 3: Legacy Donor (cumulative giving above the legacy threshold or in the Legacy Society flag). Quarterly cadence, mostly relational. Personal notes from the senior pastor on major milestones, invitations to small donor-circle events, and a single annual stewardship conversation rather than a year-end appeal cycle. These donors should never receive a templated mass email.

Track 4: Lapsed Member (no recorded gift in the last 14 months, was previously regular). A gentle, non-transactional sequence: a check-in note (“we have not seen you in a while, is everything okay?”), an invitation to a midweek service, and a pastoral phone call task assigned to a clergy member. No giving ask in the first 60 days of the sequence. The point is reconnection, not recovery.

Channel orchestration without crossing the line

Email is the easy channel. SMS is the one that can backfire. Use HubSpot SMS only for households who have explicitly opted in to text communication, marked as a flag in DonorPerfect and synced as a HubSpot tag. Time SMS sends carefully: between 9 AM and 7 PM in the household’s local timezone, never on a holy day, never during a service.

Print direct mail is still effective for legacy donors and bereaved households. Use HubSpot as the segmentation engine and a print partner for production. Trigger the print job from HubSpot via webhook when the appropriate workflow stage fires.

Tribute and memorial gifts on a separate track

A tribute gift triggers two parallel processes:

  • The donor receives a standard thank-you with the tribute description acknowledged.
  • The honoree’s family (or the honoree, for an “in honor of” gift) receives a separate acknowledgment letter, not a fundraising email. The honoree’s contact record in DonorPerfect should not be enrolled in the standard donor cultivation track based on the tribute gift alone.

Configure CRMConnect’s Tribute Management sync so the tribute honoree fields are mapped to HubSpot deal properties. Build a workflow that fires on tribute gifts and excludes the honoree’s contact from all marketing sequences for at least 90 days.

Quiet hours, opt-outs, and pastoral judgment

Three rules that have to be enforced in the workflow, not assumed:

  • Honor No Contact. A household flagged Do Not Solicit in DonorPerfect should be excluded from every automated send, period. This is not optional.
  • Quiet hours. No automated sends between 9 PM and 7 AM in the household’s local timezone. No sends on Sunday morning or on observed holy days.
  • Bereavement override. A household flagged Bereaved (Last 12 Months) skips all fundraising sends but continues to receive pastoral check-ins. Build this as a HubSpot list exclusion that sits on every fundraising workflow.

Hard-bounces and unsubscribes from HubSpot write back to DonorPerfect via custom field sync, so the church administrator does not call a household whose email has been dead for a year.

What it looks like in production

For an illustrative congregation the size of Grace Hill (540 giving households, $1.1M operating budget), moving from one generic newsletter to four life-stage tracks typically lifts engagement on the segmented sends well above the flat-list baseline. More importantly, it removes the awkwardness of asking a new family for a year-end gift in November or sending a stewardship video to a household in grief. The pastoral staff get their judgment encoded in the workflow rather than relying on memory.

Builder takeaway

  • Build the suppression layer first. Quiet hours, bereavement flag, Do Not Solicit, and hard-bounce sync into DonorPerfect have to be enforced before you turn on a single send.
  • Tribute gifts trigger two parallel workflows, not one. The donor and the honoree’s family need separate treatment.
  • Use Donor Type and Ministry Interest as the segmentation backbone. They already exist in DonorPerfect. Mirroring them into HubSpot via CodeSync and Flags-as-Tags is faster than building a new taxonomy.
