Practitioner and Location Reporting for a Multi-Disciplinary Clinic With Cliniko + HubSpot

Fred Lumiere Oct 7, 2024 4 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#HubSpot#CRMConnect#For Enterprise

Once a clinic crosses a dozen practitioners across multiple disciplines, the answer to “who is performing” stops being something you can eyeball from a Cliniko export. The same question gets asked every week by the practice manager, every month by the COO, and every quarter by the board, and the answers should not require a custom export each time.

Take an illustrative example: Harbourview Allied Health Collective, a hypothetical 4-location operation with 22 practitioners across physio, occupational therapy, dietetics, and exercise physiology. The practice manager cannot answer simple cross-discipline questions. How many shared patients does physio refer into dietetics in a month? Which practitioner has the highest rebooking rate? Which location’s calendar utilisation is dragging the network average down? Each question needs a fresh CSV, a fresh pivot, and a fresh round of joining patient IDs by hand.

The cross-discipline questions you actually need answered

A 22-practitioner clinic operates as a small network, not a single practice. Leadership needs answers like:

  • Which practitioners are at capacity and which are below 60 percent utilisation?
  • Which discipline pairings produce the most internal referrals (physio to dietetics, OT to exercise physiology)?
  • Which location has the highest lifetime value per new patient, and what explains the gap?
  • Which practitioner converts initial assessments into care plans at the best rate?

None of these survive in a flat Cliniko export. They need a relational model where practitioner, business location, appointment, patient, and case are all first-class objects you can pivot freely.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

Cliniko’s native reporting answers the basics (revenue per practitioner, appointments per day) but does not support the joins that cross-discipline reporting requires. BI tools layered on a one-way Cliniko export end up stale, lose the patient-to-patient referral relationship, and require an analyst to maintain. A pasted-spreadsheet approach is fine at 6 practitioners and impossible at 22.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to HubSpot exposes Cliniko Appointments, Patient profile, Invoices, Practitioners, Businesses (locations), and Case Information as native HubSpot objects with the right relationships preserved. The Appointments Pipeline already carries practitioner and business location on every deal, which means HubSpot reports and dashboards can partition by either dimension without any additional engineering. The bi-directional patient sync preserves referral threads even when patients see multiple practitioners across multiple disciplines.

Concrete steps

  1. Confirm the Appointment, Patient, Invoice, Practitioner, and Business syncs are all on. The first three are typically the default, but Practitioner and Business as standalone entities are what unlock cross-clinic reporting.
  2. Build a Practitioner Performance report. Group by practitioner, with columns for arrived appointments (last 30 days), DNA rate, rebooking rate (% of arrived appointments with a subsequent booking in 28 days), and average Appointment Deal Amount.
  3. Build a Location Performance report. Group by business location, with columns for calendar utilisation (arrived appointments versus practitioner-hours available), new patient acquisition (contacts with first appointment in the period), and average lifetime value across the patient cohort at that location.
  4. Build the internal referral report. Use HubSpot deal associations to identify patients with appointments across multiple disciplines. Group by source discipline and destination discipline. This is the report that tells you whether your multi-disciplinary model is actually multi-disciplinary or just co-located solo practices.
  5. Partition access by location. Use HubSpot teams aligned to Cliniko business locations so each clinic manager sees only their own contacts and deals. The integration’s location field on every deal is what makes this enforceable.

Outcome

A clinic matching Harbourview’s profile typically discovers within the first month that two or three practitioners are running at 95 percent utilisation while two others are below 55, that one location accounts for 80 percent of all physio-to-dietetics internal referrals while the other three are basically zero, and that the highest-lifetime-value location is not the one with the most marketing spend. Each of those is a decision waiting to happen. The reports update in real time, the operations director stops asking for “the spreadsheet,” and quarterly board reporting takes 20 minutes instead of two days.

What you should not do with practitioner reporting

Practitioner-level metrics are a management tool, not a public scoreboard. Two cautions worth naming:

  • Do not turn appointment volume into a leaderboard published to the clinic Slack. Different disciplines have different appointment lengths and different case complexity. A 75 minute pelvic physio assessment is not equivalent to a 20 minute follow-up adjustment.
  • Do not surface clinical metadata (case notes, diagnoses, treatment specifics) in HubSpot dashboards. The Case Information sync exposes structural data like session limits and milestones, which is operational. Clinical narrative stays in Cliniko.

Builder takeaway

  • The integration gives you Practitioner and Business as real HubSpot dimensions. Build the reports against those dimensions, not against text fields scraped from appointment notes.
  • The internal referral report is the highest-value thing a multi-disciplinary clinic can build, because it tells you whether your model is working. Most clinics never build it.
  • Use HubSpot teams plus the Business (location) field to enforce permissioning. Cross-clinic reporting at the COO level, scoped reporting at the location level, both off the same data.
