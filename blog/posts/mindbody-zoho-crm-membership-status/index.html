Real-Time Membership Status in Zoho CRM for a Two-Location Medspa

Fred Lumiere Mar 16, 2026 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Zoho CRM#CRMConnect#Bi-Directional Sync

The Renewal Conversation Reps Aren’t Having

Medspa memberships are a steady revenue floor and a steady churn risk. A six-month aesthetics membership at $299/month is roughly $1,800 in revenue per member, and the renewal conversation needs to happen 30 to 60 days before expiration to land. If the sales team only sees membership data in a Mindbody report that runs at month-end, the renewal conversation is already late.

Hypothetical: Vesper Wellness Medspa (illustrative) is a two-location medspa with seven providers, 2,200 patients, and an active membership program. The sales team works out of Zoho CRM. The clinical team works out of Mindbody. The handoff between the two has historically been “ask the front desk.” Renewals get missed, lapsed memberships sit for weeks without outreach, and the sales team only finds out at the all-hands report.

Why Off-the-Shelf Tools Fail Here

Membership data is more than a single field. A Mindbody membership has:

  • Status (active, expired, suspended, cancelled).
  • Start and end dates.
  • Sessions remaining and sessions used (for session-based plans).
  • Auto-renewal flag.
  • Linked contract (if contract-based).

A generic glue tool can pull one of these (usually status), but the renewal conversation needs all of them. The “sessions remaining” field is the one that drives the “use it before it expires” outreach, and it changes every time a session is booked, used, or cancelled.

The APIANT Approach

CRMConnect Mindbody to Zoho CRM uses dedicated custom objects in Zoho CRM to hold the full membership picture. From the API App page: “Real-time updates on membership status, sessions remaining, renewals, and expirations - all synced into Zoho CRM so your team can act on at-risk members before they leave.”

The relevant features:

  • Membership and Contract Sync with a dedicated MB Memberships custom object in Zoho CRM.
  • Two-Way Client Profile Sync to keep contact and lead records aligned.
  • Real-Time Data Sync so changes appear in Zoho CRM within seconds.
  • Custom Object Mapping so the membership data is queryable in workflows and reports.

The MB Memberships object is the source of truth on the Zoho side. Multiple memberships per patient (which is common in a medspa) live as separate records linked to the contact.

What MB Memberships Surfaces in Zoho CRM

The custom object holds the full record:

Zoho CRM custom object: MB Memberships
- membership_id
- contact_id (links to Zoho CRM contact)
- membership_name              e.g. "Aesthetic Essentials 6-Month"
- status                       active|expired|suspended|cancelled
- start_date / end_date
- sessions_total
- sessions_used
- sessions_remaining           (computed live)
- auto_renew                   true|false
- last_payment_at
- next_payment_at
- mindbody_site_id

The fields that drive workflows: end_date for renewal timing, sessions_remaining for utilization nudges, status for lapsed-member catching.

Designing the Pre-Expiration Outreach Workflow

The Zoho CRM workflow runs daily against the MB Memberships object:

  1. Day -60: Workflow creates a task for the assigned sales rep, “Renewal conversation: [patient_name].” Includes the membership name, end date, and sessions remaining as task fields.
  2. Day -45: Branch on sessions_remaining:
  3. More than 4: trigger a “use what you have” email to the patient with a booking link.
  4. 4 or fewer: skip, the patient is using the membership and is renewal-likely.
  5. Day -30: Mandatory call task for the rep, due same day. Auto-escalates to manager at 24 hours if not completed.
  6. Day -14: Email to the patient with renewal terms and a one-click renewal link.
  7. Day -3: SMS reminder.
  8. Day 0: Membership expires. If auto_renew = true and next_payment_at clears, status flips to active for the next term and the flow exits.
  9. Day +1: If status is expired, trigger the lapsed-member sequence.

The Day -30 mandatory call is the conversion driver. It is on the calendar, it has the context, it is escalated. The rep does it.

Catching Lapsed Members Before They Drift

The lapsed-member sequence runs against status = expired and end_date > now() - 30 days:

  1. Day +1 after expiry: Personal email from the rep with a “your spot is still here” offer.
  2. Day +7: SMS, “Want me to extend your last sessions another 30 days?” (Use the auto-pay status as a branch: if auto-pay failed at expiry, this is a card-on-file problem, not a churn problem.)
  3. Day +14: Manager review meeting. Rep brings the lapsed list. Decisions made for the high-LTV patients.
  4. Day +30: Lead status auto-changes to “Inactive Member” for the long-cycle win-back nurture.

The Day +14 manager review is the part that pays for itself. The data is in Zoho. The conversation happens. Decisions get made.

Representative Renewal Rate at 2,200 Patients

Illustrative composites, not a real customer:

  • Membership renewal rate baseline (manual end-of-month process): roughly 55 to 65 percent.
  • With the 60-30-3 cadence on live data: a lift into the 70 to 80 percent range.
  • Lapsed-member recovery within 30 days: a lift in the 8 to 14 percentage-point range, mostly from the Day +1 email landing while the membership is still front-of-mind.
  • “Did Patty’s membership expire?” Slack messages: down dramatically, because the data is on the record.

The lift comes from making the renewal conversation a calendared event with context, not a reaction to a monthly report.

Builder Takeaway

  • Use a dedicated custom object for memberships. A medspa patient with three concurrent memberships is unmanageable on the contact record alone.
  • Run renewal workflows on end_date, not on created_date + duration. Mindbody is the source of truth for the actual end date, and it changes more than you would think.
  • Branch the lapsed sequence on auto-pay status. A failed-card lapse is a 24-hour fix. A “they meant to cancel” lapse needs a different conversation.
