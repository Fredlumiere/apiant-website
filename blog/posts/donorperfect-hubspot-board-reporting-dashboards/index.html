Killing the monthly board-deck rebuild: real-time fundraising dashboards from DonorPerfect in HubSpot

Fred Lumiere Apr 20, 2026 5 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#HubSpot#CRMConnect

The monthly fundraising board report is the single most expensive recurring document a small nonprofit produces. Not in dollars. In hours. The development director spends three days every month pulling DonorPerfect exports, pasting them into Excel, building pivot tables, color-coding LYBUNT and SYBUNT lists, and stitching the result into slides. By the time the deck reaches the board, the data is two weeks stale and the development director has lost half a workweek they will never get back.

The pattern repeats at every K-12 foundation, alumni fund, and community foundation in the country. The board wants trend lines, segment cuts, and progress against goal. The data exists in DonorPerfect. The visualization layer does not. So the visualization layer becomes a person.

The obvious fix is a BI tool. Most of them collapse under nonprofit constraints.

Power BI and Tableau both require a direct DonorPerfect database connection or a scheduled export pipeline, plus someone who knows DAX or LOD. That someone usually does not exist on a four-person development team.

Native DonorPerfect dashboards have improved meaningfully but slice giving data the way DonorPerfect wants to slice it, not the way a school district board wants to see it. Trying to combine giving data with alumni engagement, parent participation, or event attendance requires data that is not in DonorPerfect.

Generic iPaaS tools sync contacts but not the rich financial properties (lifetime giving, average gift, days since last gift) that make a board dashboard useful.

Hand-built Excel files break the moment a column is added, a row is moved, or a new code is introduced.

The APIANT approach

The cheapest dashboard layer most foundations already pay for is HubSpot. Free reports, paid dashboards, and most importantly a permissions model that lets board members read dashboards without seeing anything they should not.

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and HubSpot syncs the financial properties that make board-grade reporting work:

  • 140+ donor properties including Lifetime Giving, Average Gift, Largest Gift, First Gift Date, Last Gift Date, Donor Type, Donor Created Date.
  • Real-time updates so dashboards reflect today’s gifts, not last week’s export.
  • Gifts Pipeline add-on writes each gift as a HubSpot deal so revenue dashboards can roll up by campaign, solicitation, time period, and segment.
  • Custom field sync carries any DonorPerfect custom field (alumni class year, parent of current student, board affiliation) into HubSpot for segmentation.
  • CodeSync for Dropdowns keeps Campaign, Solicitation, and Donor Type dropdowns aligned automatically.

Six dashboards every board chair actually reads

Illustrative scenario: Northbrook Schools Foundation, a K-12 district foundation with 6,500 alumni and family donors (hypothetical example, not a real organization).

Dashboard 1: Revenue versus goal, year to date. A single bar chart of cumulative revenue this fiscal year against last fiscal year and against the board-approved goal. Three lines, one chart, updated daily. This is the only dashboard most board chairs look at first.

Dashboard 2: Revenue by campaign. A table of every campaign this fiscal year, with revenue raised, goal, gifts received, average gift, and percent to goal. Sortable. This is the dashboard the development director uses every Monday morning.

Dashboard 3: Donor segments. A grid of donor counts by segment: New This Year, Retained from Last Year, Recovered (LYBUNT), Lapsed (SYBUNT), Upgraded Sustainers, Downgraded Sustainers. Each segment is calculated from synced Last Gift Date and Total Gifts properties. The board does not need to understand the calculation. They need to see whether the New This Year count is bigger than the Lapsed count.

Dashboard 4: Major gift watchlist. A list of contacts where Lifetime Giving is above the foundation’s major-gift threshold (often $1,000 or $5,000 depending on the org) and Last Gift Date is more than 12 months ago. This is the LYBUNT/SYBUNT list that needs a human, not an email. Assign each contact to a development officer with a follow-up task.

Dashboard 5: Recurring gift health. Active sustainers, average monthly gift, sustainers added this month, sustainers lapsed this month, recovery rate. The sustainer base is usually the most predictable revenue line and the board should see it monthly.

Dashboard 6: Pipeline of pledges and proposals. Deals in the major gift pipeline by stage: Identified, Cultivating, Proposed, Pledged, Paid. Forecast revenue for the next two quarters based on weighted pipeline value.

Sharing without giving HubSpot seats. HubSpot dashboards can be exported as scheduled email PDFs or shared via a read-only link. For boards that want a live view, create a dedicated HubSpot user with view-only permissions on the relevant dashboards. The cost is one seat for the entire board.

Killing the monthly rebuild

The fifth of every month should not feel like a fire drill. With dashboards in place:

  • Board materials are screenshots from existing dashboards plus a one-page narrative.
  • The development director writes the narrative in 90 minutes instead of building the data in three days.
  • Mid-month asks (“can you pull the donors who gave between $500 and $2,500 last year?”) become a five-minute filter, not a half-day pivot table.

Quarterly export back to DonorPerfect. For the annual report and audit, push roll-up summary fields from HubSpot back to DonorPerfect via the bi-directional sync. Annual giving totals, donor segment classifications, and campaign roll-ups should live in both systems so finance and program staff can both see them.

What not to put on the dashboard

Boards drift toward vanity metrics. Email open rates, social followers, website sessions. Resist this. The board’s job is fiduciary oversight, not marketing tactics. Keep the dashboards on cash and donors. If the marketing committee wants engagement data, give them a separate dashboard at the committee level.

Also resist the temptation to put every possible cut on the main dashboard. Six dashboards is the right number for a quarterly board cycle. Adding a seventh and an eighth turns the meeting into a data review instead of a strategy review.

What it looks like in production

For an illustrative foundation the size of Northbrook (6,500 donors, $2.4M annual giving), moving from manual board reporting to synced HubSpot dashboards typically saves 15 to 20 hours of staff time per month. Across a development team of three to five people, that is roughly $25,000 to $40,000 of annual staff capacity returned to actual fundraising work. The qualitative shift matters more: board meetings move from “let me walk you through these numbers” to “let me show you the trend and what we are doing about it.”

Builder takeaway

  • Sync the financial roll-up properties (Lifetime Giving, Average Gift, Days Since Last Gift) into HubSpot as native properties, not just deals. Dashboards perform faster when they aggregate on contact properties than when they sum deal records.
  • Six dashboards is the right number. Adding more dilutes attention. The board will follow your discipline.
  • A read-only HubSpot seat for the board is cheaper than the hours you spend rebuilding the deck. Buy the seat.
