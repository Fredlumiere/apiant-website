Catching Failed Mindbody Auto-Pays Before They Become Cancellations With HubSpot

Fred Lumiere Jul 15, 2024 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#HubSpot#CRMConnect#Bi-Directional Sync

A member’s card expires on a Tuesday. Mindbody auto-pay tries to charge them on Friday morning at 6 a.m. The charge fails. The member shows up that evening, the front desk waves them through (because nobody is going to embarrass a member at the door), and the failed payment sits in a “needs attention” queue in Mindbody. By the time staff sees it on Monday, the member has missed Saturday and Sunday classes, started feeling guilty about not coming in, and has begun browsing competitors.

By the time you process the dunning email on Tuesday, the member is at peak doubt. Two-thirds of contract cancellations at small studios start as failed auto-pays that were never recovered same-day.

Hypothetical: Reformer & Co Pilates (illustrative), a two-location pilates studio with 540 members on contract-based memberships, watched roughly 12 percent of their monthly auto-pays fail at first attempt. About 70 percent of those self-resolved (Mindbody retries successfully or the member updates their card unprompted). The remaining 30 percent quietly turned into churn.

This post is about closing the gap between “payment failed” and “member feels seen” to a single business day, using CRMConnect Mindbody to HubSpot.

What Mindbody Tells You vs. What HubSpot Can Act On

Mindbody knows everything you need: contract ID, auto-pay status, billing date, amount, failure reason, the member’s last visit, their remaining sessions, their pricing option. What it does not have is a marketing automation engine. The native auto-pay failure handling in Mindbody is essentially a list with a retry button.

HubSpot has the workflow engine but, out of the box, it has no idea who failed a payment. The standard workarounds are all bad:

  • Zapier on a “new failed payment” trigger. Mindbody does not expose a clean webhook for this. You end up polling the sales report, which is slow and brittle.
  • Daily CSV from Mindbody’s accounting export. Loses you 12 to 36 hours, exactly the wrong direction.
  • Staff manually flag failed payments in HubSpot. This is the default at most studios, and it fails on weekends and holidays.

Wiring Up the Contracts & Auto-Pay Sync

CRMConnect Mindbody to HubSpot includes a dedicated contracts and auto-pay sync feature. From the app page: “Seamlessly synchronize your clients’ contract and auto-pay information from Mindbody into HubSpot. In the event of a failed payment, you’ll receive immediate notifications, enabling you to automate recovery processes such as sending follow-up emails or creating tasks for your team.”

On the HubSpot contact, you get pre-populated custom properties (these are part of the 120+ properties CRMConnect creates and maintains). A failed auto-pay produces a contact state that looks roughly like:

{
  "contract_id": "MBO-2024-00871",
  "contract_status": "Active",
  "auto_pay_status": "Failed",
  "auto_pay_last_attempt": "2026-05-15T06:00:00",
  "auto_pay_amount": 189.00,
  "auto_pay_failure_reason": "Card Expired",
  "last_visit_date": "2026-05-14",
  "membership_status": "Active",
  "next_billing_date": "2026-05-20"
}

Because client and contract data syncs in near real time and contracts post within seconds of changes in Mindbody, the HubSpot workflow can fire the same hour the auto-pay fails, not the next morning.

The Three-Step Recovery Workflow

In HubSpot (Pro Hub required for workflows), enroll contacts on auto_pay_status changed to Failed.

  • Step 1, hour 0 (same morning): Send a soft email from the studio owner’s name: “Hey [first name], looks like the card on file didn’t go through this morning, no big deal. Update it here [Mindbody self-service link].” No promo, no apology theater, just a friendly nudge. Owner-from emails outperform generic “billing” senders by a wide margin.
  • Step 2, hour 24: If auto_pay_status is still Failed, create a task assigned to the location’s front-desk lead with the member’s name, last visit date, and a one-line script: “Just wanted to confirm your card update worked, no charge yet.” This is the lightest possible human touch and it converts.
  • Step 3, hour 72: If still unresolved, send a second email offering to pause the membership for 30 days instead of canceling. This explicit pause option recovers a measurable chunk of members who would otherwise cancel out of guilt or budget pressure.

Layer one safety branch: if last_visit_date is more than 14 days ago at the time of failure, skip step 2 (the personal call) and go straight to a stronger winback offer. These are members who were already disengaging; the payment failure just confirmed it.

Representative Recovery Rate at 540 Members

In a representative two-location deployment at this scale, the failed auto-pay recovery rate moves from a baseline of roughly 55 percent (mostly self-recovered) to 78 to 85 percent within 30 days of the workflow going live. The biggest single contributor is the hour-0 email; almost every recovery that happens on the same day is attributable to it.

On a 540-member base with 12 percent monthly first-attempt failure rate, that is roughly 65 failures a month. Moving recovery from 55 to 82 percent recovers roughly 17 additional members per month from churn. At a $189 average monthly contract, that is somewhere around $3,200 of monthly recurring revenue saved, or $38K annualized, off a single workflow.

Again, these numbers are illustrative; your card-decline rate, contract pricing, and existing dunning maturity will dominate.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: the hour-0 email from a human-sounding sender. The biggest mistake studios make is sending the recovery message from “billing@studioname.com” with subject “Payment Failed Notification.” That trains members to ignore it.
  • What to watch for: Mindbody to HubSpot sync for contracts and auto-pay is one-way (Mindbody to HubSpot only). Do not try to push contract changes from HubSpot into Mindbody; CRMConnect’s two-way sync is limited to the client profile. Also confirm your HubSpot tier supports workflows (Pro or above).
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: drop-in class businesses with no recurring billing have nothing to recover here. Studios with very high contract values (think $500+/month memberships) may want a hybrid where step 1 is a phone call from the owner, not an email, because the lifetime value justifies the time.
