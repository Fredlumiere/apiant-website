Practitioner and Location Reporting Inside ActiveCampaign for a Women's Health Clinic Using Cliniko Data

Fred Lumiere Jan 3, 2025 5 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#ActiveCampaign#CRMConnect

There comes a point in clinic growth where the monthly practitioner report stops being a reasonable thing to produce by hand. Numbers are stale by the time the report lands in the leadership team’s inbox. Segmenting by discipline takes an afternoon. There is no view of which patients are re-engaging long term versus which are quietly drifting away.

Take an illustrative example: Aurora Women’s Health Collective, a hypothetical 3-location operation with 11 practitioners including pelvic physios, women’s health GPs, and lactation consultants. Leadership currently reports practitioner performance once a month by pasting Cliniko exports into a spreadsheet. The numbers represent last month. Segmentation by discipline is a manual filter. There is no continuous picture of which practitioner’s patients keep coming back six months later.

Why the spreadsheet stops scaling

At 11 practitioners across three locations and several disciplines, the monthly spreadsheet has to answer:

  • Arrived appointments by practitioner, location, and service line.
  • Average invoice value per practitioner.
  • Repeat-visit rate (patients returning within a defined window).
  • Long-term engagement (patients seen 6 months ago who are still active).
  • Group session attendance for antenatal and postnatal classes.

Each of those is a different pivot. Producing the report eats half a day. By the time leadership reviews it, the data is two weeks out of date and the operational moments to act on it are already gone.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

Cliniko’s native reports do the basics well but cannot stay in lockstep with ActiveCampaign automations, segmentation, or marketing reporting. A separate BI dashboard built on a Cliniko export will give leadership a chart but does not feed marketing decisions. The marketer running ActiveCampaign needs the same practitioner and location facts to be visible inside their automation tool, not on a different dashboard they only check on Mondays.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to ActiveCampaign streams patient appointment activity, location visited, last appointment date, and total appointment count onto every contact, applies practitioner and service line tags automatically as appointments complete, and keeps invoice activity in sync. Reports inside ActiveCampaign and marketing automations both read off the same continuously-updated dataset. The monthly spreadsheet ritual disappears.

Concrete steps

  1. Enable the appointment sync so each ActiveCampaign contact carries Location Last Visited, Last Appointment Date, Total Appointment Count, and Appointments in Last 90 Days as custom fields.
  2. Configure Appointment Completion Tagging. When an arrived appointment is logged in Cliniko, the integration applies tags identifying the practitioner, the location, and the appointment type. This is the layer that makes ActiveCampaign reports segmentable by practitioner without manual cleanup.
  3. Treat group antenatal and postnatal classes separately. The integration distinguishes individual from group appointments, so group attendance tags do not contaminate one-to-one practitioner metrics.
  4. Build the practitioner-level dashboard inside ActiveCampaign. For each practitioner tag, surface arrived appointments per month, contacts who are first-time visits in the period, contacts who are repeat visits in the period, and average invoice value (if invoice sync is enabled).
  5. Build the location-level dashboard. For each location tag, surface arrived appointments, new patient acquisition, and the breakdown of new versus returning visits.
  6. Build the long-term engagement report. Segment: contacts whose first appointment was 6 months ago. Sub-segments: still active (an arrived appointment in the last 90 days) versus lapsed. This is the segment the marketer most needs and currently has no view of.

Outcome

A clinic matching Aurora’s profile generally finds three things within the first 60 days. First, the monthly spreadsheet ritual disappears (half a day per month recovered). Second, leadership discovers that one or two practitioners have significantly higher 6-month patient retention than the others, which opens a clinical and operational conversation that the old reporting hid. Third, location-level new patient acquisition becomes visible in real time, which lets the marketing team reallocate spend between locations weekly rather than quarterly.

Group sessions without contaminating one-to-one metrics

Group antenatal classes, postnatal recovery groups, and pelvic health classes are core revenue at a women’s health clinic. They should be measured. But mixing group attendance into per-practitioner one-to-one metrics distorts both. The integration preserves the distinction: a group session creates a group-attendance tag, not a per-attendee individual-appointment tag, so a practitioner’s one-to-one arrived count remains clean while group attendance gets its own reporting line.

The mistake worth avoiding

Once you have tags flowing automatically, there is a temptation to use ActiveCampaign tags as a parallel patient record. Resist it. ActiveCampaign tags are a signaling layer for automation and segmentation. The patient record is in Cliniko. If you find yourself building tags like “patient is currently pregnant” or “patient is in postpartum recovery,” stop. The first should not exist in marketing tooling at all (it is clinical context with consent implications). The second should be derived from Cliniko case status if it needs to drive automation, not maintained by hand in ActiveCampaign.

Builder takeaway

  • Practitioner and location tags applied at completion time are the building block. Build them first, build the reports on top.
  • Keep individual and group appointment metrics in separate streams. Mixing them creates misleading practitioner scorecards.
  • Marketing tags are not a patient record. Cliniko is the patient record. The integration keeps the two in sync without conflating them.
