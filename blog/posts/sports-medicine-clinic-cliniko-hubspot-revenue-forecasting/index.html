Building a Real Revenue Forecast for a Sports Medicine Clinic Using the Cliniko Invoices Pipeline in HubSpot

Fred Lumiere Feb 9, 2026 4 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#HubSpot#CRMConnect#Bi-Directional Sync

There is a moment in every growing clinic when the Monday morning forecast spreadsheet stops being a tool and starts being a liability. The CFO is rebuilding it by hand. Practitioners are out of sync with what it says. Leadership debates the wrong numbers because the right ones are 4 days stale. Decisions get made on vibes.

Take an illustrative example: Northpoint Sports Medicine, a hypothetical single high-volume clinic with 8 practitioners and around $2.4M in annual billings. Billable activity lives in Cliniko invoices. Pipeline, deals, forecasting dashboards live in HubSpot. Every Monday the CFO exports both, joins them in Excel, builds the 30/60/90, and emails it out. By Wednesday the numbers are wrong. By Friday nobody trusts them.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail here

The standard answer in this situation is to bolt accounting software (Xero, QuickBooks) between Cliniko and HubSpot, then build a forecast on top of the accounting layer. That works for tax. It does not work for operational forecasting because the accounting layer is invoice-issued or cash-received, not appointment-booked. By the time revenue lands in QuickBooks, the steering window has closed.

The other common pattern, a custom BI dashboard pulled from Cliniko’s API into Looker or PowerBI, gives leadership a beautiful chart but no operating surface. Practitioners cannot act on a dashboard. HubSpot is where the sales, marketing, and patient lifecycle activity actually happens. Splitting forecasting out of HubSpot just creates two systems of record nobody trusts.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to HubSpot mirrors Cliniko Appointments and Cliniko Invoices as two distinct HubSpot pipelines. The Appointments Pipeline is your leading indicator (booked deals show what revenue is on the calendar). The Invoices Pipeline is your confirmed revenue (invoice deals show what was billed, paid, or written off). Stack them together and you get a real 30/60/90 forecast that updates every time Cliniko changes, not every time the CFO opens a spreadsheet.

Concrete steps

  1. Turn on the Invoices Pipeline. Each Cliniko invoice creates a HubSpot deal with invoice number, issue date, status, net amount, tax amount, total amount, applied discounts, and patient. Deal stages mirror Cliniko invoice statuses (Draft, Pending, Paid, Voided).
  2. Turn on the Appointments Pipeline. Each Cliniko appointment creates a HubSpot deal with appointment date, practitioner, business location, appointment type, and an Appointment Deal Amount derived from the billable item. Stages mirror Cliniko appointment statuses.
  3. Sync Case Information. For every patient case, expose session limits, max invoiceable amount, and active status as properties on the HubSpot contact. This is how you avoid double-counting a patient whose insurer plan is about to cap out.
  4. Build the 30/60/90 forecast. The forecast is the sum of: (a) Invoices Pipeline deals in Paid stage with issue date in the period (recognized), plus (b) Invoices Pipeline deals in Pending stage with issue date in the period (highly likely), plus (c) Appointments Pipeline deals in Booked or Arrived stage with appointment date in the period, times your historical arrival-to-bill rate (forecasted).
  5. Slice by practitioner and service line. The Appointments Pipeline already carries practitioner and appointment type. Build a HubSpot dashboard with forecast by practitioner, forecast by service line (initial assessments, follow-ups, treatment blocks, package visits), and variance to forecast week over week.
  6. Tie marketing spend in. With HubSpot original source already on every contact, you can now produce a true marketing ROI report: ad spend in, invoice revenue out, by campaign or channel.

Outcome

A clinic matching Northpoint’s profile will typically see forecast accuracy improve from the spreadsheet baseline of 75 to 80 percent (week-3 forecast versus actuals) into the low 90s once the Invoices and Appointments pipelines are running. More importantly, the forecast updates intra-week. Leadership goes from arguing about last Monday’s numbers to acting on Thursday morning. The CFO recovers 4 to 6 hours per week previously spent on the manual rebuild. Marketing ROI conversations finally use billed revenue rather than form fills as the denominator.

Recurring-care packages without double counting

Sports medicine sells a lot of multi-visit packages: 6-pack of treatment sessions, 12-week return-to-sport programs, post-surgical rehab blocks. The Case Information sync exposes session limits and remaining capacity for each active case, so the forecast can correctly recognize the package revenue when it was actually billed (typically up front) and avoid forecasting future session revenue that is already paid in advance. Without the Case sync, package patients tend to show up double-counted in the forecast (once at sale, again at every booked session).

Builder takeaway

  • The forecast is the join of two pipelines (Appointments as leading indicator, Invoices as confirmed) plus Case Information as the capacity check. Build all three before you build a dashboard.
  • Spreadsheets are not slow because spreadsheets are bad. They are slow because they need a human to refresh them. Move the refresh into the integration.
  • Marketing ROI conversations get serious only when the denominator is billed revenue, not form fills. The Invoices Pipeline is the line that makes that conversation honest.
