Replacing Three Spreadsheets With Mindbody Indexes and ActiveCampaign Segments

Fred Lumiere Sep 5, 2025 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#ActiveCampaign#CRMConnect#Data Engine

Walk into the back office of a 230-student karate dojo and you will find spreadsheets. One for current belt rank, maintained by the sensei. One for the upcoming belt test roster, maintained by the assistant instructor. One for gear orders (uniforms, sparring pads, weapons), maintained by the front desk. One for tournament signups, maintained by the head coach. One for family memberships, maintained by whoever happens to remember whose siblings are also students.

None of these spreadsheets talk to each other. None of them feed ActiveCampaign. So when the dojo sends a belt-test reminder, it goes to everyone, including white belts whose first test is six months out, and the parents of brown belts whose kid tested last week. Unsubscribes climb. Trust degrades.

Hypothetical: Stone Pine Karate (illustrative), a single-location dojo with 230 students and a high proportion of family memberships, had exactly this problem. The sensei had tried twice to “just put it all in ActiveCampaign” and given up both times because Mindbody’s data and the spreadsheets disagreed in different ways.

This post is about replacing those spreadsheets with Mindbody indexes and custom fields, synced cleanly into ActiveCampaign segments.

What Indexes and Custom Fields Sync to ActiveCampaign

Mindbody supports two underused features for any business with structured client metadata: client indexes (think dropdowns and pick-lists like “Belt Rank” or “Age Group”) and custom client fields (free-form or structured fields like “Sibling Count” or “Tournament Eligible”).

CRMConnect Mindbody to ActiveCampaign syncs both directly. From the app page: “Automatically sync Mindbody indexes and custom fields directly into ActiveCampaign, enabling powerful segmentation and highly personalized campaigns.” And: “Automatically populate ActiveCampaign with over 120 custom fields designed specifically for wellness businesses, enabling deeper segmentation and personalized automations.”

The indexes and custom fields appear in ActiveCampaign as standard custom fields you can use in segment builders and conditional content blocks. A student record might look like:

{
  "email": "parent@example.com",
  "firstname": "Kai",
  "belt_rank": "Green Stripe",
  "age_group": "Youth 8-11",
  "family_membership_id": "FAM-00231",
  "is_paid_for_by": "parent@example.com",
  "tournament_eligible": true,
  "next_belt_test_date": "2026-06-14",
  "gear_size_uniform": "Size 3",
  "last_visit_date": "2026-05-15",
  "membership_status": "Active"
}

That single contact replaces four spreadsheet rows.

Designing Three Segments That Replace Three Spreadsheets

In ActiveCampaign, build three saved segments. Each replaces one spreadsheet.

Segment 1: Belt Test Roster. Filter next_belt_test_date is in next 30 days AND membership_status = Active. This list updates itself the day the sensei changes the test date in Mindbody. No manual roster maintenance.

Segment 2: Gear Reorder Eligible. Filter gear_size_uniform is known AND last_gear_order_date is more than 9 months ago. This finds students whose uniform is probably outgrown without nagging every family every quarter.

Segment 3: Tournament Pool. Filter tournament_eligible = true AND belt_rank in (Green Stripe, Brown, Black) AND age_group in (Youth 12-15, Teen 16-17, Adult). The tournament organizer pulls this list the morning of registration open. The list is always current.

Layer one parent-friendly segment on top: Segment 4: Family Communication Adults. Filter is_paid_for_by is empty (meaning they are the paying adult) AND family_membership_id is known. This is your parent comms list. Belt-test reminders for a 9-year-old should not go to the 9-year-old; they should go to the parent. The family relationship sync makes this trivial.

Automating Belt Tests, Gear, and Tournament Comms

With segments defined, build three small automations.

Belt Test Prep. Enrolled on Segment 1 entry. Five touches over the 30 days before test: a sign-up confirmation, a “what to expect” prep email, a uniform-check reminder, the day-before logistics, and a day-after results note (using a last_belt_test_passed custom field updated by the sensei in Mindbody).

Gear Reorder. Enrolled on Segment 2 entry. Two touches: a sizing-check email at month 9 with the student’s current size on file, and a follow-up if no order is placed within 14 days. Personalize with gear_size_uniform so the parent does not have to look it up.

Tournament Cycle. Enrolled on Segment 3 entry, gated by the upcoming tournament date stored as a campaign-level merge field. Three touches: registration open, registration close warning, day-before logistics. Drop students who register from the campaign automatically (set a tournament_registered field via the registration form, exit on field-changes).

The Mindbody Appointment Pipeline that CRMConnect creates in ActiveCampaign also tracks each student’s training appointments as deals, so the sensei can see attendance trends per student per month without leaving ActiveCampaign.

Representative Time Saved at 230 Students

In a representative deployment at dojos of this scale, the three abandoned spreadsheets recover roughly 4 to 6 hours per week of administrative time across the head sensei and the front desk. That is roughly 250 hours per year. More importantly, the staff stop trusting the spreadsheets and start trusting Mindbody as the source of truth, which means the data quality in Mindbody itself improves (because nobody is keeping a “real” copy on the side).

Email engagement metrics tend to shift sharply. Unsubscribe rate on broadcast emails typically drops by half within 60 days, because the segments mean students stop getting comms that are obviously irrelevant to them (brown belts no longer get white-belt orientation emails).

For Stone Pine, the gear-reorder automation alone covered the integration cost in the first quarter; uniform reorder rate at the 9-month mark moved from a soft 35 percent (driven by parents noticing on their own) to a clean 65 to 70 percent driven by the sizing-aware email.

These numbers are illustrative; dojos with already-rigorous record-keeping see smaller time savings, and dojos with chaotic record-keeping see much larger ones.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: the “one segment per dead spreadsheet” pattern. Do not try to build segments around theoretical user personas; build them around the actual lists your staff are maintaining on paper.
  • What to watch for: indexes and custom fields only sync as cleanly as they are populated in Mindbody. If your existing client base has half its belt_rank fields empty, you need a backfill day before the segments are useful. Also, Mindbody to ActiveCampaign sync for activities is one-way; do not try to update belt rank from ActiveCampaign back to Mindbody (use Mindbody for source-of-truth changes).
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: businesses with very few structured custom fields (think drop-in fitness classes) get less leverage. The pattern shines when there are 3+ orthogonal client attributes that drive different communications.
