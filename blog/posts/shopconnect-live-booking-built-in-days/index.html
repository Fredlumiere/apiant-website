The Feature We Avoided for Years, Built in Days

How ShopConnect learned to sell a booking, and what it taught us about building with an AI operator on an AI-first platform

Fred Lumiere Jul 22, 2026 8 min read

How ShopConnect learned to sell a booking, and what it taught us about building with an AI operator on an AI-first platform.

For as long as we have packaged integrations as turnkey products, one request has come back more than any other. Let a shopper book an appointment or a class at the moment they buy, inside the store, against real availability. We heard it for years. We never built it.

We were honest about why. The idea was small to describe and enormous to deliver. Pull live openings from a scheduling system that might span several locations. Route each booking to the right place. Do all of it inside someone else’s storefront, in their cart, and make it reliable enough to sell. Every time we looked at it, the complexity looked back. So it stayed on the “someday, maybe” list, the polite place where good ideas go to wait.

Then we built it in a few days. Conceived, planned, built, tested, and made ready to deploy as an option, in less time than we used to spend scoping the thing. This is the story of how, and why it could only have happened now.

The years-long wish

Ask anyone who sells services online what they want from their store, and you arrive at the same place. They want the sale and the booking to be one motion. Buy the massage and pick the time. Buy the class pack and grab a seat. One flow, no gap, no follow-up email that half of people ignore. That is the wish. It is obvious. It is also why it sat unbuilt, because obvious and easy are not the same word.

Why we never built it

The hard part was never the button. It was everything behind the button.

Availability has to be real and live, because nothing burns trust faster than offering a time that is already gone. A single business might run more than one location, each with its own schedule, and the store has to know which one a shopper is looking at. The booking has to land in the right place after payment, every time, without a human babysitting it. And all of it has to live gracefully inside a storefront we do not own, next to products that have nothing to do with booking, without touching a checkout that already works.

Any one of those is manageable. Together they form the kind of project that eats a quarter and still feels fragile at the end. We respected that enough to keep saying not yet.

Two worlds, one motion: the workout floor and the storefront, finally joined.

What changed

What changed was not our appetite for the problem. It was how the problem gets solved.

We now build on an AI-first integration architecture, and the difference is not a faster version of the old way. It is a different way. The platform, the connectors, the runtime that carries each request, the machinery for many accounts and many locations, the reusable building blocks, the deploy pipeline, the ability to run a real execution and watch what happens: all of it is designed to be operated, not just used. The operator is Claude Code.

That pairing is the whole story, so we will say it plainly. This is not something an AI coding tool could have produced on its own. An AI writing code in a vacuum would have hit the same wall we did, only faster. What made this possible is an AI operating an ecosystem purpose-built to be operated by AI. Claude Code did not invent booking from first principles. It composed pieces that already existed, wired the flow between them, ran real bookings to see them succeed, corrected what did not, deployed the same solution across accounts, and confirmed the result in a live store. The building blocks were APIANT’s. The judgment and the hands were the AI’s. Neither half could have done this alone.

The build, in days

It did not begin with a spec. It began with a signal, the most human kind. A product simply was not showing a “book now” option where it should have. We followed that thread.

From there it moved fast and iteratively, the way real building actually feels. We taught the storefront to offer a choice when a single purchase could unlock more than one kind of session, so a shopper picks what they want and sees real openings for it. Along the way we found and fixed a subtle flaw deep in the plumbing, the sort of quiet bug that normally derails a project for a week and instead cost us an afternoon. We rolled the same capability across multiple locations from one place, rather than rebuilding it site by site. We even reached back into the source scheduling system to correct a small setup detail so a service would become bookable, then watched it appear and work in the live store minutes later. And we wrote the full operator install guide as we went, because a capability you cannot hand to someone is only half real.

Days, not quarters. The thing we avoided for years was ready to ship in a week.

When the tools did not exist, it built them

Here is the part that still gives us pause. The system did not merely wire together pieces that were already lying around. It hit a wall the platform had never crossed: reading and writing the Shopify storefront theme itself. Most efforts stall right there. This one went and read the relevant Shopify documentation and built the missing capabilities as brand new connector actions, right inside the platform. An action to list the store’s themes. An action to upload the widget’s script to the theme. An action to upload the booking snippet. An action to fetch and rewrite the product template.

None of these were throwaway scripts. Each one became a first-class, reusable building block that any future automation can now pick up and use, no rediscovery required. That is a defining move of AI First: the AI extends the platform’s own vocabulary on demand, and every extension compounds for the next build. A coding tool writes code that lives once. An AI operator on an AI-first platform grows the platform.

Part of the install flow the system assembled. Each circle is a reusable action it built or composed, including a Shopify “List store themes” action, running in order.

Every new action is a real, configurable building block. Here one action’s settings panel, ready to be reused in any future automation.

How it works, at a glance

The magic is in the experience, not the wiring. A central account orchestrates everything and hands each request to the right location behind the scenes. The storefront reads a small amount of structured information about each product to know what is bookable and what is just a normal thing to buy. Availability is pulled live and shown as real, choosable times, or as real classes with real seats. The shopper picks, pays, and the booking is placed automatically. Multiple locations, one storefront, one cart.

The moment that used to happen in a follow-up email now happens in the cart.

Why only an AI operator on an AI-first platform could do this

Step back and notice what actually happened. No single leap here was superhuman. The leap was in the number of different kinds of work that had to happen together, fluently, in a short window. Understanding a real-world symptom. Reasoning about a multi-location architecture. Composing existing connectors instead of writing everything fresh. Running live executions and reading the results. Fixing a deep bug without losing the thread. Deploying across many accounts. Verifying in a real store. Writing the documentation.

That is not coding. That is operating. And it only works when the thing being operated was built to be operated, so that every one of those moves is something the platform already knows how to do and the AI can direct. Take away the AI-first platform and you have a clever assistant staring at a wall. Take away the AI operator and you have a powerful platform waiting for someone with a quarter to spare. Put them together and a five-year “someday” becomes a Tuesday.

What it means for builders

Buy the class pack, pick the time, done. One flow, no gap.

If you build integrations, here is the part worth sitting with. The ceiling on what you can ship just moved, and not by a percentage. The projects you have been quietly avoiding, the ones whose complexity you can feel in your chest before you have written a line, are exactly the projects this pairing is good at. Not because the problems got smaller. Because the distance between “we should” and “it is live” collapsed.

We spent years respecting a problem. We now respect it in a different way. We build it.

Availability

Live availability booking is now part of ShopConnect, our turnkey Mindbody and Shopify integration, and it is available to customers as an option today. A shopper can book an appointment or a class against real openings, across multiple locations, from a single Shopify storefront, and pay before they book. If that is the flow you have been wanting, we would love to switch it on for you.
