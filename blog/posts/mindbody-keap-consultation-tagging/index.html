Auto-Tagging Mindbody Consultations in Keap for Same-Hour Sales Follow-Up

Fred Lumiere Aug 25, 2025 6 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Keap#CRMConnect#Bi-Directional Sync

A prospective patient books a free consultation for Botox at 10:14 a.m. on a Wednesday. She is sitting at her desk, she has the decision freshly in mind, she has read enough reviews to feel committed, and she has tabs open for two other medspas across town.

The single biggest factor in whether she shows up to her consultation, and whether she becomes a patient afterward, is how fast she hears from the medspa after booking. Not how fast the system emails her a calendar invite; that is table stakes. How fast a human reaches out personally to confirm.

The standard medspa workflow looks like this. Patient books in Mindbody. Mindbody sends an automated confirmation. The booking sits in Mindbody until the sales coordinator pulls the daily list at 4 p.m. The coordinator calls everyone. By then, the patient has either talked to one of the competitors, decided not to do it, or forgotten she booked.

Hypothetical: Aurora Skin Lab Medspa (illustrative), a single-location practice with 4 injectors and 1,600 patients, tracked their consultation show-up rate and found it sat around 62 percent. Their booked-to-treated rate (consultation to actual paid procedure) was around 48 percent. The gap between “booked” and “showed up” was the costliest leak; once a patient walked through the door, conversion was strong.

This post is about closing that gap to a single hour using CRMConnect Mindbody to Keap.

What the Consultation Tag Triggers in Keap

CRMConnect Mindbody to Keap ships consultation tagging as a first-class feature. From the app page: “Automatically tag consultation appointments in Keap, helping your team quickly identify new client opportunities and trigger the right nurture sequences at the right time.”

The flow is: a new consultation appointment is booked in Mindbody. CRMConnect detects the appointment type (you map “Consultation” service types to the consultation tag at setup). The contact is created or updated in Keap with the consultation tag applied. Mindbody to Keap sync runs instantly for new bookings. The tag becomes the trigger for everything downstream.

On the Keap contact, the consultation arrives like:

{
  "email": "prospective@example.com",
  "firstname": "Mia",
  "tags": ["mindbody-consultation-booked", "first-time-consult"],
  "consultation_date": "2026-05-21T15:00:00",
  "consultation_provider": "Dr. Hassan",
  "consultation_service": "Injectables Consultation",
  "lead_source": "Website Booking Widget",
  "booked_at": "2026-05-17T10:14:00"
}

The mindbody-consultation-booked tag is the entry condition for the sales cadence. Once it fires, Keap takes over.

Designing the Sales Cadence

In Keap, build one campaign sequence triggered by the mindbody-consultation-booked tag.

Step 1, within 5 minutes: an automated confirmation email from the assigned provider’s name (use the consultation_provider field). Subject: “Looking forward to Wednesday.” Two short paragraphs, no calendar attachment (Mindbody already sent that). This email arrives while the patient still has the booking confirmation tab open. It dramatically reduces the “wait, who was I supposed to see?” cognitive load.

Step 2, within 30 minutes: a task assigned to the sales coordinator with the patient’s name, consultation type, provider, and a one-line script: “Confirming your Wednesday consult with Dr. Hassan. Any questions before then?” The coordinator calls or texts. This is the highest-leverage human touch in the entire funnel.

Step 3, 24 hours before consult: automated reminder with logistics (parking, what to bring, what to expect during a consultation). Use consultation_provider for the from-name to maintain continuity.

Step 4, 2 hours before consult: short text-style reminder. “See you at 3 p.m.” Drives the show-up rate.

Step 5, conditional on no-show: if the appointment status updates to “No Show” (synced from Mindbody), fire a soft “we missed you” message with a frictionless reschedule link.

Layering in Purchased Service Tagging After the Visit

Consultation tagging is the entry. Purchased service tagging is what makes the post-visit follow-up smart.

CRMConnect Mindbody to Keap also includes purchased service tagging: “Automatically manage Keap tags based on client purchases, enabling precise segmentation for upsell campaigns, renewal reminders, and personalized communication.” After the consult, if the patient buys a treatment, the corresponding service tag is applied to the Keap contact within seconds (Mindbody to Keap sync for sales is instant).

So a patient who consulted for injectables and bought a 30-unit Botox treatment now has tags mindbody-consultation-completed, purchased-botox, purchased-botox-30-units. That stack drives the follow-up:

  • A 90-day pre-rebook reminder fires automatically (Botox typically lasts 3 to 4 months).
  • A “complementary treatments” nurture starts (filler, skincare).
  • The consultation-booked sequence exits the patient, so they do not get redundant “did you decide?” emails after they have already purchased.

Mindbody data fields are also pre-created in Keap on day one of the integration: “CRMConnect automatically creates essential Mindbody data fields in Keap during setup, ensuring precise client data tracking from day one, no manual configuration required.” So the cadence works on day one without weeks of field-building.

Representative Booked-to-Treated Conversion at 1,600 Patients

In a representative deployment at medspas of this scale, the show-up rate on consultations typically moves from a baseline of 60 to 65 percent into the 78 to 85 percent range within 60 days of going live with the same-hour cadence. The single biggest contributor is step 2, the human-touch confirmation inside 30 minutes; show-up rate on consults that get a coordinator call within an hour of booking sits in the high 80s consistently across the practices I have seen running this pattern.

Booked-to-treated conversion (consultation to first paid procedure) tends to move from 45 to 50 percent into the 55 to 65 percent range, mostly because the patient walks in already trusting the practice. They have heard from the provider by name and a coordinator personally, all before they parked the car.

At 1,600 patients with roughly 40 to 60 monthly new consultations, lifting booked-to-treated by 12 points adds roughly 6 incremental first treatments per month. At an average first-treatment ticket of $850 (illustrative), that is somewhere around $5,100 of monthly revenue, or $61K annualized off one campaign sequence.

These numbers are illustrative; injector pricing and consultation-to-treatment conversion baselines vary wildly by practice.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: the 5-minute provider-name confirmation email and the 30-minute coordinator task. Both are the highest-leverage moves in the entire sequence. Skip either and the rest of the cadence loses most of its effect.
  • What to watch for: the consultation tag depends on consistent Mindbody service-type naming. If your team names consultations five different ways (“Free Consult,” “Discovery Call,” “Skin Eval,” etc.) you need to map all of them to the same tag at the CRMConnect configuration layer, or normalize the Mindbody service catalog first. Also: appointments flow Mindbody to Keap only; do not try to push appointment changes from Keap to Mindbody.
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: practices with very high consultation volume (200+ per month) cannot scale the human-touch step 2 with a single coordinator; you need either multiple coordinators or to gate the human touch to higher-intent inquiries only. The other steps still work.
