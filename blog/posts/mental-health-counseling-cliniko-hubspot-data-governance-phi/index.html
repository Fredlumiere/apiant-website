What to Sync and What to Leave in Cliniko: a Data Governance Pattern for a Counseling Practice on HubSpot

Fred Lumiere Aug 19, 2024 6 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#HubSpot#CRMConnect

There is a version of this conversation that happens in every mental health practice that has ever considered adding a CRM. Marketing wants to send a quarterly check-in email and a birthday note. The clinical director, correctly, points out that the moment session notes, diagnoses, or treatment plans leave the clinical system, the practice has a much larger problem than a quiet email list. The conversation usually ends with marketing being told “no” and the email list staying quiet.

Take an illustrative example: Wellspring Counseling Associates, a hypothetical telehealth-first practice with 12 licensed therapists and around 5,500 active clients. Marketing wants HubSpot. The clinical director is right to be cautious. The right answer is not “no”, it is “yes, but with field-level control.”

Why “sync everything” is the wrong default

The convenience of a one-click “sync all fields” integration is exactly what makes it dangerous in a counseling context. Once SOAP notes, diagnoses, treatment plans, or session narrative land in a marketing CRM, three bad things happen at once. The marketing team now sits inside the scope of any clinical compliance posture the practice maintains, even if they never open the records. The HubSpot user permission model (which was not designed for protected health information) becomes the practice’s de facto clinical access control. Any third-party HubSpot app or integration the marketing team installs inherits that surface area.

The right pattern is field-level, not record-level. Marketing should see what marketing needs. Clinicians should retain everything else, in the clinical system, where it belongs.

The three buckets

Every data point in Cliniko falls into one of three buckets. Naming them out loud, before configuring anything, is the work that prevents future problems:

  • Marketing safe. Name, email, phone, address, marketing communication preferences, appointment date and time (not type detail), invoice totals (not line items). This is what flows to HubSpot.
  • Operations safe. Appointment status (Booked, Arrived, DNA, Cancelled), business location, practitioner identity, case milestones, session limits, max invoiceable amount. This can flow to HubSpot if the practice has a clear operational use case for each field.
  • Clinician only. Session notes, SOAP notes, treatment goals, diagnoses, assessment scores, narrative case content, any free-text clinical field. This never leaves Cliniko.

The integration owner and the clinical director should agree on which Cliniko field falls into which bucket, in writing, before turning anything on. The list belongs in a shared document the compliance officer can review annually.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to HubSpot is built around field-level configuration rather than all-or-nothing record sync. Patient demographics, appointment status, invoice headers, and contact preferences can sync independently. Case Information sync exposes structural fields (milestones, limits, status) without exposing narrative notes. Nothing in Cliniko’s clinical note layer is part of the sync surface. Marketing preferences and consent are mirrored from Cliniko into HubSpot one-way, so the clinical system remains the source of truth.

Concrete steps

  1. Write the field map first. List every Cliniko field you plan to sync, tag it with its bucket (marketing safe, operations safe, clinician only), and have the clinical director and compliance officer sign off. Clinician only fields are not part of the configuration.
  2. Enable the patient demographic sync for marketing safe fields only. Confirm that no clinical fields appear in the field map.
  3. Enable the Appointments sync for status changes only. The appointment type field, if it reveals clinical specifics (for example, “trauma intake assessment”), should be either suppressed or replaced with a generic operational label before it hits HubSpot.
  4. Enable the Invoices sync for headers only (invoice number, date, total, status). Do not sync line items if line items reveal service type detail.
  5. Mirror consent and marketing preferences from Cliniko one-way into HubSpot. If a client revokes marketing consent in Cliniko, that change must propagate. If marketing somehow toggles a HubSpot preference, that must not push back to Cliniko.
  6. Configure HubSpot user permissions so marketing roles cannot see the operations-safe fields they do not need (appointment status, business location, practitioner identity), and operational roles cannot see marketing campaign content they have no reason to access.

A working example

A marketing-safe automation in this setup: a quarterly “we are here when you need us” email to clients who consented to marketing communication and have no booked appointment in the next 30 days. The automation can see the appointment scheduling fact. It cannot see the clinical reason there is no appointment. It does not need to.

A marketing-unsafe automation, by contrast: a “we noticed you missed your last appointment” email triggered by a Did Not Arrive status. In a counseling context that may be inappropriate to send via marketing infrastructure at all. The clinical team should own that touchpoint directly in Cliniko, with clinical judgment about whether and how to follow up. The integration can surface the signal, but the practice’s policy should decide what becomes an automated email and what stays a clinician-led conversation.

Outcome

A counseling practice matching Wellspring’s profile that adopts this pattern typically gets the marketing benefit they wanted (a 5 to 8 percent lift in client re-engagement at lapse, modest but real) without the compliance exposure they were rightly worried about. The clinical director can describe in one paragraph exactly what crosses the boundary and what does not. The compliance officer has a field map they can review in 20 minutes per quarter rather than an opaque integration they have to audit field by field.

Quarterly governance review

Configure the calendar invite now. Once a quarter, the integration owner, the clinical director, and the compliance officer review:

  • The current field map (any additions since last review).
  • HubSpot user permission changes (any new roles, any role-permission changes).
  • Any new HubSpot apps or integrations the marketing team has installed (these inherit access to the synced data).
  • A sample of synced records to confirm no clinical content has accidentally crossed the boundary.

Twenty minutes once a quarter. The alternative is a much longer conversation with a regulator.

Builder takeaway

  • Build the field map before you turn on the integration. The map, signed by the clinical director, is the document that prevents arguments later.
  • Use field-level sync, not record-level. CRMConnect is designed for this. Treating the integration as all-or-nothing is the configuration choice that creates exposure.
  • Calendar the quarterly governance review now. Twenty minutes per quarter is cheap insurance against an integration that drifts.
