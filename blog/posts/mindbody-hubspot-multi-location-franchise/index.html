Running 9 Mindbody Sites Through One HubSpot Account, Cleanly

Fred Lumiere Feb 16, 2026 6 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#HubSpot#CRMConnect#For Enterprise

Every franchise running on Mindbody hits the same fork in the road around location three or four.

Option A: give corporate one HubSpot account and stuff every site’s clients into it. Marketing gets centralized reporting. Franchisees lose access to their own data, and they revolt when corporate “marketing assistance” emails their members offers for a service that location does not even sell.

Option B: give every franchisee their own HubSpot portal. Franchisees are happy. Corporate has nothing. Cross-location reporting becomes nine CSV exports stitched together in Google Sheets every Monday.

Both options are wrong. The right setup is one HubSpot account, with strict per-location data partitioning, and reports that respect both views.

Hypothetical: Iron Lantern Fitness (illustrative), a 9-location boutique gym franchise across 3 regions with 6,400 total members, was running Option A and three franchisees were openly threatening to leave the network over it. This post is how they got to a sane Option C using CRMConnect Mindbody to HubSpot.

Partitioning Contacts and Deals by Business Unit

CRMConnect ships with explicit support for HubSpot Business Units and user-level access partitioning. From the app page: “Easily control which HubSpot users or business units can access specific contacts and deals. CRMConnect ensures your client data is visible only to designated teams or business units, ideal for franchise networks and multi-location operations requiring precise user permissions and data management.”

Practically, you set up one HubSpot Business Unit per region (or per franchisee, depending on how you want to slice). Each Mindbody site is mapped to the appropriate Business Unit at the CRMConnect configuration layer. Every synced contact, deal, and engagement is tagged accordingly.

A franchisee logs into HubSpot and sees only their site’s contacts. Corporate logs in with admin access and sees the rollup. The friction at the user-permissions layer disappears.

Business Units in HubSpot are a Marketing Hub Enterprise feature. If you are not at Enterprise, you can approximate this with HubSpot Teams plus access control on properties, but Business Units is cleaner and the right pattern for franchise networks above 5 locations.

Location Tagging as the Backbone of Cross-Site Reporting

Business Units alone do not solve the cross-location reporting problem; they actually make it slightly harder, because corporate now needs to ask the right questions across partitions.

CRMConnect’s location tagging feature is the answer: “Automatically tag clients in HubSpot based on the specific Mindbody locations where they book, visit, or purchase. CRMConnect keeps each client’s record consistently updated across all your sites, providing a unified view of client activity. This ensures clear segmentation, personalized communications, and valuable insights for multi-location or regional operations.”

Each contact gets a locations_tagged multi-select property listing every site they have ever interacted with, plus a primary_location single-select for the one they use most. So a member who joined at Iron Lantern Downtown and started visiting Iron Lantern Eastside three months later has both tags, and corporate can ask “how many of our members are cross-using two locations?” with a single HubSpot filter.

{
  "email": "athlete@example.com",
  "firstname": "Devi",
  "primary_location": "Iron Lantern Downtown",
  "locations_tagged": ["Iron Lantern Downtown", "Iron Lantern Eastside"],
  "business_unit_id": 12012,
  "first_visit_date": "2025-09-12",
  "last_visit_location": "Iron Lantern Eastside",
  "last_visit_date": "2026-05-10",
  "membership_status": "Active"
}

This is one of those features that sounds boring on paper and becomes the single most-used filter in the entire account once franchisees see it.

Cross-Location Reporting Corporate Will Actually Use

The corporate marketing dashboard at Iron Lantern ended up with five reports. None of them required custom code.

Report 1: Member overlap matrix. Cross-tab primary_location against locations_tagged. Surfaces cross-location usage by region. Used to plan staff scheduling for popular cross-traffic times.

Report 2: Per-location lapsed members. Same logic as the single-location version, but grouped by primary_location first. Lets each franchisee see their own lapsed list inside the shared portal without seeing other franchisees’ members.

Report 3: Regional revenue by service category. Mindbody Sales Pipeline grouped by location and service type. Surfaces “Eastside is killing it on intro packs, Downtown is killing it on memberships” patterns that drive what corporate promotes per region.

Report 4: Membership status by location. Grouped count of membership_status per primary_location. Cleanest churn early-warning indicator at the network level.

Report 5: Cross-regional report card. CRMConnect’s app page calls this out explicitly: “Effortlessly run comprehensive reports across all your Mindbody locations directly in HubSpot. Ideal for data-share and cross-regional businesses, this capability helps you accurately measure key performance indicators, compare location performance, and uncover insights to drive network-wide growth and efficiency.”

The bi-directional client sync also matters at franchise scale. When a member updates their email at one location, the “Push to Mindbody” custom property pattern lets you propagate the change to every site they appear at, rather than holding stale data in 8 of 9 locations.

Representative Rollup at 6,400 Members

In a representative deployment at this scale (9 locations, ~6,000 members), the first month typically surfaces 8 to 14 percent of members touching more than one location. Corporate marketing usually has no idea this number is that high; they assume cross-traffic is a rounding error. It is not, and once it is visible, it changes how regional promotions are designed (you stop sending Downtown-only offers to Eastside-tagged members and vice versa).

Franchisee satisfaction is the harder metric to quantify, but the qualitative shift is consistent: complaints about “corporate marketing emailing my members the wrong stuff” drop to roughly zero within 60 days, because the Business Unit partitioning prevents the wrong-list problem at the source.

Iron Lantern’s representative cross-traffic was 11 percent, or roughly 700 members. The first cross-location nurture they built (a “try our second location” sequence) converted about 12 percent of those to actual visits, adding roughly 80 incremental monthly visits across the network with zero new ad spend.

These numbers are illustrative; cross-traffic rates depend heavily on geographic density.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: the Business Unit + location-tag combination. Do not try to use just one. Business Units control access; location tags drive reporting. Both layers are needed.
  • What to watch for: Business Units require Marketing Hub Enterprise. If you cannot get there, fall back to HubSpot Teams plus property-level permissions; it works but it is more setup. Also, sync sales and visits centrally to the rollup pipelines, not per-location; otherwise corporate cannot do network reports.
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: franchises with completely independent ownership (true license agreements with no data-share clause) often cannot legally centralize. In that case, each franchisee runs their own CRMConnect tenant against their own HubSpot, and corporate gets nothing. Solve the contract before you solve the integration.
