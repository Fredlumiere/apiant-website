Automated Keap Custom Fields From Mindbody: Cutting Setup From Days to Minutes

Fred Lumiere Feb 10, 2025 6 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Keap#CRMConnect#Data Engine

Ask any personal trainer who switched CRMs in the last two years how the first month went. They will tell you about the spreadsheets, the imports, and the slow, soul-crushing work of recreating every Mindbody custom field by hand inside Keap.

Each trainer at a high-touch personal training studio tracks 8 to 15 custom data points per client: goals, body composition, injury history, training preferences, dietary considerations, milestone dates, equipment preferences at home, schedule constraints. In Mindbody, these live as client indexes or custom fields. In Keap, by default, none of them exist. You have to create each field one at a time, decide on its type, write a label, and then write the mapping logic that populates it from Mindbody.

A diligent trainer can build maybe 4 custom fields per hour, between the field creation, the Mindbody mapping, and the test imports. Multiply by 15 fields, multiply by however many trainers want different views, and the setup work alone runs into the multi-day range. Most studios give up halfway through and end up with a half-populated Keap that nobody trusts.

Hypothetical: Granite Bay Personal Training (illustrative), a single-location studio with 5 trainers and 140 high-touch clients, tried this manually twice. Both attempts collapsed at the field-creation step. The trainers literally spent their lunch breaks for two weeks creating Keap custom fields, hated it, and reverted to using Mindbody for everything plus a side spreadsheet for goal tracking.

What Fields CRMConnect Creates on Day One

CRMConnect Mindbody to Keap ships with automated custom field creation. From the app page: “CRMConnect automatically creates essential Mindbody data fields in Keap during setup, ensuring precise client data tracking from day one, no manual configuration required.”

On day one, the integration creates the wellness-business essentials in Keap:

  • Profile fields: mindbody_client_id, pricing_option_name, membership_status, total_visits, last_visit_date, last_visit_service, last_visit_staff, next_scheduled_appointment
  • Membership and contract fields: membership_expiration_date, remaining_sessions, contract_id, auto_pay_status
  • Purchase and intro fields: intro_offer_purchased, intro_offer_purchase_date, last_purchase_item, last_purchase_amount, last_purchase_date
  • Engagement fields: first_visit_date, total_visits, lifetime_revenue

Beyond the standard set, CRMConnect maps Mindbody client indexes and custom client fields into matching Keap custom fields automatically. So a personal training studio that has been using a Mindbody custom field for client_goals (a structured set: weight loss, mobility, strength, sport-specific, post-injury) gets that exact field in Keap without anyone typing the values into a Keap dropdown manually.

How Sales Data Sync Keeps Fields Useful Over Time

Day one is the easy part. The hard part is the next 12 months, when clients buy new packs, hit milestones, change goals, and update their preferences. A static field is useless; a continuously updated field drives automations.

CRMConnect’s sales data sync is instant. Profiles, appointments, and sales sync within minutes (Mindbody to Keap is instant; Keap to Mindbody runs every 15 minutes). Purchased service tagging maintains the relevant Keap tags as purchases happen: “Automatically manage Keap tags based on client purchases, enabling precise segmentation for upsell campaigns, renewal reminders, and personalized communication.”

So a client who buys a new 12-pack triggers automatic updates to pricing_option_name, remaining_sessions, last_purchase_item, last_purchase_amount, and last_purchase_date, plus the appropriate purchase tag (purchased-12pack-pt-2026q2 or similar). Any Keap automation built on those fields sees the change within seconds. Renewal campaigns fire. Milestone congratulations fire. Trainer task notifications fire.

The custom fields stay useful because they stay current, not because anyone is updating them by hand.

Building Trainer-Specific Views Without Custom Code

With the fields in place, each trainer can build their own Keap saved-search views without any custom-code work.

Sam’s view: filter to primary_trainer = Sam AND membership_status = Active. Sort by next_scheduled_appointment. This is Sam’s daily working list. New clients appear automatically. Old clients drop off when their membership lapses.

Pack-renewal view: filter to remaining_sessions <= 3 AND membership_status = Active, grouped by primary_trainer. Each trainer sees their own renewal pipeline.

Milestone view: filter to total_visits >= 100 AND milestone_100_celebrated is empty. Drives automatic milestone congratulations and gift-card sends (the studio’s retention ritual).

Injury watch view: filter to injury_notes is known AND last_visit_date in last 7 days. Lets the head trainer scan for clients with active injuries who recently trained, for follow-up form-check conversations.

None of this requires custom code. None of it requires the trainers to maintain spreadsheets. The fields populate themselves; the views update themselves.

Representative Setup Time Saved at 140 Clients

In a representative deployment at studios of this scale, the manual setup work that a do-it-yourself approach typically requires (4 to 6 hours per trainer multiplied by 5 trainers, plus 8 to 12 hours of admin field-creation work) collapses to roughly an hour of configuration with CRMConnect’s pre-built field set. That is roughly 30 to 40 hours of trainer and admin time recovered immediately at setup.

Beyond the day-one setup, the recurring time savings come from not having to retroactively add new fields when the studio adopts a new program. A new “Mobility Mondays” workshop launches in Mindbody; the workshop attendance flows into Keap automatically as a purchase tag, without anyone touching Keap’s field manager.

For Granite Bay, the qualitative win was the trainers actually using Keap for the first time. After two failed manual setups, the third attempt with automated fields stuck because the trainers did not have to do any of the setup work themselves. The clients they had been tracking on side spreadsheets came across with their goals and injury notes intact, because Mindbody had those fields populated and CRMConnect mapped them automatically.

These numbers are illustrative; setup time savings depend heavily on how many Mindbody custom fields you have populated and how disciplined your existing field naming is.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: lean on the automated field creation rather than trying to be clever. The temptation is to design “better” Keap fields than the ones Mindbody has. Resist; let the integration’s pre-built set carry you for the first 60 days, then add fields only where you have a concrete automation that needs them.
  • What to watch for: the automated field creation handles standard wellness data points; truly custom Mindbody indexes still need to be mapped at the CRMConnect configuration step. Also, Mindbody to Keap sync for appointments and sales is one-way; only client profiles sync two-way. Do not try to drive Mindbody field changes from Keap.
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: very small studios with minimal client data (think drop-in only, no goals tracking) get less out of the rich field set; the standard subset is plenty. Conversely, studios with extremely bespoke Mindbody data models may need consulting time to map their non-standard indexes properly.
