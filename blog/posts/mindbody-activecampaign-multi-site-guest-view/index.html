Unifying 3 Mindbody Retreat Sites Into One ActiveCampaign Guest View

Fred Lumiere Dec 25, 2025 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#ActiveCampaign#CRMConnect#For Enterprise

Three Mindbody sites in three countries. A repeat guest who booked the lodge in Vermont last summer just booked the lodge in Costa Rica for January. Marketing has no idea. Each Mindbody site exports its own CSV in its own format. The Vermont site uses one set of custom fields, the British Columbia site uses another, and the Costa Rica site uses a third that includes a “Visa Requirements” column nobody else has.

When the marketing director tries to merge these into ActiveCampaign manually, she gets three contact records for the same person. Three separate “welcome series” fire. The guest gets confused and asks customer support why she is being treated as a new lead at a retreat she has been to four times.

Hypothetical: Cedar Hollow Wellness Retreat (illustrative), with 3 retreat sites across 2 countries and seasonal cohorts of 40 to 60 guests, ran this scenario for three years. The fix is not a better spreadsheet; it is a single source of guest identity across all three sites.

How Two-Way Sync Handles the Same Guest Across Sites

CRMConnect Mindbody to ActiveCampaign uses two-way client profile sync that handles the multi-site case explicitly. From the app page: “Automatically keep Mindbody client profiles and ActiveCampaign contacts in perfect step, and push any tagged lead from ActiveCampaign straight into the right Mindbody site.”

The mechanics matter here. ActiveCampaign holds one contact per email address (its standard model). CRMConnect maps multiple Mindbody client IDs (one per site) onto that single ActiveCampaign contact, attached as a multi-value field. So a repeat guest who has visited two sites has one ActiveCampaign contact with two Mindbody client IDs and a combined visit/sales history.

A repeat-guest record looks like:

{
  "email": "guest@example.com",
  "firstname": "Sara",
  "mindbody_client_ids": [
    {"site": "Vermont", "id": "VT-2024-00112"},
    {"site": "Costa Rica", "id": "CR-2025-00089"}
  ],
  "primary_site": "Vermont",
  "sites_visited": ["Vermont", "Costa Rica"],
  "total_visits_all_sites": 5,
  "last_visit_date": "2026-01-22",
  "last_visit_site": "Costa Rica",
  "last_visit_service": "7-Night Restorative Retreat",
  "lifetime_revenue": 14750.00
}

When marketing sends a “thank you for your most recent retreat” email, it goes to the right person with the right context, once.

The matching logic is documented in CRMConnect’s matching rules: if first name, last name, and email match across sites, the same ActiveCampaign contact is updated. CRMConnect’s unified data processing engine reads each site’s Mindbody client object and reconciles them into the single ActiveCampaign contact regardless of which custom fields each site decided to populate, because the field mappings are configurable per site rather than hardcoded.

Building One Repeat-Guest Segment for All 3 Locations

In ActiveCampaign, create one segment called Repeat Guests, Network-Wide.

Filter: sites_visited contains more than one site OR total_visits_all_sites > 1.

That single segment is the foundation for every cross-site campaign Cedar Hollow runs. Specifically:

  • Anniversary nurture: built off first_visit_date regardless of site.
  • Cross-site upsell: “You loved Vermont in fall, you’ll love Costa Rica in winter.” Drives roughly 8 to 14 percent of all cross-bookings.
  • Loyalty tier: thresholds on lifetime_revenue and total_visits_all_sites. Guests who hit Tier 3 get a hand-written note from the retreat director, automated via task in ActiveCampaign.

The retreat-by-retreat segments still exist for site-specific operational comms (logistics, weather, packing lists), but everything strategic is built off the network-wide segment.

Tying Membership and Contracts Into Retreat Renewals

For retreats with annual passes or multi-retreat packages, CRMConnect’s membership and contract sync extends the same logic. From the app page: “Automatically track each client’s membership status, expiration dates, and remaining sessions to effortlessly manage renewals and upsells.” And: “Continuously sync client contract and auto-pay statuses, proactively alerting your team about upcoming renewals or payment issues.”

A guest on a Cedar Hollow annual pass might have visit history at all three sites and a contract attached to the Vermont site as the originating purchase. The ActiveCampaign contact carries the full picture: pass type, expiration date, sites covered, remaining retreat credits. Renewal automation fires once, 45 days before expiry, with copy that references the guest’s actual cross-site usage rather than a generic “renew now” pitch.

The sales and visit syncs follow predictable cadences: profiles instantly, bookings/visits/sales within seconds, the Mindbody Sales Pipeline reconciled daily, the appointment pipeline every 15 minutes. For retreats that book months in advance, real-time is overkill anyway; the daily reconciliation is sufficient for operational reporting.

Representative Repeat-Booking Lift Across Sites

In a representative deployment at retreat networks of this scale, the first season of clean cross-site segmentation typically lifts repeat-booking rate (defined as any guest with 2+ retreat bookings inside 18 months) from a baseline of 18 to 25 percent into the 32 to 40 percent range. The biggest contributor is the cross-site upsell campaign, which converts because it leverages context that did not exist in any of the three Mindbody sites individually.

For Cedar Hollow’s seasonal cohort math, lifting repeat-booking rate by 12 points across roughly 240 unique annual guests yields somewhere around 28 additional retreat bookings per year. At an average retreat price of $2,800 (illustrative), that is roughly $78K of incremental annual revenue against essentially fixed retreat overhead.

The qualitative win is bigger: guests stop being surprised to be “remembered” when they arrive at a site they have never visited but a sister site has. That changes how they refer friends.

These numbers are illustrative; cross-site dynamics depend heavily on geographic proximity and brand coherence across sites.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: the network-wide repeat-guest segment as the single source for strategic campaigns. Per-site segments exist only for operational comms.
  • What to watch for: the multi-site sync depends on consistent email-based identity matching. If guests historically booked one site under their work email and another under their personal email, CRMConnect cannot magically link them; you may need a one-time manual reconciliation pass before going live. Also, two-way sync is limited to the client profile; visits, sales, memberships, and contracts flow Mindbody to ActiveCampaign only.
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: networks where the sites are deliberately positioned as different brands (different names, different marketing) do not want a unified guest view by default; they want intentional brand separation. In that case, run separate ActiveCampaign accounts per site and skip this pattern entirely.
