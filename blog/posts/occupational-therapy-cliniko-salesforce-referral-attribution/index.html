Referral Channel Attribution for an Occupational Therapy Network With Cliniko + Salesforce

Fred Lumiere Oct 7, 2025 4 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#Salesforce#CRMConnect#Bi-Directional Sync

When referrals come in through Salesforce but conversion happens in Cliniko, leadership cannot see which channels actually fund the business. The intake coordinator sees referrals. The clinical schedulers see appointments. Nobody sees the link, and so nobody can answer the question that decides next year’s partnership investments: which referral channels actually produce completed care plans.

Take an illustrative example: Meridian Occupational Therapy Group, a hypothetical regional provider with 6 clinics, 28 OTs, and a referral base that mixes insurers, GPs, school districts, and self-referrals. The intake team works in Salesforce. Scheduling and clinical outcomes live in Cliniko. Leadership cannot tell which referral channels convert to completed initial assessments, let alone completed care plans. Channel investment decisions get made on gut feel.

The intake bottleneck

A typical intake flow at a 6-clinic network looks like this: referral arrives via fax, portal, or phone; intake coordinator creates a Salesforce lead with referral source attribution; lead is assigned to a clinic; patient is created in Cliniko at the assigned clinic; appointments get scheduled in Cliniko; treatment happens in Cliniko. The Salesforce lead and the Cliniko patient are two separate records with no live connection. Whether the referral ever resulted in a completed initial assessment, let alone a completed care plan, is a question that has to be reconstructed manually from a Cliniko export.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

A one-way push from Salesforce to Cliniko (create the patient) and a quarterly CSV pull back (here is what happened) gives you stale, lossy attribution. By the time the CSV is reconciled, the operational moment to retrain the intake team or adjust a referral partnership has passed. The bigger problem is that referral source attribution gets lost in translation: the Salesforce lead’s source field rarely makes it onto the Cliniko patient as a usable, reportable field. So even with a manual export the report cannot answer “completed care plans by referral channel.”

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to Salesforce provides bi-directional patient and contact sync, so the Salesforce contact created at intake is the same record that gets enriched with live Cliniko activity throughout the patient journey. Cliniko Appointments stream into Salesforce as custom objects with status (Booked, Arrived, DNA, Cancelled). Cliniko Case Information (referral source, referring practitioner, milestones, session limits) is exposed as Salesforce objects too. Salesforce reports can now group by referral channel and count what actually matters: arrived initial assessments and completed care plans.

Concrete steps

  1. Configure bi-directional patient sync. The Salesforce contact created at intake propagates to Cliniko as a patient. Any Cliniko-side updates (corrected phone number, updated address, merged duplicate) propagate back to the Salesforce contact.
  2. Enable Cliniko Appointments as Salesforce custom objects. Each appointment is a Salesforce record stamped with the patient, practitioner, clinic, appointment type, and status. Status changes in Cliniko propagate within minutes.
  3. Enable Cliniko Case Information as Salesforce objects. The referral source field, referring practitioner, milestones, session limits, and case status all become reportable Salesforce dimensions.
  4. Build the channel attribution report. Group by referral source. Columns: leads received, contacts with a Booked appointment, contacts with an Arrived initial assessment, contacts with a completed care plan (case marked closed at milestone completion), total billed.
  5. Layer in cost. If marketing or partnership spend is tracked per channel in Salesforce, the report now gives you cost per arrived initial assessment and cost per completed care plan, not just cost per referral.
  6. Configure multi-clinic dispatch. The integration carries the assigned clinic (Cliniko business location) as a field on every appointment. Use Salesforce assignment rules to route a new referral to the right of 6 clinics based on geography, specialty, or insurer plan, without losing the referral source attribution.

Outcome

A network matching Meridian’s profile will typically discover within the first quarter that one or two referral channels they had assumed were strong are actually middling on completed care plans (lots of referrals, low conversion to arrived assessment), while a smaller channel is producing the highest-value patient cohort. That is a partnership-investment conversation the leadership team could not have before. The intake coordinator stops rebuilding the report by hand. The regional director gets a single Salesforce dashboard with completed care plans, billed revenue, and cost per channel, refreshed in real time.

Multi-clinic dispatch without losing attribution

The mistake to avoid here: routing a referral to a different clinic by manually creating a new lead at the destination clinic. That breaks the original referral source attribution. The right pattern is to keep the original Salesforce contact and update the assigned clinic field. The integration creates or updates the Cliniko patient at the correct clinic without losing the lineage.

Builder takeaway

  • Bi-directional patient sync is the foundation. Without it, attribution breaks the first time a record is updated on either side.
  • Appointments and Case Information as Salesforce custom objects unlock the reports you actually need. Without them, you have leads in Salesforce and outcomes in Cliniko and nothing in between.
  • Channel attribution is not “leads received.” It is “completed care plans by channel, cost-adjusted.” Build that report first. Everything else is supporting evidence.
