Cutting Chiropractic No-Shows With HubSpot Workflows Built on Live Cliniko Appointment Data

Fred Lumiere Feb 17, 2025 4 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#HubSpot#CRMConnect#Bi-Directional Sync

A 14 percent Did Not Arrive rate is not a front-desk discipline problem. It is a data plumbing problem. Treat it as a discipline problem and you will buy more reminder software, run more huddles about it, and stay at 14 percent forever.

Consider an illustrative example: Cedar Park Chiropractic, a hypothetical 2-location, 6-chiropractor practice. Their front desk sends manual reminder texts when they remember. Recall calls to lapsed patients happen only when the schedule is quiet, which is exactly the wrong time, because the schedule is quiet because patients are not rebooking. The DNA rate sits at 14 percent and stays there.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

Staffed reminder calls are the most expensive intervention a clinic can run. A receptionist costs $20 to $30 fully loaded per hour and can call maybe 25 patients in that hour, half of whom go to voicemail. That is north of $2 per attempted contact for a task a workflow can do for less than a cent.

Standalone SMS reminder tools usually work on a flat schedule (24 hours before, 2 hours before) and have no idea what type of appointment is coming up, who the practitioner is, or whether the patient has missed before. They cannot send a different message to a new-patient initial consult versus a 4th adjustment in a care plan. They certainly cannot trigger a same-day recall the moment Cliniko flips an appointment to Did Not Arrive.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to HubSpot syncs next-appointment metadata, appointment counts, and appointment status onto every HubSpot contact in near real time. Cliniko Appointments also flow through as deals on a dedicated Appointments Pipeline, so HubSpot workflows can branch on the stage (Booked, Arrived, Did Not Arrive, Cancelled) the moment Cliniko changes it. That gives you a workflow surface that always has the truth, never lags by a day, and never has to be rebuilt because somebody updated a record in the wrong place.

Concrete steps

  1. Confirm Next Appointment Date, Next Appointment Time, Next Appointment Practitioner, and Next Appointment Type are syncing as HubSpot contact properties. These are the four fields every downstream workflow keys off.
  2. Build the pre-appointment reminder workflow. Enrollment trigger: Next Appointment Date is known. Branches: 48 hours before, send a personalized SMS that names the practitioner and appointment type. 24 hours before, send a confirmation email with a one-click confirm link. 2 hours before, send a final SMS.
  3. Build the no-show recovery workflow. Enrollment trigger: a deal on the Cliniko Appointments Pipeline moves to the Did Not Arrive stage. Action: create a task for the front desk with a 4-hour SLA, send a soft-touch SMS to the patient (“we noticed you missed today, want us to reschedule?”), and start a 3-message email sequence over the next 7 days.
  4. Build the lapsed-patient nudge. Enrollment trigger: contact has Last Appointment Date older than 45 days AND no Next Appointment Date set. Send a 2-touch sequence (an email at day 45, a softer email at day 60) inviting them to rebook, with a link straight to your Cliniko online booking page.
  5. Suppress reminders for clinical edge cases. Patients flagged in Cliniko with a hold or with sensitive case status should not enter marketing-style workflows. Use the Cliniko patient status field, mirrored into HubSpot, as an exclusion criterion on every workflow.

Outcome

In an illustrative deployment matching Cedar Park’s profile, a clinic typically pulls the DNA rate from 14 percent down to 7 to 9 percent in the first quarter just from the reminder workflow. The lapsed-patient nudge usually recovers 12 to 18 percent of contacts that had gone cold past 45 days, which at a $90 average visit and 200 lapsed patients a month is real money. Front desk time spent on reminder texts and recall calls drops by 6 to 10 hours per location per week. None of that requires a new headcount, just a workflow that always has live appointment data to work with.

What to NOT automate

The reminder and recall sequences described above are administrative. They are not clinical. Anything that touches the clinical relationship belongs to the practitioner, not to a HubSpot workflow:

  • Post-incident follow-ups (a patient who came in for an acute episode) should be handled by the treating practitioner directly. Do not enroll them in a generic post-visit drip.
  • Sensitive case content (specifics of the complaint, the diagnosis, the treatment plan) should never appear in a marketing email. Keep this in Cliniko, full stop.
  • Patients in protected categories (workers comp, motor vehicle accident cases, minors) should be excluded from marketing automation by default, with explicit opt-in if marketing communication is ever appropriate.

Builder takeaway

  • Stop treating no-shows as a discipline problem. Treat them as a workflow problem with a clear data dependency on Cliniko appointment status.
  • The four properties that earn their keep on every contact are Next Appointment Date, Time, Practitioner, and Type. Get those syncing first. Everything else is a downstream branch.
  • Draw a hard line between admin automation and clinical communication. The integration makes both visible. Only one belongs in HubSpot.
