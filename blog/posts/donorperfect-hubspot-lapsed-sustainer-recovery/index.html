Catching lapsed sustainers in 24 hours: a DonorPerfect to HubSpot recovery workflow for animal rescues

Fred Lumiere Aug 5, 2024 6 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#HubSpot#CRMConnect#Bi-Directional Sync

Recurring gifts are the most valuable line on a nonprofit’s revenue statement and the most quietly fragile. A monthly sustainer at $25 produces $300 a year. Lose them and you do not just lose this month’s charge. You lose the predictable cash flow that pays staff salaries between appeals.

The most common cause of sustainer loss is not donor dissatisfaction. It is an expired credit card that nobody noticed. A processor declines the recurring charge. DonorPerfect logs the failure. The marketing director, working in HubSpot, does not see it. Sixty days later somebody runs a sustainer report, spots a gap, and emails the donor to ask if everything is okay. By then the donor has moved on, the saved card is canceled, and the relationship is colder than it needed to be.

Sustainer recovery is one of those problems that looks easy on a whiteboard and is hard in production.

The native DonorPerfect Failed Transactions report lives in DonorPerfect. Whoever runs it has to remember to run it, has to know what to do next, and has to live in two systems. In most shops the development director runs it weekly. Weekly is too late.

Generic iPaaS connectors typically sync new gifts but ignore the absence of an expected gift. They have no concept of “this donor had a recurring schedule, and the expected charge did not happen.” You can fake it by looking at Last Gift Date in HubSpot and filtering for sustainers whose last gift was more than 35 days ago, but only if Last Gift Date is actually syncing in real time and only if the recurring-status field is also flowing through.

DIY Zapier builds get partway there and break the first time DonorPerfect adds a new field or a Pledge code changes name.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect’s bi-directional sync was built to carry the full DonorPerfect donor record, not a slice of it. That includes the fields that make sustainer recovery actually work:

  • Last Gift Date and First Gift Date mirror in real time. The moment DonorPerfect records a successful charge, HubSpot sees it.
  • Recurring gift status, Next Scheduled Gift Date, and Pledge Balance flow through as standard contact properties.
  • Flags Sync as Tags mirrors DonorPerfect Flags as HubSpot multi-select tags. Use this to mark Active Sustainer, Card Update Sent, Recovery In Progress, or anything else your team already tracks.
  • CodeSync for Dropdowns keeps DonorPerfect Pledge Type and Frequency codes consistent with HubSpot dropdown values so segmentation does not drift.
  • Custom field writeback lets HubSpot update a DonorPerfect custom field (for example, Recovery Sequence Status) so DonorPerfect users see what HubSpot has done.

The combination means HubSpot can run an active list of sustainers who should have given by now and have not, and trigger the recovery workflow within hours instead of months.

Wiring up same-day sustainer recovery

Illustrative scenario: Cedar Hollow Animal Rescue, a no-kill shelter with three chapters and 4,800 lifetime donors (hypothetical example, not a real organization).

Step 1. Define your sustainer list. In HubSpot, build an active list with these criteria: Recurring Gift Status is Active AND Last Gift Date is more than 35 days ago AND Pledge Balance is greater than 0. The 35-day window catches both monthly and quarterly schedules with a small grace period. Adjust if you have a meaningful number of weekly or bi-weekly sustainers.

Step 2. Add suppression rules. Exclude contacts tagged Card Update Sent in last 7 days to avoid spamming donors who are already in motion. Exclude contacts flagged Deceased, Major Donor (those need a personal call, not an automated sequence), and Do Not Solicit from the DonorPerfect side.

Step 3. Build the recovery sequence. A workflow that respects the donor and recovers most fixable failures looks like this:

  • Day 0 (within 24 hours of detection): SMS or email titled along the lines of “We could not process this month’s gift,” with a one-click update-card link that lands on the DonorPerfect-hosted donation form, pre-filled with the donor’s recurring schedule.
  • Day 3: a softer follow-up email focused on the work, not the failure. A story about a recent foster placement, then a quiet note: “We had trouble processing your monthly gift. Update your card here when you have a minute.”
  • Day 7: a phone call task assigned to the donor relations coordinator, with the contact’s giving history, last conversation note, and recurring schedule pre-loaded in HubSpot from the DonorPerfect sync.
  • Day 14: a final email from the executive director or board chair, framed as a check-in: “We have not heard from you and want to make sure everything is okay.”
  • Day 21: if no successful charge has been recorded, write a Sustainer Lapsed flag back to DonorPerfect via custom field sync and remove the contact from the recovery list.

Step 4. Close the loop. When DonorPerfect records a new successful recurring charge for a contact in the recovery sequence, the Last Gift Date update fires automatically. Build an exit condition on the workflow: Last Gift Date is less than 1 day ago removes the contact from the sequence. Write Recovery Successful to a DonorPerfect custom field so the development director sees it on the constituent record without opening HubSpot.

Step 5. Handle the edge cases.

  • Processor errors that resolve themselves: many gateways retry a soft decline 48 hours later. Wait 48 to 72 hours before triggering the sequence so you do not contact donors whose card actually worked on the second try.
  • Tribute gifts: a one-time tribute is not a sustainer failure. Confirm your sequence only triggers on contacts with Recurring Gift Status is Active, not just any donor with no recent gift.
  • Card expirations versus charge declines: both surface as failed transactions in DonorPerfect. The card-update message works for both, but the SMS version should be saved for expirations (where the donor must act) and avoided for declines that might just be a temporary bank issue.

What it looks like in production

For an illustrative organization the size of Cedar Hollow (3 chapters, $1.6M revenue, somewhere around 600 active sustainers), a properly wired same-day recovery sequence usually recovers 40% to 55% of lapsed sustainers inside the first 21 days, compared to roughly 15% to 20% on a quarterly manual outreach cadence. On 600 sustainers with a typical 8% to 10% annual involuntary churn rate, that is 48 to 60 expected lapses per year, of which the better sequence saves an extra 15 to 25 sustainers. At a $20 average monthly gift, that is $3,600 to $6,000 in recovered annual revenue plus the multi-year value of donors who would otherwise have churned permanently.

The bigger win is qualitative. The shelter staff stop running a monthly recovery report and start treating sustainer health as a system that runs itself, with their attention reserved for the major-donor calls that actually need a human.

Builder takeaway

  • The trigger that matters is not “Last Gift Date is old.” It is “Last Gift Date is old AND Recurring Gift Status is Active.” Without the second clause you will spam every donor who ever gave once.
  • Wait 48 to 72 hours before firing the sequence to let processor retries do their job. Donors get annoyed when you ask them to update a card that the gateway successfully charged this morning.
  • Write recovery status back to DonorPerfect so the development team sees the work in the system they live in, instead of switching to HubSpot to find out what happened.
