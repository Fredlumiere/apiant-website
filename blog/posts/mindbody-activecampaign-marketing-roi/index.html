Closing Medspa Marketing ROI With Mindbody Sales Pipeline in ActiveCampaign

Fred Lumiere Jan 6, 2025 6 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#ActiveCampaign#CRMConnect#Data Engine

The marketing director at a busy medspa is allocating $18,000 a month across Meta, Google Search, and an email-only campaign segment. At the end of the quarter, the CEO asks the question every wellness exec asks: “Which channel sold the most Botox?”

The honest answer is “I don’t know.” ActiveCampaign tells her which emails got opened. Meta Ads Manager tells her which ads got clicked. Mindbody tells her how much was sold. Nothing joins them at the patient level. So the budget allocation defaults to whichever channel had the loudest internal advocate that quarter.

Hypothetical: Vela Aesthetics Medspa (illustrative), a two-location medspa with 11 providers and 2,800 active patients, ran exactly this scenario for two years. The marketing director knew she was guessing. The CEO knew it too. Nobody had the time to wire up real attribution because it would mean a custom build connecting Mindbody sales to ActiveCampaign campaigns at the patient level.

That custom build is what CRMConnect ships out of the box as the Mindbody Sales Pipeline for ActiveCampaign.

How the Sales Pipeline Add-On Works

CRMConnect Mindbody to ActiveCampaign creates a dedicated Sales Pipeline in ActiveCampaign as a deal pipeline. From the app page: “Automatically log every client purchase as a detailed deal, clearly connecting revenue with marketing efforts, staff activities, and follow-ups.” And: “ActiveCampaign helps you clearly connect customer actions, like purchases or membership sign-ups, to specific marketing activities. Easily measure how well your campaigns and channels perform, so you can see exactly what’s driving your ROI.”

Each Mindbody purchase becomes an ActiveCampaign deal automatically, with:

  • Amount
  • Item (service, package, membership, retail product)
  • Staff (provider)
  • Location
  • Date
  • Associated contact

Because ActiveCampaign deals can be tied to campaign automations via the last_campaign_engaged_with and standard automation membership fields, you can build a join that did not exist before: “this patient engaged with this email campaign in the 14 days before this purchase.”

The pipeline refreshes every 15 minutes; the daily visit reconciliation runs once per day. So same-day campaign attribution is feasible.

A deal record looks roughly like:

{
  "deal_pipeline": "Mindbody Sales Pipeline",
  "deal_value": 1850.00,
  "item": "Botox - 30 units",
  "staff": "Dr. Reyes",
  "location": "Vela Downtown",
  "associated_contact": "patient@example.com",
  "purchase_date": "2026-05-15T14:30:00",
  "last_campaign_engaged_with": "Spring Botox Refresh - Existing Patients"
}

Mapping Campaigns to Purchases

The attribution rule that actually works in wellness is “last-touch within window.” Whichever campaign the patient last engaged with (open, click, or active automation membership) inside a defined window (commonly 14 to 30 days) before purchase gets the credit.

This is not the only attribution model, but it is the one ActiveCampaign supports natively and it is honest about its limitations. Multi-touch attribution sounds better in theory and is essentially impossible to ground in real wellness purchase data without dedicated analytics tooling.

To make the model real, do three things:

  1. Tag campaigns clearly. Every campaign and automation should have a clean name that reads as a line item in a report. “Spring Botox Refresh - Existing Patients” and “New Patient Welcome - Injectables” should never be the same campaign.
  2. Build a custom field for last_campaign_engaged_with if it does not already exist, populated by every automation entry-point. Use the field as the joining key from deal back to campaign.
  3. Filter the report to a window. Use the deal’s purchase_date minus the contact’s last_email_engaged_date. Drop anything outside the 30-day window into an “untracked” bucket.

The Mindbody client indexes and custom fields sync also helps here. From the app page: “Automatically sync Mindbody indexes and custom fields directly into ActiveCampaign, enabling powerful segmentation and highly personalized campaigns.” If you tag patients by procedure interest in Mindbody, those indexes flow to ActiveCampaign, so you can segment campaigns by interest and attribute purchases back without guessing.

Reading the ROI Report Honestly

Build the ROI report as a custom ActiveCampaign deal report grouped by last_campaign_engaged_with, summing deal value over the last 30 days. Then add a denominator: divide by the campaign’s reported cost (you may need to maintain campaign costs in a side spreadsheet, or in a dedicated campaign_cost custom object if you are willing to build one).

What the marketing director at Vela learned in the first 30 days of doing this honestly:

  • The “Spring Botox Refresh” email campaign drove 4x the per-dollar revenue of the matching Meta ad set. Email was being underfunded.
  • The Google Search budget was driving high deal volume but mostly low-margin retail product purchases. The reported ROI looked great until she controlled for margin.
  • Two of the eleven providers were getting almost all the email-driven appointments. The other nine were essentially walk-in only. That changed how the providers were marketed individually.

Honest reading also means acknowledging what you cannot attribute. Walk-in retail purchases from patients who never engaged with any campaign go into the “untracked” bucket. That number is usually 20 to 40 percent of revenue. The mistake is to pretend it is zero or to assign it proportionally; both distort the picture.

Representative Reallocation Decisions at 2,800 Patients

In a representative deployment at medspas of this scale, the first full quarter of honest attribution typically produces 2 to 4 budget reallocation decisions. The most common patterns: cutting an underperforming Meta ad set by 30 to 50 percent, doubling the budget on the best-performing email segment, and reallocating retargeting spend from broad audiences to engaged-but-not-purchased patients (a segment that suddenly becomes easy to define).

For Vela specifically (illustrative), the first reallocation moved roughly $4,000/month out of broad-audience Meta retargeting and into a “high-engagement, no-purchase-in-90-days” segment campaign. Reported campaign ROI on the reallocated dollars roughly doubled within 60 days. The total ad budget stayed flat; only the mix changed.

These numbers are illustrative; ROI lift from reallocation depends entirely on how poorly the baseline allocation was performing.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: the last-touch-within-30-days attribution rule and the explicit “untracked” bucket. Both are simple and both are honest.
  • What to watch for: the Mindbody Sales Pipeline refreshes every 15 minutes for new deals; daily visit reconciliation is once per day. Same-day attribution is fine; sub-hour attribution is not. ActiveCampaign deal pipelines and SMS attribution reports require Professional tier. Mindbody to ActiveCampaign sales sync is one-way (Mindbody to ActiveCampaign only).
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: businesses with very long consideration cycles (think six months between first ad impression and first purchase) need real multi-touch attribution; last-touch will overweight the closing-window campaigns and miss the awareness-driving ones.
