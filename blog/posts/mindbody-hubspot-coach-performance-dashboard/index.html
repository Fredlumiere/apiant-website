Building a Coach Performance Dashboard in HubSpot From Mindbody Class Data

Fred Lumiere Jan 15, 2025 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#HubSpot#CRMConnect#AI Co-Pilot

Ask a CrossFit head coach which of their staff has the best retention rate. They will give you a confident answer. Ask them to back it up with numbers, and the answer becomes “well, you know, you can just feel it.”

Mindbody reports are built for accounting, not coaching. You can pull a class-attendance export. You can pull a sales report. You can pull a no-show list. None of them join. Nobody in the box has the time, or the SQL, to glue them together every week. So performance conversations stay impressionistic, lapsed members get noticed three weeks late, and the coach with the highest revenue per hour is usually not the one getting the credit.

Hypothetical: Anvil Strength CrossFit (illustrative), a single-location box with 320 members and 7 class times a day, ran into this every quarterly review. The owner wanted to pay coaches a small variable bonus tied to retention and class fill rates. He could not, because he could not measure either with any confidence.

This post walks through how CRMConnect Mindbody to HubSpot’s built-in pipelines and properties become the source data for a coach performance dashboard that takes about an afternoon to build.

Pipelines You Get for Free With CRMConnect

CRMConnect ships two HubSpot deal pipelines out of the box, pre-populated and continuously updated.

Mindbody Appointment Pipeline. From the app page: “CRMConnect creates and maintains a dedicated Mindbody Appointment Pipeline in HubSpot, automatically representing each appointment as a deal. Each deal contains detailed information, including associated purchases, schedule changes, appointment statuses, and remaining session counts.” Updated every 15 minutes.

Mindbody Sales Pipeline. Itemizes each client purchase as a deal, with attribution to staff, location, and service. Reconciled daily.

Combined with the 120+ custom HubSpot properties CRMConnect maintains on each contact, you now have:

  • Per-contact: last_visit_date, last_visit_staff, total_visits, next_scheduled_appointment, remaining_sessions, membership_status
  • Per-appointment deal: staff (coach), service (class), location, status, purchase association
  • Per-sale deal: amount, item, staff, location, date

That is enough to build coach-level reports. You do not need a data warehouse, dbt, or a BI seat.

Designing the Coach Performance Dashboard

Inside HubSpot Reports (Pro Hub required, Sales Hub Professional or above for full custom reporting), build four reports and pin them to a single dashboard called “Coach Performance.”

Report 1: Classes coached, last 30 days. Deal report filtered to the Mindbody Appointment Pipeline, grouped by staff, count of deals where status = Completed. Stack by class type.

Report 2: Revenue attributed, last 30 days. Deal report on the Mindbody Sales Pipeline, grouped by staff, sum of amount. This is purchase revenue tied to interactions with that staff member, not gross studio revenue.

Report 3: Class fill rate by coach. Deal report on the Appointment Pipeline, grouped by staff and class, count of Completed vs. total scheduled. Sort descending.

Report 4: Lapsed members per coach. Contact report filtered to last_visit_date is more than 14 days ago AND last_visit_staff is known, grouped by last_visit_staff. This is the unsung report. It shows you, by coach, how many of “their” members are at risk right now.

For Anvil’s quarterly review, the owner added a fifth: retention cohort by coach onboarding. Filter contacts to total_visits >= 1 AND created_date between Q1 dates, grouped by first_visit_staff (a property you populate at signup via workflow). Compare percent still active 90 days later. This is the closest you can get to a real retention attribution without spending months on it.

Catching Lapsed Members Before Coaches Notice

The lapsed-member report is the part coaches actually use day-to-day. Build a HubSpot workflow that enrolls contacts on last_visit_date crossed 14 days AND membership_status = Active, and routes a task to the contact’s last_visit_staff with a one-line script: “Saw [first name] hasn’t been in for two weeks. Quick text from you?”

This works because CRMConnect’s last-visit sync is rich: “CRMConnect keeps HubSpot updated with each client’s most recent visit details, including date, type (appointment, class, or arrival), service provided, staff member, and event name.” The coach knows exactly which class the member last attended and can reference it in their text.

For attribution at the appointment level, the appointment deal record in HubSpot looks roughly like:

{
  "deal_pipeline": "Mindbody Appointment Pipeline",
  "deal_stage": "Completed",
  "service": "CrossFit 6:00am",
  "staff": "Marco",
  "location": "Anvil HQ",
  "remaining_sessions": 14,
  "associated_contact": "athlete@example.com",
  "scheduled_date": "2026-05-17T06:00:00",
  "purchase_associated": "10-Class Pack"
}

Representative Insights From the First Quarter

In a representative deployment at boxes of this scale, the first quarter of coach dashboards typically surfaces three patterns. First, the coach with the highest perceived charisma is rarely the highest on retention; retention correlates more strongly with the coach who texts lapsed members. Second, the 6 a.m. class consistently has the lowest fill rate and the highest retention rate, which is counterintuitive until you realize 6 a.m. attendees are extremely committed. Third, lapsed-member texts from the coach who taught their last class convert to a return visit at roughly 30 to 40 percent, versus under 10 percent for generic re-engagement emails.

At Anvil’s scale (320 members, 320 monthly potential lapses), recovering even 15 percent of would-be churners per month is roughly 5 to 8 retained members, or somewhere around $700 to $1,100 in saved monthly MRR. Over a year that pays for the entire integration multiple times over.

These numbers are illustrative; the lapsed-text conversion rate is highly dependent on coach culture and how personal the texts actually feel.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: the four-report dashboard structure. Do not overbuild. Coaches stop looking at dashboards with more than 5 widgets, in my experience.
  • What to watch for: the Mindbody Sales Pipeline reconciles daily, not in real time, so revenue numbers will be one day behind. The Appointment Pipeline updates every 15 minutes, which is fine for everything except live “are we filling tonight’s class” use cases. Also, attributing revenue to a coach is fuzzy by nature; a sale at the front desk after a class may or may not be the teaching coach’s “credit.”
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: appointment-based businesses (one-on-one PT, medspas) get cleaner attribution because every appointment has exactly one provider. Group-class businesses with rotating coaching are noisier and need a clear definition of “credited coach” before you build the dashboard.
