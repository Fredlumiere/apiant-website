Multi-Location Lead Routing Between Mindbody Sites and HighLevel Sub-Accounts

Fred Lumiere Jun 9, 2025 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#HighLevel#CRMConnect#For Enterprise

The Franchise Lead Ownership Fight

Multi-location boutique gym brands almost always have the same internal fight. A lead fills out the corporate website form. Corporate marketing got the click. The lead lives close to Franchise A and works near Franchise B. They visit both gyms in their first month. Who owns the lead? Who gets the commission? Who pays for the ads that brought them in?

Hypothetical: Tradewind Athletic Network (illustrative) is a 12-location franchise brand with 7,200 members. Each franchisee runs their own HighLevel sub-account. Corporate runs the brand-wide marketing. Without clean routing rules baked into the integration, every cross-location member is a Slack argument waiting to happen.

Why Off-the-Shelf Tools Fail at Franchise Scale

Multi-location HighLevel and Mindbody together is a sub-account problem and a site_id problem. Most generic tools handle one or the other:

  • They route by zip code (which breaks at city boundaries and ignores the actual gym the member uses).
  • They route to a single “central” HighLevel account (which the franchisees cannot access).
  • They route to multiple HighLevel sub-accounts but with no dedup, so the same member creates a contact in two sub-accounts.

Custom builds work but require ongoing maintenance every time a new franchise opens, a sub-account is reconfigured, or a member moves to a different gym. The integration has to be the boundary, not the spreadsheet a corporate ops manager maintains by hand.

How Multi-Location Routing Works in CRMConnect

The configuration is straightforward:

  1. Each Mindbody site is registered in CRMConnect with a stable site_id.
  2. Each HighLevel sub-account is registered with a stable subaccount_id.
  3. A site to sub-account map is defined: site_id -> subaccount_id.
  4. The routing rule for new leads (from a centralized capture form):
  5. If the lead form includes a location tag, route to the mapped sub-account and push to the mapped Mindbody site.
  6. If no tag, route to a “central” holding sub-account for triage.
  7. The routing rule for existing members:
  8. Member’s mindbody_home_location field defines the primary sub-account.
  9. Cross-location visits are recorded as events on the same contact, in the home sub-account.

The smart duplicate logic prevents the most common failure: the same email signing up at Site A and Site B in the same week. CRMConnect detects the match, merges into a single contact, and tags both locations on the profile.

Mapping Sites to Sub-Accounts Without Duplicates

Routing configuration:
site-01 (Downtown) -> subaccount-tradewind-downtown
site-02 (Pearl)    -> subaccount-tradewind-pearl
site-03 (Eastside) -> subaccount-tradewind-eastside
...
site-12 (Airport)  -> subaccount-tradewind-airport

HighLevel contact custom fields:
- mindbody_home_location_id          primary site, drives sub-account ownership
- mindbody_home_location_name
- mindbody_visited_sites_30d         list of site_ids
- mindbody_last_visit_site_id        most recent visit location
- mindbody_lead_source_site_id       set at lead capture, never overwritten

The home_location_id is the lead-ownership field. It is set at the moment the lead becomes a paying member and can be reassigned through a documented process (member moves cities, primary gym changes). The visited_sites_30d field captures cross-location activity without changing ownership.

Cross-Site Member Activity Without Account Crossfire

The corporate ops team gets a clean view across all sub-accounts. Each franchisee sees only their own contacts. The cross-location activity is logged where it should be:

  • Member’s home sub-account: full history, primary contact record.
  • Visited (non-home) site’s reporting: appears in a “guest visits” segment, not as a contact record.

A franchisee at Site B who notices their gym is getting frequent visits from a Site A member can request a transfer through the corporate ops process. The system supports the move, but does not allow either franchisee to grab the contact directly. The rule is enforced at the integration layer.

The result: no Slack arguments. Ownership is in one place. Cross-location revenue is attributed to the location that earned it through usage, not the location that found the lead.

Representative Operational Lift at 7,200 Members

Illustrative composites, not a real customer:

  • Duplicate contacts across HighLevel sub-accounts per quarter: down from a recurring cleanup project to roughly 5 to 15 surfaced merges per quarter.
  • Corporate ops hours per week on franchise lead disputes: roughly 6 to 10 hours, reclaimed.
  • Lead-to-first-visit time for centrally captured leads: cut by 1 to 2 days, because the routing happens at intake instead of in a manual hand-off.
  • Member transfer requests handled inside one process (rather than ad hoc): all of them, because the integration enforces a single home location.

The lift is mostly political. The integration ends the argument by making the ownership rule a system, not a negotiation.

Builder Takeaway

  • Bake the site to sub-account map into the integration. Routing in spreadsheets does not scale past three franchises.
  • Use home_location_id as the single ownership field. Cross-location visits are activity, not ownership claims.
  • Let the integration enforce the dedup. Smart matching at the boundary saves quarterly cleanup projects.
