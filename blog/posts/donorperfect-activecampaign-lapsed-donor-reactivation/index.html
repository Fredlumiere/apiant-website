A lapsed-donor reactivation workflow for homeless services: DonorPerfect to ActiveCampaign in real time

Fred Lumiere Aug 2, 2024 6 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#ActiveCampaign#CRMConnect#Bi-Directional Sync

Acquiring a new donor costs five to ten times what it costs to reactivate a lapsed one. Every fundraising textbook says this. Most agencies act as if it were not true.

The reason is operational, not philosophical. Reactivation requires knowing which donors are lapsed (and how long), pulling them into the right cadence at the right moment, and stopping the cadence the second a gift arrives. None of those steps work cleanly when DonorPerfect holds the giving data and ActiveCampaign holds the email engine and nothing connects the two in real time.

So most agencies run a single annual “we miss you” appeal that gets sent to everyone who has not given in two years, recovers a small fraction, and counts as the lapsed-donor program. Meanwhile, the donors who lapsed 13 months ago (the most recoverable cohort) get the same generic message as the donors who lapsed 36 months ago. Both groups underperform.

Lapsed-donor work is structurally a real-time problem. The recoverability of a lapsed donor decays sharply with time. A donor at 13 months is several times more recoverable than the same donor at 24 months. By 36 months, most are gone.

Native DonorPerfect lapsed reports surface the right list but cannot run an email cadence. Native ActiveCampaign cannot see DonorPerfect gift dates without an integration.

Generic iPaaS connectors sync new gifts but ignore the absence of expected gifts. Lapse triggers require asking “this person used to give regularly and has not given in 13 months,” which is harder than “this person just gave.”

Manual CSV uploads work for an annual blast but not for an ongoing program where new contacts cross each lapse threshold every day.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and ActiveCampaign is built around real-time bi-directional sync of the fields that drive recoverability:

  • Last Gift Date updates in ActiveCampaign the moment DonorPerfect records a gift.
  • Total Gifts and Largest Gift sync as custom fields so you can branch by historical value.
  • Flags as Tags Sync carries DonorPerfect Flags into ActiveCampaign so major-donor or board flags exclude contacts from automated reactivation.
  • Bi-directional sync writes reactivation status back to DonorPerfect so the development team sees journey state in the system they live in.
  • Gifts Pipeline writes new gifts as ActiveCampaign deals, which is what closes the reactivation loop the second a gift arrives.
  • CodeSync keeps Campaign and Solicitation codes consistent so reactivation reports stay accurate across appeal cycles.

Three reactivation sequences by lapse window

Illustrative scenario: Harbor Light Outreach, an urban homeless services agency with 2,900 active donors and a $9.5M budget (hypothetical example, not a real organization).

Window 1: 13 to 17 months lapsed (recently lapsed). These donors most likely lapsed accidentally. A card expired, an email change broke the renewal series, or the year-end ask landed during a busy week. The right cadence is warm and short:

  • Day 0: a personal note from a program director, naming the impact the donor’s previous gifts made, no ask.
  • Day 7: an updated story from the same program, with a soft renewal ask.
  • Day 14: a recurring-gift invitation, framed as easier than remembering to give annually.
  • Day 30: if no gift, mark Lapsed 18 Months and stop the sequence.

Window 2: 18 to 23 months lapsed. Recoverability drops but is still meaningful. The cadence shifts to a single high-quality piece of content and a clearer ask:

  • Day 0: a year-in-review email focused on the agency’s most consequential program from the past year.
  • Day 14: a renewal ask with a matching-gift element if one is available.
  • Day 30: a final attempt with a smaller gift suggestion ($25 to $50, regardless of the donor’s historical amount).
  • Day 45: stop the sequence.

Window 3: 24+ months lapsed. Treat as a separate cold-list acquisition effort, not as reactivation. A single annual reengagement email per year plus inclusion in the next direct mail drop. Do not run a multi-touch cadence; the open and engagement rates will not justify the deliverability cost.

Suppression rules across all three windows. Use Flags as Tags to exclude contacts flagged Major Donor, Board Member, Staff, Deceased, or Do Not Solicit. Major donors who lapse need a personal call from the executive director, not an automated sequence. Build the suppression as a list-level filter so it applies to all three windows automatically.

Closing the loop

The most important step in reactivation is stopping the sequence the moment a gift arrives. Two pieces:

  • Exit on new gift. Configure each sequence to exit immediately when Last Gift Date is less than 1 day ago. This prevents a donor who reactivates on Day 3 from receiving the Day 7 email and feeling like the agency was not paying attention.
  • Write reactivation status back to DonorPerfect. When a sequence completes successfully (gift recorded), write Reactivated 2026 to a DonorPerfect custom field. When a sequence completes unsuccessfully, write the lapse stage (Lapsed 18 Months, Lapsed 24 Months). The development team should see journey state inside DonorPerfect without opening ActiveCampaign.

What to measure

The standard reactivation metrics:

  • Reactivation rate by window. Recently lapsed (13-17 months) should run 8% to 15%. Mid-lapse (18-23 months) typically 3% to 6%. Deep lapse (24+ months) below 2%.
  • Second-gift rate among reactivators. The real value of reactivation is the donor who gives once and then gives again. Track twelve-month giving among reactivated donors.
  • Retained value at 12 months. Reactivated donors who give twice in twelve months are often more valuable than first-time acquisitions, because the relationship history reduces their churn risk.
  • Cost per reactivation. Sum the staff time, ad spend, and platform costs allocated to reactivation and divide by reactivations recorded. Compare to cost per new donor acquisition. Reactivation should win by a wide margin.

Edge cases worth handling

Donors who lapsed for a reason. Some donors lapse because they stopped supporting the cause, moved, or had a life change. Honor the unsubscribe and the do-not-contact request immediately. Do not retry these donors in a future reactivation campaign.

Tribute and memorial gifts. A donor who gave once as a tribute gift is not really a lapsed donor. They were a one-time relationship to the honoree, not the agency. Exclude tribute-only givers from the standard reactivation cadence.

Recurring sustainers who lapsed. A sustainer whose recurring schedule expired (card update problem) needs the sustainer recovery workflow, not the lapsed-donor cadence. Make sure your reactivation list excludes contacts who are currently in active sustainer recovery.

Hard bounces. ActiveCampaign marks the address as bounced. Write that back to DonorPerfect so the direct mail team can verify the postal address or suppress.

What it looks like in production

For an illustrative agency the size of Harbor Light (2,900 active donors, $9.5M budget), a properly staged reactivation program typically recovers an additional 5% to 8% of recently lapsed donors above the baseline annual appeal, plus a smaller incremental recovery in the mid-lapse cohort. On a base of 800 recently lapsed donors, that is 40 to 65 additional reactivations per year, each carrying first-year revenue around $80 to $150. Add the multi-year retention lift on reactivated donors who give again and the program pays for itself in the first cycle.

Builder takeaway

  • Three windows, three cadences. One generic “we miss you” send treats every lapsed donor the same and underperforms in every cohort.
  • Exit on new gift is non-negotiable. A donor who reactivates on Day 3 must not receive the Day 7 email.
  • Write reactivation status back to DonorPerfect. The development team needs to see journey state in the system they live in or the program will be invisible to them and lose internal support.
