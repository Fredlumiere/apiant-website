Per-Location Reporting in Keap for a 4-Site Boutique Gym Network

Fred Lumiere Dec 18, 2024 6 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Keap#CRMConnect#For Enterprise

A 3-person marketing team at a 4-location boutique gym is, in practice, a 1.5-person team after you account for ad management, content production, and weekly meetings. The remaining time is supposed to handle per-location reporting for four different general managers, each of whom wants to know how their site is performing this week and how it compares to last month.

Without proper per-location data in Keap, that reporting work looks like this: pull four Mindbody CSV exports (one per site), open four Keap reports, cross-reference in a Google Sheet, build four mini-dashboards, send four emails, repeat next week. Each cycle eats 4 to 6 hours of one marketer’s week. Over a year that is 200 to 300 hours of pure reporting overhead with no creative output to show for it.

Hypothetical: Ember Athletic (illustrative), a 4-location boutique gym network with 2,100 total members and a central marketing team of 3, was running exactly this. The marketers were burning out. The general managers were complaining that their location’s data felt like an afterthought. The owner kept hearing “we need a BI person” and resisting because that is a $90K hire.

The right answer is not a BI person. The right answer is having the integration tag site-level data into Keap properly so reporting becomes a saved-search problem, not a manual-export problem.

Tagging Contacts by Mindbody Site in Keap

CRMConnect Mindbody to Keap supports multiple Mindbody locations syncing into a single Keap account. The FAQ on the app page is explicit: “Can multiple locations sync to one Keap account? Yes, easily manage multiple Mindbody locations in one Keap account.”

Site identity is carried into Keap as a combination of:

  • A primary_location field on each contact (their home site)
  • A locations_visited multi-value field listing every site they have visited or purchased at
  • Site-specific tags (site-ember-downtown, site-ember-eastside, site-ember-north, site-ember-westgate)
  • All standard Mindbody fields (last visit, last purchase, etc.) carrying site context

The purchased service tagging feature also encodes the site of purchase in the tag, so a class-pack bought at Eastside applies a purchased-10pack-eastside tag while the same pack bought at Downtown applies purchased-10pack-downtown. This is the foundation for per-location reporting in Keap without duplicating contacts.

A member who joined at Downtown and started visiting Eastside three months later has one Keap contact that looks like:

{
  "email": "member@example.com",
  "firstname": "Andre",
  "primary_location": "Ember Downtown",
  "locations_visited": ["Ember Downtown", "Ember Eastside"],
  "tags": [
    "site-ember-downtown",
    "site-ember-eastside",
    "purchased-unlimited-downtown",
    "year-1-member"
  ],
  "last_visit_location": "Ember Eastside",
  "last_visit_date": "2026-05-15",
  "last_visit_service": "Strength Class",
  "last_visit_staff": "Yusra",
  "membership_status": "Active"
}

Building Per-Location Pipelines Without Duplicating Records

The temptation is to give each location its own Keap account. Do not. You will lose the cross-location member view (which is one of the few things Keap actually does well), you will pay 4x the licensing, and you will need to reconcile cross-site memberships manually.

The right pattern is one Keap account with location-tagged saved searches and per-location campaign sequences.

Per-location lapsed-member view: filter tags contains site-ember-eastside AND last_visit_date crossed 14 days AND membership_status = Active. This is the Eastside GM’s lapsed list, scoped to her members only. Each GM gets their own bookmarked URL.

Per-location new-member welcome: four versions of the same campaign sequence, each gated by site tag. The Downtown welcome references Downtown-specific instructors and class times. The Eastside welcome references Eastside details. Same automation skeleton, location-aware content.

Per-location retention reports: Keap saved searches grouped by primary_location, filtered by membership status thresholds. The marketing team builds these once and they update themselves.

For cross-location strategic campaigns, the locations_visited multi-value field is the join. A “try our new Eastside location” campaign sent only to Downtown-primary members who have never visited Eastside takes one filter to define: primary_location = Ember Downtown AND locations_visited does not contain Ember Eastside.

Catching Site-Level Underperformance Early

The retention question every multi-site owner cares about: “Is one of my locations leaking members faster than the others?” Without per-location tagging, this question takes a week to answer and is usually answered too late.

With Keap saved searches keyed off site tags, the answer takes about 90 seconds: pull a count of contacts with tags contains site-ember-westgate AND last_visit_date crossed 21 days AND membership_status = Active, divide by total site-ember-westgate active members, and you have the site’s drift rate. Compare across the four sites. If Westgate is at 12 percent monthly drift while the other three are at 6 to 7 percent, you have a problem to investigate this month, not three months from now.

The two-way client profile sync (with the 15-minute Keap-to-Mindbody push for any changes made in Keap) means the marketing team can correct site-tag errors without breaking Mindbody. If a member’s primary location was set incorrectly, fixing it in Keap propagates back to Mindbody on the next sync.

Representative Time Saved at 2,100 Members

In a representative deployment at networks of this scale, the per-location reporting workload typically drops from 4 to 6 hours per week per marketer down to under 30 minutes (mostly spent reviewing the saved searches and writing the GM commentary). For Ember’s 3-person marketing team, that is roughly 12 to 16 hours per week recovered, or 600 to 800 hours per year, which is roughly one-third of a full-time headcount.

The qualitative win matters more. General managers stop feeling like afterthoughts because their data appears in their inbox automatically and on time. Marketing stops being the bottleneck for site-level conversations. The conversation in the weekly leadership meeting shifts from “marketing hasn’t sent the reports yet” to “Westgate’s drift rate ticked up, let’s talk about it.”

For Ember specifically, the marketing team was able to launch two new ad-creative tests per month after the reporting overhead came off their plate, both of which had been backlogged for the previous six months. The lift from those tests covered the integration cost in the first quarter.

These numbers are illustrative; the per-location reporting workload depends heavily on how often GMs actually use the reports they ask for.

Builder Takeaway

  • What to copy: the one-Keap-account, location-tagged pattern. Do not split into multiple Keap accounts unless you have a hard legal reason (truly independent franchise ownership with no data-share clause).
  • What to watch for: site tagging only works if Mindbody site naming is consistent and clean. Audit your Mindbody site names before going live; “Downtown” vs. “Ember Downtown” vs. “downtown-1” being three different site identifiers in Mindbody will produce three different Keap tags that mean the same thing. Also, Mindbody to Keap sync for appointments and sales is one-way; only client profiles sync two-way (instantly Mindbody to Keap, every 15 minutes Keap to Mindbody).
  • When this pattern doesn’t fit: networks where each location has fundamentally different services or pricing (think a fitness studio chain that also owns a medspa under the same parent company) probably want distinct Keap instances, because the campaign logic will not be portable across the locations anyway.
