Multi-Site Appointment Reporting in Klaviyo for a 6-Location Strength Network

Fred Lumiere Apr 21, 2026 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Klaviyo#CRMConnect#For Enterprise

The Hidden Cost of Site-Blind Reporting

A six-location boutique gym runs a central marketing team out of one office, but the members live at one (sometimes two) specific gyms. Every operational decision (staffing, programming, peak-hour adjustments) is per-site. Every marketing campaign in a centralized Klaviyo account should be per-site too. The problem is that the site dimension usually never makes it from Mindbody into Klaviyo, so the central team ends up sending the same blast to every member regardless of home gym.

Hypothetical: Northwind Strength Club (illustrative) has six locations, 4,800 members, and a central ecommerce and Klaviyo team. The team can pull a “last appointment booked” segment, but they cannot break it down by site. So “we noticed you booked a session, here is a tip from your coach” goes out signed by the wrong head coach, with the wrong gym address, half the time.

Why Off-the-Shelf Tools Fail at This

Most generic glue tools treat Mindbody as a single endpoint. They pull appointment events and push them to Klaviyo, but they drop or flatten the site_id in the process. Even when site_id makes it through, it lands as a string on a single profile attribute that gets overwritten on the next sync. There is no clean way to attribute “appointment at Site B” separately from “appointment at Site C” on the same profile.

Building this in-house is doable but tedious: per-site pipelines, per-site rate-limit accounting, per-site reconciliation. Most teams build one and never get to the second.

The APIANT Approach

CRMConnect Mindbody to Klaviyo includes Multi-Location Support as a first-class feature, with appointment sync that preserves the site dimension end-to-end. Three pieces matter here:

  • Multi-Location Support for handling appointment events from many Mindbody sites under one Klaviyo account.
  • Appointment Sync and Tracking for upcoming, completed, and canceled appointments per site.
  • Real-Time Client Profile Sync so the home-location attribute on the Klaviyo profile stays accurate.

Each appointment event lands in Klaviyo with the site_id attached. The profile carries a home location attribute. Segmentation and flows can branch on either.

What Appointment Sync Exposes in Klaviyo

The shape of the data on the Klaviyo side:

Profile attributes:
- mindbody_home_location_id          e.g. "site-04"
- mindbody_home_location_name        e.g. "Northwind Pearl District"
- mindbody_last_appointment_at       ISO timestamp
- mindbody_last_appointment_site_id  e.g. "site-02"

Event: "Booked Appointment"
Properties:
- site_id
- site_name
- appointment_type
- coach_name
- scheduled_at

The home location is the member’s anchor. The per-event site_id captures cross-site visits (a member who travels and books at a different gym). Both are queryable.

Designing Per-Site Flows From a Central Account

The pattern is a single Klaviyo flow with a site-based branch at the top:

  1. Trigger: “Booked Appointment” event.
  2. First split: properties.site_id. Branch into six (or however many) site-specific paths.
  3. Each path: uses the correct site’s coach signature, address block, and parking instructions.
  4. Wait until 2 hours after appointment.
  5. Post-appointment follow-up: signed by the per-site coach, with a one-click rebook link.

You only build the flow once. The branching does the per-site work. The central marketing team stays central, the message stays local.

For broadcast campaigns (not flows), build per-site lists as Klaviyo dynamic segments on mindbody_home_location_id. A “weekend hours change” announcement at Site B only goes to members anchored to Site B. The site name is on the profile, so the merge variable in the subject line writes itself.

Cross-Site Loyalty Without Crossing Customer Boundaries

A member with appointments across two sites is a different segment than a single-site member, and they need different handling. Use the event stream to compute a cross-site activity attribute:

mindbody_distinct_sites_30d: int
  • 1: standard per-site flows.
  • 2 or more: invite to a “network” tier with cross-site perks.

This is where centralization actually pays off. A single-site member should feel like they are talking to their gym. A multi-site member should feel like they are part of the network. Both can come out of one Klaviyo account if the site dimension survives the sync.

Representative Per-Site Engagement Lift at 4,800 Members

Illustrative composites, not a real customer:

  • Open rate on post-appointment follow-ups (generic, single-coach signature): roughly 28 to 34 percent.
  • Open rate with per-site coach signature, address, and rebook link: a lift into the 42 to 50 percent range.
  • Rebook within seven days from the same follow-up: a lift in the 5 to 9 percentage-point range.
  • Time the central marketing coordinator spends each week building site-specific segments by hand: down by an order of magnitude, because the flow does it once.

These numbers move with branding, coach quality, and your existing baseline. The mechanism is unchanged: the right site signature on the right member matters more than the email copy.

Builder Takeaway

  • Treat site_id as a profile attribute and an event property, not a single overwritten field. You need both for flows and broadcasts to work.
  • Branch a single flow on site_id rather than building one flow per location. Six paths in one flow is six times less maintenance than six separate flows.
  • Compute a distinct_sites_30d attribute for network-tier perks. The math is cheap and the segment is real.
