Attendance and Cancellation Sync: Saving Swim School Re-Enrollment in Klaviyo

Fred Lumiere Nov 4, 2025 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Klaviyo#CRMConnect

Why Seasonal Programs Bleed at Re-Enrollment

Swim schools, kids’ martial arts, music academies, any program that runs in seasons, all share the same shape: an eight to twelve week session, a hard re-enrollment cutoff at the end, and a parent on the receiving end who is juggling four other activities and a part-time job. If you miss the parent’s attention in the two-week window before the cutoff, the spot goes to a waitlist family and your child does not come back next session.

Hypothetical: Tidepool Swim Academy (illustrative) is a 620-kid swim school running seasonal sessions out of one indoor pool. The actual re-enrollment process is fine. The breakdown is upstream: the parents who lose momentum (two cancellations in a row, a vacation, a sick week) are not flagged anywhere. Klaviyo sends the same generic re-enrollment blast to everyone the week before signup opens. The kids most at risk of dropping never get a targeted save attempt.

What Mindbody Tells You vs. What Klaviyo Can Act On

Mindbody knows everything that matters here. Every class has an attendance list. Every cancellation is timestamped. Every enrollment has a start and end. The problem is that none of it gets to Klaviyo. The front desk pulls a “missed three or more classes” report on a Friday, hands it to the marketing coordinator on Monday, who builds a one-off Klaviyo segment by Wednesday. By then the cancellation window has already closed.

Off-the-shelf glue tools (Zapier, Make) can sometimes push a single attendance event, but they choke on the volume (a swim school with 14 weekly classes generates thousands of attendance records per session) and they cannot dedup the no-show vs. excused-absence distinction without a custom layer of logic.

The APIANT Approach

CRMConnect Mindbody to Klaviyo handles attendance and cancellation as first-class sync events. Three pieces from the API App do the work:

  • Class Sync and Attendance Tracking keeps Klaviyo current on upcoming bookings, attendance, and cancellations.
  • Real-Time Client Profile Sync keeps parent contact records aligned, with the kid’s enrollment status as profile attributes.
  • Visit Tracking reconciles recent visits daily so a missed-class flow does not fire on a false negative.

The parent’s email is the unique key on the Klaviyo side. Kids without their own email (which is almost all of them at this age) attach to the parent profile, so the flow targets the human who actually books the class.

Designing the Cancellation-Recovery Flow

The first flow you want is short and aimed at the wobble, not the cliff:

  1. Trigger: Mindbody cancellation event, scoped to a class that was within 24 hours.
  2. Wait 4 hours. Avoids ambushing a sick parent.
  3. Email 1: We saved your spot for next week. Reaffirms the kid’s next scheduled class with the date and time pre-filled from the Klaviyo profile.
  4. Branch on mindbody_cancellations_last_30d:
  5. If less than 2: end flow.
  6. If 2 or more: continue.
  7. Email 2 (two days later): A note from your child’s coach. Offers a makeup class slot. This is the targeted save attempt that the blast cannot do.

The branching is what makes this useful. A single cancellation is noise. The third cancellation in a month is a signal, and your save attempt should reflect that.

Designing the Re-Enrollment-Window Flow

The second flow runs once per session and only against parents whose kids are on the borderline:

  1. Trigger: Date-based, 14 days before re-enrollment opens.
  2. Audience: profiles where mindbody_active_enrollment is true.
  3. Branch on mindbody_attendance_rate_session:
  4. 80 percent or higher: standard “secure your spot” email.
  5. 50 to 80 percent: “we noticed some missed classes” email with the coach’s note and a check-in survey.
  6. Under 50 percent: skip email, route to the head coach for a manual call. (Klaviyo profile is tagged for the front-desk dashboard.)
  7. Wait 7 days, then suppress profiles who have re-enrolled.
  8. Final reminder 48 hours before cutoff for the still-not-renewed segment.

The “skip email, route to a coach” branch is the part most automation skips. The lowest-attendance families do not need another email, they need a human. Klaviyo does not have to do the call, it just has to flag the right people.

Klaviyo profile attributes for swim school re-enrollment:
- mindbody_active_enrollment: true|false
- mindbody_cancellations_last_30d: int
- mindbody_attendance_rate_session: 0-100
- mindbody_next_class_at: ISO timestamp
- mindbody_kid_name: string (custom attribute on parent profile)

Representative Re-Enrollment Lift at 620 Kids

Illustrative composites, not a real customer:

  • Re-enrollment rate baseline before the flows: roughly 65 to 70 percent.
  • Re-enrollment rate with attendance-segmented flows: a lift into the 75 to 82 percent range, mostly from the 50 to 80 percent attendance segment that previously received the generic blast.
  • Front-desk hours per session spent building lists: roughly 6 to 8 hours, fully reclaimed.
  • “Did anyone follow up after my kid missed last week?” parent complaints: down sharply, because someone did.

Your real lift depends on your baseline, your pricing, and whether the head coach actually makes the manual calls. The integration just gives you the right list.

Builder Takeaway

  • Treat cancellations and attendance as Klaviyo events, not as report exports. The latency difference is the entire game.
  • Branch on attendance rate, not just on “did they enroll.” Three buckets (healthy, wobbling, gone) deserve three different treatments.
  • Reserve human outreach for the lowest-attendance segment and let Klaviyo handle the rest. The tooling should tell you who to call, not pretend a sequence can replace the call.
