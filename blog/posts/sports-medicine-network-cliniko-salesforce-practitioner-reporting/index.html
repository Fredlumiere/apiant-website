Network-Level Practitioner and Location Reporting for a Sports Medicine Group With Cliniko + Salesforce

Fred Lumiere Sep 18, 2025 4 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#Salesforce#CRMConnect#For Enterprise

The hardest report in a multi-clinic sports medicine network is the one leadership most wants and the one that never seems to get built. Each clinic has its own Cliniko schedule. Each contracted team wants its own case and outcome report. The COO wants a network-level view. Stitching all of that together from five Cliniko exports is the project that perpetually gets bumped because every quarter it is too much manual work to justify.

Take an illustrative example: Apex Sports Medicine Network, a hypothetical group with 5 clinics across 2 states, 24 practitioners, and contracts with two professional sports teams. Each clinic, each contracted team, and the network leadership all want different views of the same underlying data. Pulling those reports from 5 separate Cliniko exports and stitching them together is a manual, error-prone job. It is also the report leadership uses to decide on team contracts and clinic-level investment.

The reporting tax of running 5 clinics on 5 separate Cliniko schedules

A typical month at a 5-clinic, 24-practitioner network produces something like 2,800 to 3,500 appointments, 1,200 to 1,500 invoices, and 30 to 50 active funded cases across contracted teams and individual referrals. Each clinic exports its own data. Each contracted team report requires filtering for that team’s athletes. The network-level view requires reconciling practitioner identities and clinic identities across the five exports (the same practitioner may work at two clinics; the same clinic may serve multiple teams). Producing all of this manually consumes between 1.5 and 2.5 days per month. Errors compound.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

Cliniko’s reporting is single-instance by design. BI dashboards layered on individual exports give per-clinic views but not a network roll-up unless someone maintains a careful entity-resolution layer between exports. Spreadsheet-based consolidation works at 2 clinics and breaks at 5. A custom data warehouse can solve it, but is a significant ongoing engineering investment relative to the value of the reports.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to Salesforce supports Cliniko Practitioners and Cliniko Businesses (locations) as native Salesforce custom objects. That means every practitioner and every location, across however many Cliniko instances feed the integration, lands in a single Salesforce org with a stable identity. Appointments and Invoices stream in as Salesforce objects with practitioner and business as related records. Network-level, clinic-level, and contracted-team reports all become Salesforce dashboards built on the same dataset.

Concrete steps

  1. Enable Cliniko Practitioner and Business syncs to Salesforce custom objects. Resolve practitioner and clinic identities once at setup so the same practitioner across two clinics is one Salesforce Practitioner record.
  2. Enable Cliniko Appointments and Invoices as Salesforce objects, with relationships to the Practitioner and Business records. Appointments and invoices from all 5 Cliniko instances land in the same Salesforce org with the right parent relationships.
  3. Build the practitioner-level report. Group by Practitioner. Metrics: arrived appointments (last 30 days), DNA rate, average billed per session, rebooking rate (% of arrived appointments followed by a booking within 28 days), utilisation (arrived appointments versus scheduled hours).
  4. Build the location-level report. Group by Business (clinic). Metrics: utilisation, new patient acquisition, average case length (in sessions), revenue per practitioner FTE, share of contracted-team activity.
  5. Build the contracted-team report. Filter on the case referral source or a case tag that identifies the team. Output: athletes seen, sessions delivered, case milestone progress, average return-to-play time (if the practice tracks that as a case milestone). One report per contracted team, refreshed automatically.
  6. Build the COO dashboard. A single Salesforce dashboard with arrived appointments, billed revenue, DNA rate, and utilisation by clinic, alongside total network-level numbers. The brief: the COO can read it in 90 seconds and know whether the network is on plan.

Outcome

A network matching Apex’s profile typically eliminates the monthly manual report cycle entirely (1.5 to 2.5 days recovered per month) and shifts the contracted-team reports from a manual deliverable into an automated one (with corresponding improvement in contract retention because the team partners now get clean reports without a delay). The COO dashboard becomes the leadership team’s single source of truth. Decisions about clinic investment, practitioner hiring, and contract pricing get made with real numbers instead of educated guesses.

Multi-clinic, multi-instance considerations

Where multiple Cliniko instances feed a single Salesforce org, two things deserve attention. First, practitioner identity resolution should be confirmed at setup so a practitioner working across two clinics is not double-counted. Second, business location naming should be standardized so the COO dashboard groups correctly. Both are one-time configuration tasks that pay off every reporting cycle thereafter.

Builder takeaway

  • Practitioner and Business as Salesforce objects are the foundation. Without them, every cross-clinic report is a manual reconciliation.
  • Build the COO dashboard last, but design for it first. The atomic reports (practitioner, location, contracted team) should all roll up cleanly into the executive view.
  • Contracted-team reports tend to be the most visible win. Partners notice when their monthly report arrives reliably and without errors. That is a contract-renewal lever.
