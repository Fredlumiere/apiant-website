An Always-On Reminder and Recall Engine for an Osteopathy Practice With Cliniko and ActiveCampaign

Fred Lumiere Jul 1, 2024 5 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#ActiveCampaign#CRMConnect

A shared inbox can handle reminder duties for one practitioner. Maybe two. At two locations and five osteopaths it stops working, and you can see it stop working in the schedule: holes that should have been pre-booked, lapsed patients nobody chased, a Did Not Arrive rate that creeps up because nobody got the 24-hour text.

Take an illustrative example: Brookline Osteopathic Health, a hypothetical 2-location, 5-osteopath practice. Reception sends manual reminders from a shared inbox. Lapsed patients (no visit in 60+ days) get noticed only when someone runs a Cliniko report, usually too late to recover them at any meaningful rate. The practice owner wants reminders and recall to “just happen” without buying yet another standalone tool that does not know what is on the actual schedule.

Why off-the-shelf tools fail

Standalone reminder apps tend to operate on a flat schedule pulled from a calendar feed. They do not differentiate between a new patient initial assessment and a 4th follow-up, between a single appointment and a group mobility class, between a location the patient has been to before and a brand new clinic. They certainly cannot send a recall sequence the moment a patient becomes “lapsed” based on Cliniko activity, because they cannot see lapse. Most importantly, they cannot suppress a reminder once the appointment is actually completed, which results in awkward post-visit “your appointment is tomorrow” messages that erode trust.

A shared inbox is the other end of the spectrum: highly contextual but does not scale. The receptionist remembers that Mrs. K prefers SMS over email, that Mr. R always comes for the 7am slot, and writes a personal note. That works for 20 reminders a day, not 100.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to ActiveCampaign syncs the appointment data ActiveCampaign needs to drive both reminders and recall: next appointment date, last appointment date, location visited, total appointment count, lifetime value. It applies completion tags when an appointment arrives, which means reminder automations can suppress themselves the moment the patient is in the chair. The integration is multi-location aware out of the box: business location is a field on the contact, so reminders and recall sequences can be branded per clinic without splitting into separate automations.

Concrete steps

  1. Map the four contact properties that earn their keep on every patient. Last Appointment Date Booked, Last Appointment Date Completed, Location of Last Appointment, Total Appointment Count. These four power every downstream automation.
  2. Build the pre-appointment reminder automation. Entry: contact has a Next Appointment Date within 48 hours. Sequence: 48 hour email confirmation, 24 hour SMS reminder, 2 hour SMS final. Use the location field to inject the correct address and parking note.
  3. Add the completion suppression. Use Appointment Completion Tagging so that once the appointment arrives (or moves to DNA or cancelled), the contact is removed from any active reminder branch. This is the rule that prevents the embarrassing “your appointment is tomorrow” sent after the visit happened.
  4. Build the lapsed patient recall automation. Entry: contact has Last Appointment Date Completed older than 60 days AND no Next Appointment Date set AND marketing consent equals true. Sequence: a soft check-in email at day 60, a more direct rebook offer at day 75, a “we will stop messaging” final at day 90. Segment by location so the message comes from the clinic the patient actually went to.
  5. Handle the group mobility classes. Cliniko distinguishes individual appointments from group appointments. Build a separate group-class reminder automation that goes 24 hours and 2 hours before the class, since the cadence and message are different from a 1:1 treatment.
  6. Add a “missed appointment” recovery branch on top of the standard recall. Entry: appointment moved to DNA. Same-day soft message offering to rebook, then a follow-up at day 3 if no booking has been made.

Outcome

A 2-location osteopathy practice running this setup typically pulls the DNA rate down from the 13 to 16 percent band into 7 to 9 percent within a quarter, because nearly every appointment gets three contextual touches and the front desk no longer forgets the awkward ones. The lapsed patient recall sequence usually recovers 10 to 15 percent of contacts that have gone past 60 days without a visit, which for a 5-osteopath practice typically translates to 30 to 50 rebookings per month at an average treatment value of $110 to $140. Reception time spent on reminder texts and recall calls drops by an estimated 8 to 12 hours per week across both locations, which is most of a full FTE of administrative time freed up.

Measuring the result

Build three reports in ActiveCampaign on top of this:

  • Reminder workflow performance: open rate, click-to-confirm rate, no-show rate by reminder cadence.
  • Recall workflow performance: contacts entered, rebooks attributed within 30 days, revenue recovered (joined with Cliniko invoice activity if you also stream invoice data).
  • DNA recovery: DNA appointments per month, rebooked within 7 days, revenue recovered.

The first two pay for the integration on day one. The third is the report you bring to the next leadership meeting.

Builder takeaway

  • Four properties. Last Booked, Last Completed, Location, Total Count. Everything else branches from those.
  • Completion suppression is the rule that protects trust. Build it before the reminder copy.
  • Recall is not a campaign, it is a permanent background process. Set it up once, let it run, monitor monthly.
