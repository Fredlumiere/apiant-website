How a regional food bank turned first-time DonorPerfect gifts into a 30-day HubSpot welcome series

Fred Lumiere Jul 30, 2024 6 min read
#Nonprofit#DonorPerfect#HubSpot#CRMConnect

The cardinal rule of donor retention is brutally simple: a first-time donor who never hears from you again gives a second time at roughly half the rate of one who receives a thoughtful welcome inside the first thirty days. The math is unforgiving. A regional food bank running a holiday appeal can pull in 800 new online donors in three weeks, watch 60% of them never give again, and never know which step of the funnel broke.

The step that broke is usually the same step. The gift hits DonorPerfect at 11 PM on a Tuesday. Nobody hand-exports the constituent file until Friday morning. By the time those donors land in HubSpot, the welcome workflow does not feel like a welcome. It feels like a delayed receipt from a stranger.

You can stitch this together with native exports, Zapier, or a part-time integration consultant. None of them survive contact with reality.

Native DonorPerfect exports run on a human schedule. Someone has to remember. During the holiday surge, that someone is also writing thank-you letters, reconciling the merchant account, and answering board questions.

Generic iPaaS connectors map a contact and stop. They do not understand DonorPerfect’s data model: the difference between a Constituent and a Gift, the role of Solicitation and Sub-Solicitation codes, the way Pledges differ from one-time gifts, what a tribute record looks like. You end up with HubSpot contacts that have a name and an email and almost no signal you can segment against.

Consultants build something that works on day one and slowly rots. Three months in, the development director adds a new appeal code in DonorPerfect, the HubSpot dropdown does not know it exists, the workflow enrollment trigger silently stops firing, and nobody notices until renewal season.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect for DonorPerfect and HubSpot is a purpose-built API App, not a generic connector. It syncs over 140 donor properties from DonorPerfect into HubSpot bi-directionally and in real time. That includes the date fields that matter most for triggering workflows: First Gift, Last Gift, Donor Created, plus campaign, solicitation, donor type, communication preferences, and the full Gift record itself.

Three features carry most of the weight for a welcome series:

  • Real-time donor updates push First Gift Date into HubSpot the moment DonorPerfect records the gift. That timestamp becomes your enrollment trigger.
  • CodeSync for dropdowns keeps your DonorPerfect appeal codes, campaign codes, and donor types mirrored as HubSpot dropdown values. Add a new appeal code in DonorPerfect and it appears in HubSpot without anyone retyping it.
  • Gifts Pipeline (optional add-on) writes each gift as a HubSpot deal with amount, campaign, and solicitation attached. That makes second-gift conversion measurable without an export-to-Excel ritual.

Wiring up the 30-day welcome series

Here is the concrete build, illustrated against a hypothetical scenario at Riverbend Food Alliance (illustrative example, not a real organization).

Step 1. Confirm the property sync. Inside CRMConnect’s configuration, verify that First Gift Date, Last Gift Date, Donor Created Date, Total Gifts, Largest Gift, and the Donor Type code are flowing from DonorPerfect into the HubSpot contact record. These five properties carry the welcome series.

Step 2. Build the enrollment trigger. In HubSpot, create a workflow with the trigger First Gift Date is known AND First Gift Date is less than 1 day ago. Set re-enrollment off. This guarantees one welcome per first-time donor and avoids retriggering when a major gift officer later edits the contact record.

Step 3. Sequence the touches. A 30-day welcome that works for a food bank looks like this:

  • Day 0: instant thank-you email signed by the executive director, with the gift amount, date, and impact statement pulled from DonorPerfect via the synced fields.
  • Day 2: a short story email featuring a partner pantry (no identifying details about the families served, just the program model).
  • Day 7: a volunteer invite, segmented by the donor’s ZIP code if the location field is mapped.
  • Day 14: a behind-the-scenes video from the warehouse floor.
  • Day 21: a soft second-gift ask framed around a specific need (refrigerated truck fuel, weekend distribution staffing).
  • Day 30: a short survey: how did you hear about us, what would you like to see, do you prefer email or text.

Step 4. Suppress the wrong donors. Use Flags Sync as Tags to mirror DonorPerfect Flags into HubSpot as multi-select tags. Add suppression rules to the workflow: skip donors flagged as Board Member, Staff, Tribute Giver (those should land in a memorial-specific track), or Major Donor Prospect (those need a human, not an email cadence). Honor DonorPerfect communication preferences. If a donor has No Email set in DonorPerfect, that flag arrives in HubSpot as a tag and excludes them from the workflow automatically.

Step 5. Handle hard bounces and opt-outs. HubSpot will mark a contact as hard-bounced. Mirror that status back into DonorPerfect as a custom field via bi-directional sync so your direct mail team does not keep mailing a known-dead address. Same with unsubscribes: a HubSpot opt-out should write back to DonorPerfect’s communication preferences, not just sit in HubSpot.

Step 6. Measure the second gift. With the Gifts Pipeline add-on, each new DonorPerfect gift creates a HubSpot deal. Build a report that counts deals where the Contact’s First Gift Date is between 1 and 365 days ago and the deal is at least the second gift on that contact. That is your true second-gift conversion rate.

What it looks like in production

For an illustrative organization the size of Riverbend Food Alliance (1,200 active monthly donors, $4.2M budget), a properly wired welcome series typically moves second-gift conversion on first-time online donors from somewhere around 17% to the high 20s or low 30s within twelve months. On 800 net-new holiday donors, that is roughly 80 to 130 additional second gifts. Average online gift in the food bank space sits in the $60 to $90 range, so you are recovering $5,000 to $11,000 in incremental annual revenue from a workflow that costs nothing to operate once it is built.

That number ignores lifetime value. The donors who give twice in the first year are dramatically more likely to give in year two and three, and a meaningful slice convert to monthly sustainers. Multiply the second-gift lift across a three-year cohort and the integration pays for itself many times over.

Builder takeaway

  • Triggering off First Gift Date instead of Constituent Created Date avoids welcoming people who were entered for tribute gifts or event RSVPs but never actually gave.
  • Mirroring DonorPerfect Flags as HubSpot tags is the cheapest way to keep welcome workflows from sending the wrong message to board members, staff, and major-gift prospects.
  • Writing HubSpot hard-bounce status and unsubscribes back to DonorPerfect closes the loop and stops dead addresses from burning postage on the next direct mail drop.
