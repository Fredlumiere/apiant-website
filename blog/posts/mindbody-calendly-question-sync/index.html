Calendly Intake Answers Inside Mindbody Custom Fields, Before the Session Starts

Use CalendarConnect's Calendly question sync to push intake answers into Mindbody custom fields and indexes so trainers prep before the client walks in.

Fred Lumiere Mar 4, 2026 5 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Calendly#CalendarConnect#Data Engine

Why Trainers Are Reading Intakes Mid-Session

An acupuncture clinic asks new clients five intake questions before their first session: primary complaint, prior treatments, pregnancy status, medication list, and preferred treatment style. Calendly captures these answers cleanly during booking. The provider sees the answers in her Calendly notification email.

Then she walks into the treatment room, opens Mindbody, and sees only the client’s name and appointment time. The intake answers are in an email she has to dig through. She either prints every Calendly email (a paper trail nobody wants) or asks the questions again in person, which annoys clients and wastes session time.

There is a regulatory dimension for clinical settings. Treatment notes and intake data need to live in the clinical system of record, not in a Calendly email history. Some clinics fail audit because intake data is scattered across email archives instead of stored in Mindbody (or in the clinical EHR that integrates with Mindbody). Structured intake at the integration layer fixes the compliance story too.

Mapping Calendly Questions to Mindbody Fields

Calendly’s intake answers are tied to the Calendly event. They do not natively flow into Mindbody at all. Generic integrations might pass the answers as a notes field, which is better than nothing but still requires the provider to scroll through free-text notes during a session.

The right answer is structured: each Calendly question maps to a specific Mindbody custom field or client index so the provider can see them inline on the client profile, in the same place every time.

Free-text notes are the lowest-quality way to store structured intake. The provider has to read a blob of text every time and re-parse the same five answers. Multiply by 60 weekly consults and the provider is doing the same low-value parsing task hundreds of times a month. Worse, free-text notes are not queryable: the clinic cannot answer ‘how many clients this quarter mentioned pregnancy’ without manually scanning every note.

The APIANT Approach

CalendarConnect’s Calendly Question Sync is exactly this. Each Calendly intake question maps to a Mindbody custom field or index. Primary complaint goes to a custom field. Pregnancy status goes to an index. Medications go to a longer custom field. The provider opens the Mindbody client profile and sees structured intake data in predictable locations.

Custom Field Mapping is configurable per event type. A standard new-client consult maps five questions to five fields. A massage-only intake maps three. The clinic owner configures the mapping once and the structure stays consistent across providers and sessions.

Structured fields enable queries the clinic actually wants to run. ‘Which providers see the most pregnancy-related clients’ becomes a one-click report. ‘How many new clients this month mentioned a specific complaint’ becomes a one-click report. The clinic uses this for staffing decisions, marketing decisions, and specialty-training decisions.

Field mapping per event type lets the clinic tailor intake by service. A fertility-acupuncture event type asks five questions specific to that focus, mapped to fields that other providers do not see. A general intake asks five different questions mapped to general fields. The provider sees the right structured data for the appointment in front of her, not a generic blob.

Designing the Pre-Session Prep Workflow

  1. Build the five Calendly intake questions on the new-client event type.
  2. In Mindbody, create or identify the matching custom fields and indexes.
  3. In CalendarConnect, map each Calendly question to its Mindbody field. Save.
  4. Run a test booking. The provider checks the Mindbody profile and sees the five answers populated.

  5. Roll out to all providers. The intake reading time before each session drops to 30 seconds.

  6. Plan the field strategy with the clinic owner. Decide which intake answers belong in standard Mindbody fields, which in custom fields, and which in indexes. Indexes are best for categorical data the clinic wants to report on; custom fields are best for free-text or one-off data.

  7. Document the field map for compliance. A short reference showing which Calendly question populates which Mindbody field makes audit and provider training trivial.

Representative Prep-Time Lift at 150 Clients

Hypothetical: an acupuncture clinic with four providers running ~60 new-client consultations per week. After Question Sync, provider prep time before each new client drops by approximately 4 minutes per appointment. Across 60 weekly consults, that is 4 hours per week of provider time recovered, plus a significantly better first-session experience because the provider already knows the client’s history when she walks in.

There is a marketing-automation effect too. With structured intake fields populated, the clinic can run targeted follow-up: a fertility-specific email cadence for clients who indicated fertility goals, a recovery-specific cadence for clients who indicated post-surgical recovery. Generic email blasts get replaced with relevant messaging, and open and click rates lift accordingly.

Edge Cases Worth Knowing

Conditional questions in Calendly (a question that only shows if the prior answer was ‘yes’) need explicit field handling. CalendarConnect leaves the Mindbody field blank if the Calendly question was not asked, rather than writing a ‘not asked’ marker. The clinic can choose to interpret blank as ‘not asked’ or to require a value at intake.

Multi-select Calendly questions (a checkbox list) map to multi-value Mindbody indexes cleanly. The provider sees the selected options as a structured list on the client profile, which is queryable and reportable.

Updates to intake answers across appointments (a returning client whose medication list changed) overwrite the prior values by default, with the previous values preserved in the appointment-level note. The provider sees current state without losing history.

Builder Takeaway

  • Structured intake belongs in a structured field. Notes are a fallback, not the answer.
  • The provider’s prep time is the most expensive minute in the clinic. Optimize it.
  • Map once at the integration layer. Do not push intake-reading into a provider’s daily email habit.
