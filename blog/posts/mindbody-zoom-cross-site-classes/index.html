Cross-Site Zoom Classes for an 11-Location Virtual Fitness Franchise

How ZoomConnect's cross-site class capability, centralized franchise management, and multiple Zoom account support unlock one instructor teaching across many sites.

Fred Lumiere Sep 1, 2025 6 min read
#Fitness & Wellness#Mindbody#Zoom#ZoomConnect#For Enterprise

The Franchise Virtual Class Problem

An 11-location virtual fitness franchise wants to run shared Zoom classes that any client from any location can book into. A popular instructor at Site 4 should be able to teach a 6am virtual class that Site 1 through Site 11 clients can all attend. Today, each site runs its own Mindbody and its own Zoom, the franchise has duplicate classes, half-empty rooms, and instructor cost spread too thin.

Consolidating would let the franchise run fewer, bigger virtual classes with the strongest instructors. But the booking and access flow has to work across all 11 Mindbody sites cleanly, or the franchise loses more in operational chaos than it saves in instructor cost.

Instructor recruiting is the second-order problem. Strong virtual instructors are scarce. If each site has to recruit its own virtual roster, the weaker sites end up with weaker instructors, which depresses virtual attendance at those sites, which depresses instructor revenue, which makes recruiting even harder. The franchise enters a doom loop of bad virtual product at the smaller sites.

How Cross-Site Classes Work in ZoomConnect

Multi-site Mindbody franchises run on a Site ID per location. A class lives on one site. Bookings from other sites cannot natively attend that class in Mindbody. DIY solutions try to duplicate the class across all 11 sites and reconcile attendance manually, which means 11 calendar entries to keep in sync and 11 attendance lists to merge.

Off-the-shelf integrations have no concept of cross-site classes. They treat each site as an island. The franchise either does not consolidate or accepts the manual overhead, neither of which is good.

Reporting becomes a per-site nightmare. The franchise wants to know aggregate virtual revenue, virtual attendance trends, popular instructor across the network, and where to invest in more virtual capacity. Per-site Mindbody reports do not aggregate cleanly because they reflect different schedules, different prices, and different membership tiers. Manual rollup is slow and error-prone.

The APIANT Approach

ZoomConnect’s Cross-Site Class Capability is built exactly for this case. One Zoom class can accept bookings from multiple linked Mindbody Site IDs, across different time zones. The instructor sees one roster. Each client sees the class through her home site, books normally, and gets her unique Zoom link.

Centralized Franchise Management gives the franchise HQ one console to manage all cross-site classes, instructor assignments, and reporting. Multiple Zoom Account Support lets the franchise run more than one Zoom Pro account if class concurrency exceeds a single account’s limits. Scalable Enterprise Add-Ons cover the franchise’s growth path.

Multiple Zoom Account Support handles a practical scaling problem. A single Zoom Pro account caps at 100 concurrent participants per meeting. A popular cross-site class can exceed that. ZoomConnect can rotate across multiple Zoom Pro accounts (or use a Zoom Business or Enterprise account) so the franchise is never capped by Zoom’s seat limits. The instructor sees one room; the system handles the underlying account routing.

Pricing alignment across sites is tricky and ZoomConnect’s centralized model helps. The franchise can choose: each site charges its own price for the cross-site class (if pricing varies regionally), or all sites charge the same price (if the franchise wants uniformity). The integration respects whichever model is in Mindbody and settles instructor revenue accordingly.

Centralized Management Without Cross-Franchise Visibility

  1. Link all 11 Mindbody sites to the franchise’s ZoomConnect subscription.
  2. Designate which class types are cross-site. The franchise picks the 6am, 12pm, and 7pm popular slots.
  3. Build the cross-site class once in the master site. ZoomConnect makes it bookable from each linked site.
  4. Clients from any site see the class in their local schedule and book normally.
  5. The instructor gets one Zoom room, one roster, one paycheck.

  6. Centralized reporting at HQ shows attendance by site, instructor revenue by class, and cross-site retention metrics.

  7. Set the revenue-share rules per site. A cross-site class that gets attended by clients from all 11 sites needs revenue-share rules: does the hosting site get 100 percent, does revenue follow the client’s home site, or does the franchise pool and redistribute. ZoomConnect supports each model; the franchise picks once and the system enforces it.

  8. Standardize the cross-site instructor pay model. Avoid the trap where cross-site instructors earn differently than per-site instructors. Standard pay across both models keeps the instructor pool stable.

Representative Operational Lift at 11 Locations

Hypothetical: an 11-location virtual fitness franchise with ~7,800 members. After consolidating to cross-site shared Zoom classes for the three peak slots, the franchise runs 18 classes per week instead of 33 (the old per-site duplication). Average class size lifts from roughly 14 to roughly 31. Instructor cost per attended visit drops by around 40 percent. HQ time on schedule coordination drops from a part-time role to a weekly review meeting.

Member retention improves because the virtual product gets noticeably better. A member at Site 7 who used to attend the so-so Site 7 instructor twice a week now attends the strong cross-site instructor four times a week. Her engagement deepens, her renewal probability rises, and the franchise’s overall virtual revenue compounds from a quality lift, not just an attendance lift.

Edge Cases Worth Knowing

Time zone scheduling for cross-site classes can be subtle. A 7am Eastern class is 4am Pacific. The franchise needs to decide: is the class scheduled in one time zone (and bookable from any site at the local equivalent), or scheduled per site (which would require duplicate classes)? ZoomConnect supports the first model, which is almost always the right answer.

Sub instructor coverage across sites needs explicit handling. If the cross-site instructor calls out, the sub instructor takes over the cross-site class for all 11 sites’ bookings, not just her home site. ZoomConnect updates the Zoom host for the single class, which propagates to all booked clients regardless of site.

Reporting at the franchise level needs the underlying data to be clean. ZoomConnect emits per-site attribution on attendance, so HQ can answer questions like ‘which sites’ members attended cross-site classes most often’ without having to reconcile 11 separate reports.

Builder Takeaway

  • Multi-site franchises with virtual offerings should consolidate the popular slots. Per-site duplication is the wrong shape.
  • Cross-site class capability is an integration feature, not a Mindbody feature. The franchise needs middleware that understands the Site ID model.
  • Centralized reporting at HQ is the second prize. The first is fewer, fuller, stronger classes.
