Field-Level Data Governance for a Chiropractic Group Syncing Cliniko Into Salesforce

Fred Lumiere Jul 17, 2025 5 min read
#Healthcare#Cliniko#Salesforce#CRMConnect

A one-time CSV export from Cliniko into Salesforce is the worst-of-all-worlds setup. It is just live enough to feel like an integration. It is just stale enough to be wrong. It is just lossy enough that nobody fully trusts it. And it provides exactly zero of the audit trail a compliance officer needs to sign off on the practice’s data handling.

Take an illustrative example: Summit Spine and Joint Chiropractic, a hypothetical group with 3 clinics, 9 chiropractors, and around $1.4M in annual revenue. The practice sells multi-visit care plans. The compliance officer wants strict control over which patient fields flow into Salesforce, who in sales and marketing can see them, and a clear audit trail. The current setup is a periodic CSV export nobody fully trusts. The marketing team is asking for live data. The compliance officer is asking for governance. Both are right.

Why the one-time CSV is the worst-of-all-worlds for compliance

Three reasons:

  • No field-level scoping. A CSV export tends to be “all patient fields or no patient fields,” which is the opposite of the principle of least privilege.
  • No audit trail. Once the CSV is uploaded, there is no log of which records were imported, when, or by whom. A compliance review has nothing to look at.
  • Stale by design. A monthly or quarterly export means Salesforce reflects historical truth. Updates and deletions on the Cliniko side (consent revocations, merged duplicates, record corrections) do not propagate.

The compliance officer’s instinct that something is wrong with this setup is correct. The right answer is field-level, bi-directional, logged.

The APIANT approach

CRMConnect Cliniko to Salesforce supports field-level sync configuration: every Cliniko field that flows to Salesforce is explicitly opted-in, not opted-out. Bi-directional patient sync means updates on either side propagate, and consent or correction changes are honored within minutes rather than quarters. Sync logs are persisted and queryable, giving the compliance officer the audit trail the CSV approach cannot provide.

Concrete steps

  1. Write the field map before configuring anything. List every Cliniko field that will sync to Salesforce. Tag each as marketing-safe, operations-safe, or clinician-only. The clinician-only fields are not configured. The compliance officer signs the map.
  2. Enable bi-directional patient sync for marketing-safe and operations-safe demographic fields only. Examples in-scope: name, email, phone, mailing address, marketing communication preferences, business location. Examples out-of-scope: any free-text clinical field, any diagnosis field, any treatment plan field.
  3. Enable Case Information as Salesforce objects, with field-level scoping. In-scope for Salesforce: case status, session limits, sessions completed, max invoiceable amount, milestone progress. Out-of-scope: clinical narrative, case notes, free-text fields describing the patient’s condition or response to treatment.
  4. Align Salesforce profiles and permission sets with the sync map. Clinical-facing roles see operations-safe fields. Billing-facing roles see invoice and payment fields. Marketing-facing roles see marketing-safe fields only. Cross-role access is explicit, not default.
  5. Configure marketing preferences and consent as one-way Cliniko to Salesforce. The Cliniko consent field is the source of truth. If a patient revokes consent in Cliniko, that change propagates to Salesforce immediately. Consent changes initiated in Salesforce do not push back unless a controlled, logged process is in place.
  6. Configure the sync log retention. Confirm what is logged (record created, record updated, fields modified, source of change), confirm the retention period meets the practice’s compliance posture, and confirm the compliance officer knows how to query it.
  7. Schedule the quarterly governance review. Twenty minutes per quarter, with the compliance officer, the integration owner, and the clinical director. Agenda below.

Quarterly governance review

The agenda is short and consistent:

  • Confirm the field map has not changed without sign-off. Note any additions.
  • Confirm Salesforce profile and permission set changes since last quarter. Identify any new roles or new accesses that touch synced data.
  • Review the sync log for the quarter. Spot-check a small sample of records for any clinical content that should not have crossed the boundary.
  • Review marketing consent counts. Confirm Cliniko and Salesforce agree on opt-in counts. Investigate any discrepancy.
  • Document the review in the practice’s compliance file.

Twenty minutes per quarter. The CSV approach offers zero minutes of review surface because there is nothing to review. The integration’s audit trail is what makes the review possible.

Outcome

A chiropractic group matching Summit’s profile that adopts this pattern moves from “the marketing team and the compliance officer disagree about whether the integration is safe” to “both teams have a written, signed, and quarterly-reviewed governance posture.” Operationally, the marketing team gets the live data they wanted (multi-visit care plan progress, lapsed-patient signals, lifetime value) without the records that would expose the practice. The compliance officer gets the audit trail they need. The CFO stops getting questions about reporting accuracy.

Salesforce profiles and permission sets, aligned to what Cliniko exposes

A common failure mode: marketing teams end up with Salesforce permissions that let them see fields the integration synced for operational use (case status, session limits) because nobody scoped the permission set at setup. The fix is one-time but it has to happen on day one. Separate clinical, billing, and marketing permission sets. Map each to the subset of synced fields they need. Default deny everything else.

Builder takeaway

  • The field map is a written, signed document. It is also the deliverable that turns the integration from “trust me” to “here is the policy.” Build it before turning anything on.
  • Bi-directional sync is not optional for compliance. The moment a patient revokes consent in Cliniko, that change has to reach Salesforce within minutes, not quarters.
  • The audit trail is the difference between a passing compliance review and a failing one. The integration provides it. Make sure the compliance officer knows how to use it.
